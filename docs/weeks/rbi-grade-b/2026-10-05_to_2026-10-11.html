01

RBI & Monetary Policy

  • ★ The 63rd Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) raised the policy repo rate by 25 basis points (bps) to 5.50%. The meeting ran from 5 to 7 Oct 2026 under Governor Sanjay Malhotra, and the vote was unanimous. The Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) rate is 5.25%, while the Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) rate and Bank Rate are both 5.75%. This reverses the easing bias of earlier meetings, so the current repo rate is the first fact to learn.
  • ★ The MPC changed its stance to "calibrated tightening", but not unanimously. Two members, Dr. Nagesh Kumar and Prof. Ram Singh, wanted the stance kept at neutral. The other members present were Dr. Poonam Gupta, Shri Saugata Bhattacharya and Shri Indranil Bhattacharyya. The message is that rate cuts are off the table and the next move can only be a hike or a pause, depending on underlying inflation, broadening price pressures and second-round effects of the supply shock. MPC minutes are due on 21 Oct 2026 and the next meeting is on 2–4 Dec 2026.
  • ★ The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) projected real GDP growth of 7.1% for 2026-27. The quarterly path is Q2 7.2%, Q3 6.9% and Q4 6.8%, with 7.1% for Q1:2027-28. The Governor called this an upward revision of 40 bps, following a stronger-than-expected Q1:2026-27 print of 7.8% from the National Statistics Office (NSO). Downside risks include a deficient south-west monsoon and a strong El Niño, which could hurt the rabi season and rural demand. Bilateral trade agreements are expected to support merchandise exports.
  • ★ The CPI inflation projection for 2026-27 is 5.2%, with a quarterly path of Q2 4.9%, Q3 6.0% and Q4 5.7%. Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation was 4.8% in Aug 2026 (July: 4.5%), driven by food and fuel, with sugar and onion spiking. Core inflation was 4.2% (3.9% for the three preceding months) and 2.9% excluding precious metals. Headline CPI is expected to average almost 5.8% over the next three quarters, which is the number to compare against the 4% target and the 6% upper tolerance limit of the flexible inflation targeting framework. The 2026-27 core projection is 4.4% and Q1:2027-28 CPI is 5.6%.
  • The policy was framed by a hostile global backdrop. The West Asia conflict re-escalated in September and caused crude volatility. The US Federal Reserve hiked by 25 bps in September, and global bond yields are at record highs. Other risks cited were AI-related asset valuations, high public debt and fiscal sustainability concerns. The Governor noted that global inflation is projected to rise sharply, prompting tightening by major central banks.
  • ★ On 10 Oct 2026 RBI tightened forex derivative rules through A.P. (DIR Series) Circulars No. 25 and No. 26. Cancelled INR forex derivative contracts (deliverable or non-deliverable) cannot be rebooked with any Authorised Dealer (AD), though rollover on maturity is still allowed. The threshold for hedging contracted exposures without an underlying exposure was cut from USD 100 million to USD 5 million, for both ADs and exchange-traded INR currency derivatives. ADs must also obtain an undertaking that the same exposure is not hedged with another AD.
  • ★ A Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR) was introduced for INR forex derivatives above USD 2 million notional. ADs must keep a cash reserve with RBI equal to 20% of the INR equivalent of the notional amount. It applies to contracts hedging current account exposures where the user buys foreign currency against INR. Together with the lower hedging threshold, it is aimed at curbing speculative pressure on the rupee.
  • RBI opened a special dollar window for public sector oil marketing companies (OMCs) from 12 Oct 2026. RBI will sell US dollars through designated bank(s) to meet the entire daily dollar requirement of Indian Oil Corporation Ltd, Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Ltd and Bharat Petroleum Corporation Ltd. It stays in force until further notice and removes OMC demand from the open market, a major source of dollar demand.
  • The Statement on Developmental and Regulatory Policies (7 Oct 2026) covered Account Aggregators and a new market forum. Interoperability will be allowed among NBFC-Account Aggregators (NBFC-AA), so customers can share data across Financial Information Providers through any NBFC-AA. SEBI-regulated Depositories can include bank deposit information in the Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) through NBFC-AAs, with both measures targeted for 31 Dec 2026. A Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets will also be formed to engage market participants on money, government securities and forex markets.
  • Liquidity stayed in heavy surplus, with Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions at the policy-linked rate. Overnight VRRR auctions on 5 and 6 Oct cleared at 5.24%, and after the hike cut-offs were 5.49%. On 8 Oct a 29-day VRRR notified ₹2,00,000 crore, received offers of ₹79,655 crore (all accepted) and reverses on 6 Nov 2026. Premature reversal requests must go through the E-Kuber portal at least two working days before the original reversal date.
  • Government securities (G-Sec) auctions continued the borrowing calendar. On 9 Oct the Centre sold ₹36,000 crore: 7.06% GS 2041 (₹23,000 crore, cut-off price ₹96.18, yield 7.4898%) and 7.43% GS 2076 (₹13,000 crore, cut-off price ₹95.33, yield 7.8015%). In the 7 Oct Treasury Bill auction the cut-off yields were 5.5747% (91-day), 6.0999% (182-day) and 6.2869% (364-day). A ₹23,000 crore T-Bill auction is set for 14 Oct, with retail allocation capped at 5%.
  • State Government Securities (SGS) auctions showed rising state borrowing costs. On 6 Oct, 13 States/UTs had a notified amount of ₹25,100 crore, of which ₹23,600 crore was accepted. Delhi and Punjab accepted nothing in some re-issues, and Meghalaya's 13-year SGS 2039 had the highest cut-off at 8.08%. Another auction of ₹31,200 crore is scheduled for 13 Oct 2026. SGS are eligible for the Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
  • An Open Market Operations (OMO) sale of ₹25,000 crore was announced for 13 Oct 2026. It is a multi-security auction by the multiple price method, covering 7.88% GS 2030, 6.10% GS 2031, 7.95% GS 2032, 7.26% GS 2033, 7.18% GS 2033 and 7.10% GS 2034. OMO sales drain durable liquidity, consistent with the tightening stance.
  • Forex reserves were US$734,606 million as on 2 Oct 2026, according to the Weekly Statistical Supplement (WSS). They fell US$12,952 million in the week and are up US$43,498 million since end-March 2026. Foreign Currency Assets are US$604,747 million, gold US$106,413 million, Special Drawing Rights US$18,656 million and the Reserve Position in the IMF US$4,789 million.
  • WSS monetary data show strong credit growth as of 30 Sep 2026. Scheduled commercial bank deposits were ₹28,395,693 crore (up 17.8% y-o-y), bank credit ₹22,994,658 crore (up 19.4%) and Money Stock M3 ₹33,766,475 crore (up 16.9%). Under the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025 a fortnight is now the 15th and last calendar day of a month, effective 15 Dec 2025, instead of alternate Fridays.
  • The Order Books, Inventories and Capacity Utilisation Survey (OBICUS) Round 75 covers Jul–Sep 2026 (Q2:2026-27). It is a quarterly manufacturing-sector survey conducted since 2008. It continues RBI's series of forward-looking surveys that inform growth assessments.
02

Banking & Financial Sector

  • ★ RBI extended Section 35A directions on six Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs) by a further three months. The Directions are issued under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, "in the public interest". The new end-dates are 07 Jan 2027 for Samarth Urban Co-operative Bank (Osmanabad), Sadbhav Nagrik Sahakari Bank Maryadit (Chhatarpur) and Samarth Sahakari Bank (Solapur). Motiram Agrawal Jalna Merchants Co-operative Bank (Jalna) runs to 10 Jan 2027, and The Baghat Urban Co-operative Bank (Solan) and Ramgarhia Co-operative Bank (New Delhi) to 08 Jan 2027. RBI repeats that an extension does not imply satisfaction with the bank's financial position, and Ramgarhia's Directive has been rolled over since July 2022.
  • ★ RBI finalised two capital-framework directions for commercial banks, both effective 1 Apr 2027. The Standardised Approach for Counterparty Credit Risk (SA-CCR) was drafted on 10 Jun 2026 (feedback until 1 Jul) and covers netting sets, banks acting as clearing members of SEBI-recognised exchanges, option premium deferment and disclosure templates. The Credit Valuation Adjustment (CVA) Framework was drafted on 7 Aug 2026 (feedback until 28 Aug). It offers a simpler approach for eligible banks, more granular supervisory risk weights, clearer recognition of CVA hedges and separate systematic and idiosyncratic components in the Basic Approach (BA-CVA). Both continue the Basel-aligned capital reforms.
  • Paytm Payments Bank Limited was excluded from the Second Schedule to the RBI Act, 1934. The notification (DoR.LIC.No.S3674/16.13.215/2026-27) is dated 31 Jul 2026 and was published in the Gazette on 07 Sep 2026. Removal from the Second Schedule ends its scheduled bank status.
  • The Ministry of Cooperation held its 9th National Review Workshop at Bastar, Chhattisgarh on 8–9 Oct 2026. The theme was "Sahkar se Samriddhi: The Road Ahead". Secretary Dr. Ashish Kumar Bhutani said cooperative bank autonomy must come with accountability and that the Ministry is coordinating with RBI on resource mobilisation and concessional farm finance. The venue, a district long affected by Left Wing Extremism, was suggested by Union Home and Cooperation Minister Amit Shah.
  • The Cooperation Ministry set specific targets for Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS) and cooperative banking. The aim is about 95,000 new PACS over 2–3 years, and the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD) is finalising a uniform Core Banking Solution platform. PACS computerisation is scheduled to end March 2027, with phase one due by December 2026. The 10th workshop is proposed for Mahabalipuram, Tamil Nadu, in January 2027.
  • The Department of Financial Services (DFS) reviewed financial inclusion schemes under Secretary Sanjay Lohiya. Schemes covered included PMJDY, PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY, PMMY, Stand Up India, PM SVANidhi, PM Vishwakarma, PM Surya Ghar and the Kisan Credit Card. Credit lines on UPI and Credit Cards for Micro Enterprises (CCME) were discussed. A financial inclusion saturation campaign will run from 21 Oct to 31 Dec 2026.
  • DFS mandated scheme-wise and bank-wise reporting of Scheduled Caste beneficiaries after an intervention by the National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC). DFS directions dated 5 Oct 2026 went to SLBC/UTLBC Convenors, with an action-taken report due 9 Oct 2026. Schemes covered include PMMY, Stand-Up India, PMEGP, PM SVANidhi and CGTMSE. NCSC acts under Article 338(5)(c) of the Constitution.
  • The 4th UK-India Financial Markets Dialogue took place on 29 Sep 2026 in London. Participants included SEBI, RBI, IFSCA, PFRDA, IRDAI, the Bank of England, the Financial Conduct Authority and HM Treasury. It referenced the India-UK Financial Partnership, GIFT IFSC, London's role as an offshore rupee trading hub and the G20 Roadmap for Enhancing Cross-Border Payments. It also noted that the UK-India Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) came into force on 15 July 2026.
  • The National Land Monetization Corporation (NLMC) ran land e-auctions under the Department of Public Enterprises. RINL's Visakhapatnam land comprises 459 parcels of 93,572.21 sq yards in two phases (12 Oct and 16 Oct 2026), with an Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) of ₹2 lakh per plot on the RailTel e-Nivida platform. A separate HIL (India) Limited parcel of about 5.119 acres at Najafgarh Industrial Area has a reserve price of ₹829.69 crore and an EMD of ₹16.60 crore. This is the third NLMC auction in two months and ties in with the government's asset monetisation drive.
  • Cyber-fraud prevention through telecom and banking data is a rising policy theme. The Financial Fraud Risk Indicator (FRI) has prevented suspected losses of over ₹5,000 crore, and Sanchar Saathi is linked with 1,600+ organisations on the Digital Intelligence Platform (DIP). The Minister of State for Communications said Indians lost over ₹22,800 crore to such fraud in 2024. India welcomed an International Telecommunication Union (ITU) proposal for joint trials against cross-border telecom fraud.
  • Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal met global financial firms in New York. They included Blackstone, Neuberger Berman, IBM, MetLife, General Atlantic and AIA Group. The meetings were linked to India's vision of "Insurance for All by 2047" and to attracting long-term capital.

Government Schemes & Budget

  • ★ The Union Cabinet approved a ₹10,000 crore commitment to the SME Growth Fund (SGF) on 6 Oct 2026. It was announced in Para 28 of Union Budget 2026-27 and works through an Alternative Investment Fund (AIF) that provides patient growth equity to SMEs. Most of the money goes to small and medium manufacturing enterprises, including industrial clusters in Tier II and Tier III cities. It fills a gap because existing funds focus on early-stage and micro enterprises.
  • ★ The Cabinet approved the Integrated Transport & Logistics Authority (ITLA), a special purpose vehicle (SPV). It will prepare a National Transport Master Plan with a 10+ year horizon and evaluate short-term (about 5-year) sectoral plans. It will technically appraise and monitor projects of ₹500 crore or more and build a National Transport Data Repository from GSTN e-way bills, FASTag and Vahan. It complements PM GatiShakti and will help update the National Logistics Policy (2022).
  • ★ Union Budget 2026-27 announced seven high-speed rail corridors of about 4,000 km costing ₹16 lakh crore. Indicative times include Delhi–Varanasi 3 hr 50 min and Mumbai–Pune 48 min. The existing Mumbai–Ahmedabad High-Speed Rail (508 km, 320 kmph, 12 stations) targets first service in Aug 2027. The corridors are the headline Budget infrastructure item in this week's backgrounders.
  • ★ The Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses completed one year on 11 Oct 2026. It was launched on 11 Oct 2025 with an outlay of ₹11,440 crore over 2025-26 to 2030-31 and aims for self-reliance by 2030-31. Targets are nearly 350 lakh tonnes of production, yield of 1,130 kg/ha and 310 lakh ha of area, focused on Tur, Urad and Masoor. Pulses production reached 274.09 lakh tonnes in 2025-26 (3rd Advance Estimates, vs 256.83 lakh t), while imports fell 31.52% to ₹31,793.59 crore. NCCF and NAFED can procure 100% of Tur, Masur and Urad for four years (2025-26 to 2028-29).
  • ★ Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY) has built about 8 lakh km of rural roads over 25 years. PMGSY-IV has an outlay of ₹70,125 crore for about 62,500 km of all-weather roads and 25,000 unconnected habitations. The two-day PMGSY Chintan Shivir at Bharat Mandapam (5–6 Oct 2026) stressed contractor responsibility during the five-year maintenance period and tools like OMMAS, GPS-VTS and AI.
  • ★ The 57th GST Council meeting (8 Oct 2026) focused on process reforms rather than rate cuts. It continues the 56th meeting's "Next-Gen reforms" on rate rationalisation. Key recommendations are complete withdrawal of arrest powers (omission of Section 69 of the CGST Act), prosecution threshold raised from ₹1 crore to ₹5 crore and a general penalty (Section 125) cut from ₹25,000 to ₹10,000. A minimum of ₹10,000 now applies for show cause notices, and the pre-deposit cap for penalty-only appeals is ₹40 crore. Refunds get 90% provisional automatic sanction for zero-rated and inverted-duty claims. Recommendations take legal effect only via circulars, notifications and law amendments.
  • GST rate and procedural clarifications were also issued. Psyllium seeds (Isabgol) get NIL GST, and re-treaded tractor tyres are aligned with new tractor tyres. EV passenger transport can opt for 5% GST with restricted ITC, delivery services through e-commerce operators are taxed at 5% without ITC, and blocked-credit restrictions under Section 17(5) were removed for items like outdoor catering and health insurance. An optional Annual Return Quarterly Payment (ARQP) scheme was approved in principle for turnover up to ₹5 crore and exclusively B2C supplies.
  • VB–G RAM G notified 375 permissible works, up from 266 (a 41% expansion). The Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025 has four pillars: Water Security (128 works, the highest), Core Rural Infrastructure (111), Livelihood-related Infrastructure (98) and Disaster Preparedness (38, a new category). Livelihood works rose to 26% of the total from 1.1%, and works are planned through the Viksit Gram Panchayat Plan.
  • The sports allocation in Union Budget 2026-27 is a record ₹4,479.88 crore (₹1,219 crore in 2013-14). Khelo India was revamped for 2026-27 to 2030-31 with a combined outlay (including support to National Sports Federations) of ₹36,441 crore. It follows the earlier ₹3,790.50 crore outlay for 2021-22 to 2025-26, and ₹500 crore was earmarked for sports goods manufacturing. The Target Olympic Podium Scheme (TOPS) pays a monthly stipend of ₹50,000.
  • The government approved a cap on trade margins for non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs at 30% of MRP. The expected price cut is up to 70%, saving patients about ₹2,500 crore a year. The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) will notify it, based on a list from a Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS) expert committee. It extends the Feb 2019 precedent, when 42 drugs were capped under Paragraph 19 of the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013.
  • The Joint Squad for Health (JOSH) Teams were launched on 8 Oct 2026 under TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan. Union Health Minister J.P. Nadda launched them at Bharat Mandapam, with each team having 5 MY Bharat Volunteers and 5 NCC Cadets under a Class-I officer. Phase 1 covers 55 districts in 11 States/UTs, and 67,933 Panchayats were recognised as TB Mukt. India's TB incidence is down 21% against a global 12%, per the WHO report cited.
  • Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) 2.0 shifts focus from building infrastructure to sustainable service delivery. Rural tap connections now exceed 15.96 crore households against 3.27 crore at launch in August 2019. A Jal Seva Adhayan study found pipeline leakages are the leading cause of supply disruption. Priorities include Har Ghar Jal certification in PM-JANMAN villages and Jal Arpan handover to Gram Panchayats.
  • The Rural Self Employment Training Institutes (RSETI) programme has 647 institutes covering 634 districts. About 64 lakh people have been trained, with about 72% settled in livelihoods. The Ministry of Rural Development wants a Model RSETI in every State and AI-enabled integration on the Kaushal Rural portal.
  • Several technology and industry schemes got funding or milestones. The Technology Development Board signed ₹130 crore for Project VeerAI (an AI vision chip) under the Research Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund, whose total outlay is about ₹1 lakh crore. The Telecom Manufacturing Zone at Gwalior gets ₹493 crore of central assistance for Phase 1 and a 51:49 SPV between Madhya Pradesh and the Department of Telecommunications (DoT). The Gyan Bharatam Mission has a foundational corpus of ₹500 crore.
  • Healthcare coverage and digital health figures were updated. Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY gives ₹5 lakh cover per family per year, and over ₹2 lakh crore has been spent on treatment. eSanjeevani crossed 50 crore teleconsultations (50,00,92,730 on 15 Sep 2026) across all 36 States/UTs.
  • Special Campaign 6 (2–31 Oct 2026) continues the institutionalised drive started in 2021. The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG) is nodal and the focus is e-waste, cleanliness and pendency reduction. The preparatory phase was 15–30 Sep 2026, and the target dashboard shows 12,45,073 cleanliness sites and 6,18,516 public grievances.

Economy & Trade

  • ★ The India-EFTA Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (TEPA) marked its first anniversary at the 2nd India-EFTA Prosperity Summit (7 Oct 2026). It has been in force since 1 October 2025 and is India's first FTA with an economic bloc in Europe. The European Free Trade Association (EFTA) states are Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein. Under Article 7.1, EFTA aims to raise FDI into India by USD 100 billion over 15 years (USD 50 bn within 10 years plus USD 50 bn over the next 5) and facilitate 1 million jobs.
  • ★ TEPA market access is asymmetric in India's favour. EFTA commitments cover 92.2% of tariff lines, equal to 99.6% of India's exports, with full coverage of non-agricultural products. India's commitments cover 82.7% of tariff lines, equal to 95.3% of EFTA exports. EFTA states import goods and services worth over USD 500 billion a year.
  • ★ The state visit of Swiss President Guy Parmelin produced five outcomes on 5 Oct 2026. They were an MoU on Migration and Mobility Partnership (multiple-entry visas up to 5 years), an Agreement on Exchange of Young Professionals (300 persons annually per country, rising to 500) and an MoU on transportation technologies. The others were a Letter of Intent between DST and the Swiss National Science Foundation and an Implementation Arrangement between DST and Zurich University of Applied Sciences on AIT India. Switzerland will host the "Geneva AI Summit" next year, and India hosted the world's largest AI Summit in February 2026.
  • ★ India's bilateral and regional trade architecture saw further activity. The 15th ASEAN-India Trade in Goods Agreement (AITIGA) Joint Committee met in Jakarta (6–9 Oct 2026), and the next meeting is in January 2027 in India. The UK-India CETA has been in force since 15 July 2026. Goyal said exports were USD 863 billion last year, with a target of USD 1 trillion this year, and urged FTA utilisation desks in every State Council of the Confederation of Indian Industry (CII).
  • The TRAI "Indian Telecom Services Yearly Performance Indicators 2025-26" show rapid growth. Total telephone subscribers rose from 1,200.80 mn to 1,330.58 mn (+10.81%) and tele-density from 85.04% to 93.26%. Internet subscribers reached 1092.79 mn and broadband 1,065.88 mn. Wireless ARPU rose to ₹180.73 a month, data usage rose 24.74% to 2,85,376 PB and revenue per GB fell to ₹8.02.
  • Telecom revenue figures for 2025-26 are exam-relevant. Gross Revenue (GR) was ₹4,04,067 crore (+8.59%), and Adjusted Gross Revenue (AGR) was ₹3,34,659 crore (+10.44%). Private operators hold 92.32% of subscribers and public sector operators 7.68%. Rural tele-density is 60.46% against 151.47% in urban areas.
  • Steel data for H1 FY 2026-27 show production growth and rising prices. Crude steel production was 85.1 Mt in Apr–Sep 2026 (+2.9%), and finished steel consumption was 84.9 Mt (+7.5%). India was a net importer of finished steel by quantity (imports 4,141.8 thousand tonnes against exports of 3,547.8). A Steel Industry Safety Council chaired by the Steel Secretary is to be set up, and NMDC commissioned a 2 MTPA pellet plant at Nagarnar as part of a ₹5,427 crore project.
  • Coal India Limited (CIL) offtake in H1 FY 2026-27 was 384.2 MT, up 7.6% from 357.0 MT. Power-sector despatch was 302.8 MT (about 79% of offtake), and domestic coal-based generation was 640.05 billion units (+9.9%). Daily stock drawdown at power plants fell from 0.243 MT/day in September to 0.116 MT/day in October.
  • The National Household Travel Survey (NHTS), July 2025–June 2026, is India's first all-India household travel survey. It covered 14,274 villages, 10,377 urban blocks, 4,92,023 households and 20,34,371 persons, defining a trip as travel of more than 1 km. Students mostly walk (37.0%) or take buses (29.5%), and workers use two-wheelers (42.6%). Mass transit is 5.0% of urban commutes, led by Maharashtra (20.7%), West Bengal (16.5%) and Delhi (14.4%).
  • The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) published statistical reforms. A monthly Index of Services Production (ISP) for the formal sector began on a trial basis in July 2026 with base year 2024-25, covering 19 sub-sectors and about 60% of services GVA. An approach paper would raise coverage to about 78.4%, with comments due 16 Oct 2026. A draft GSDP expenditure-side guideline (base year 2022-23) says no State/UT compiles Private Final Consumption Expenditure.
  • The first international cargo moved from Bogibeel Terminal, Dibrugarh to Bangladesh on 5 Oct 2026. Union Minister Sarbananda Sonowal flagged off 540 tonnes of methanol from Assam Petro-Chemicals (Namrup) to Pangaon Port, Dhaka, via National Waterway-2 and the Indo-Bangladesh Protocol Route. National waterways cargo grew from 18 MMT (2013-14) to 218 MMT (2025-26).
  • Sagarmanthan 2026, the 3rd edition, highlighted India's maritime growth. India's seafarers grew from 1.27 lakh to 3.23 lakh in 12 years, making India the second-largest supplier. Port capacity rose from 1,561 to 2,817 MTPA, and inland waterways cargo grew from 29 to 218 million tonnes. The India-Korea shipbuilding framework and the India-EU Green Shipping Corridor were also discussed.
  • Energy and defence-industry numbers were updated. India's installed capacity was 558.8 GW on 30 Sep 2026, of which non-fossil capacity is 308 GW (55%). Cumulative wind capacity is 58.52 GW (Aug 2026) with a 100 GW by 2030 goal. Defence exports were a record ₹38,424 crore in FY 2025-26 (up over 60%), with a ₹50,000 crore target by 2029.
  • Onion prices eased as Kharif arrivals picked up. The Maharashtra weighted average mandi price fell from ₹4,030 per quintal (16 Sep) to ₹3,475 (3 Oct). Buffer stock of over 2.0 lakh quintals was disposed of through 8 Kanda Express rakes and 467 trucks.
  • The Ayush Service Sector Report (Research and Information System for Developing Countries) sized the sector at US$36.92 billion in FY2024-25. It grew at a 16.91% CAGR since FY2022-23 and supports 1.76 million livelihoods. Women form 43% of the workforce, and the projection is US$216–318 billion by 2047.

International Affairs & Organizations

  • Swiss President Guy Parmelin made a state visit to India, received by President Droupadi Murmu on 5 Oct 2026. Cooperation areas include trade, science and technology, infrastructure (Varanasi ropeway, Jewar Airport), defence production and nuclear energy. Both sides called for peace on Ukraine and West Asia and for reform of global institutions. Switzerland earmarked CHF 45 million for science and technology cooperation.
  • The 153rd Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU) Assembly was held in Arusha, Tanzania. Australia's Milton Dick, Speaker of the House of Representatives, was elected IPU President on 9 Oct 2026, succeeding Tulia Ackson. Anda Filip had been elected Secretary General at the 152nd Assembly in Istanbul, the first woman in the IPU's 137-year history. India's delegation was led by Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman Harivansh, who exercised the Right of Reply against Pakistan's Kashmir remarks.
  • The IPU adopted an emergency item on climate-related disasters in the Himalayan region. The item was proposed by Nepal and supported by India. The Indian delegation also held bilaterals with Milton Dick and the Speaker of Tanzania's Parliament, Mussa Azzan Zungu.
  • UNFCCC Pre-COP31 was held in Nadi, Fiji, co-hosted by Australia, Fiji and Tuvalu. Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav led India's delegation and stressed Equity and Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC), Article 9.1 on finance, Article 6 cooperation and the 1.5°C goal. COP31 will be held in Antalya, Türkiye, in November 2026. India offered coastal resilience and space-based expertise to Pacific partners.
  • India and Japan operationalised the Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM) under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement. The Operational Manual was launched on 30 Sep 2026 in New Delhi. The Memorandum of Cooperation was signed on 7 Aug 2025 and the Rules of Implementation were adopted on 8 June 2026.
  • The 20th East Asia Summit (EAS) Energy Ministers' Meeting was held in Manila. MoS Shripad Yesso Naik represented India and backed the ASEAN Power Grid, recalling PM Modi's pledge to train 400 professionals at the 22nd ASEAN-India Summit. India is exploring a 2,000 MW interconnection with Singapore, possibly a 3,000 km corridor via Myanmar, Thailand and Malaysia. The Philippines President said Modi is welcome in Manila for the ASEAN-India and East Asia Summits in November.
  • India and Ghana signed a five-year agriculture MoU in the presence of EAM Dr S. Jaishankar and Ghana's Foreign Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa. It is between ICAR and CSIR-Crops Research Institute, Accra, and its Phase 1 focuses on tomato and tuber crops, with Arima Farms Ltd as the commercial partner. It strengthens South-South and India-Africa cooperation.
  • World leaders congratulated PM Modi on 25 years as head of government (7 Oct 2026). Modi became Gujarat Chief Minister on 7 Oct 2001 and Prime Minister in May 2014 (26 May 2014). Messages came from Bhutan, Nigeria, the Philippines, Japan (PM Sanae Takaichi), South Korea (President Lee Jae-myung), Ethiopia, Seychelles and others. Japan-India's 75th anniversary of diplomatic relations falls in 2027, and 2026 marks fifty years of India-Seychelles ties.

Sports / Environment / Science & Tech

  • India won 85 medals (21 Gold, 27 Silver, 37 Bronze) at the 20th Asian Games, Aichi-Nagoya, Japan (19 Sep–4 Oct 2026). The slogan was "Imagine One Asia", and it was Japan's third time hosting after Tokyo (1958) and Hiroshima (1994). Golds came from Archery (5), Shooting (3), Boxing (3), Cricket (2), Hockey (2), Kabaddi (2), Wrestling (2), Athletics (1) and Golf (1). The men's cricket team beat Pakistan by 19 runs in the final, and the men's hockey team beat Malaysia 5-1.
  • The Asian Games trivia is a likely question source. The Games are held every 4 years by the Olympic Council of Asia, with 43 sports, 469 events and the mascot "HONOHON". The first Asian Games were held at New Delhi in March 1951, conceived by Prof. G.D. Sondhi, with 11 nations and India finishing 2nd behind Japan. India's contingent this year was 501 athletes in 36 disciplines, with flag-bearers Manu Bhaker and Pawan Sehrawat.
  • The 5th Asian Para Games will be held on 18–24 Oct 2026 at Aichi-Nagoya. It is Japan's first time hosting the Para Games, with about 3,000 athletes from 45 National Paralympic Committees in 18 sports. At Hangzhou 2022 India won a record 111 medals (29 gold).
  • The 94th Fit India Sundays on Cycle was held at Varanasi on 11 Oct 2026. The theme was "Nasha Mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat", and Dr. Mansukh Mandaviya led over 500 cyclists on a 5.5 km rally from Namo Ghat. Asian Games medallists Prachi Chaudhary, Parul Chaudhary, Jay Kumar and Raj Kumar Pal were felicitated.

This is under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (amended 2023), and East West Seeds India Ltd was the largest contributor at about ₹2.01 crore. Agri-seed was the largest sector at ₹7.16 crore, and since 2008 NBA has realised about ₹278 crore and disbursed over ₹200 crore.

About ₹19,700 crore was spent over five years, with 2,150+ mitigation structures built or proposed, including viaducts (₹8,300 crore) and animal underpasses (₹4,600 crore).   • A study coordinated by ARIES, Nainital, mapped OJ 287, a blazar about 4 billion light years away. The central black hole mass was estimated at at least 3.89 billion solar masses. The Aryabhatta Research Institute of Observational Sciences is an autonomous institution under the Department of Science and Technology.
