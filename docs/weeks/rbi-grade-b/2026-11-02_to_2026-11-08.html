01

RBI & Monetary Policy

  • RBI to conduct a 4-day Variable Rate Repo (VRR) auction on 09 November 2026 for ₹50,000 crore. The auction runs from 9:30 AM to 10:00 AM under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF), with reversal on 13 November 2026 (Friday). Operational guidelines are the same as in Press Release 2021-2022/1572 dated 20 January 2022. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) announced it on 06 November 2026 after reviewing "current and evolving liquidity conditions". It follows a week in which the RBI was absorbing liquidity, so it signals a shift in the liquidity picture.
  • A week of Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions, all cleared at a 5.49% cut-off. On 03 November, ₹50,000 crore was notified and ₹23,905 crore accepted. On 04 November, the first overnight auction drew ₹73,907 crore of offers against ₹50,000 crore notified, with ₹50,015 crore accepted and 67.64% partial acceptance. A second overnight auction the same day (₹25,000 crore notified) accepted only ₹1,180 crore. On 05 November, ₹7,767 crore was accepted against ₹75,000 crore notified, and the 3-day VRRR on 06 November accepted ₹18,133 crore against ₹25,000 crore notified. The statements in these notes do not restate the policy repo rate, so check the latest Monetary Policy Committee decision separately for that figure.
  • Liquidity operations data show heavy absorption at the end of October. The Weekly Statistical Supplement shows net absorption of ₹1,82,442 crore on 01 November 2026. Daily Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) balances ran between roughly ₹1.46 lakh crore and ₹2.05 lakh crore from 26 October to 01 November. The Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) saw small, volatile usage. This is the backdrop for the RBI's reverse repo auctions that week.
  • Foreign exchange reserves stood at US$ 717,056 million (₹68,08,728 crore) on 30 October 2026. They fell US$ 2,691 million over the week. Foreign Currency Assets were US$ 591,118 million (down US$ 2,088 million), gold was US$ 102,591 million (down US$ 559 million), and Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) were US$ 18,558 million. The Reserve Position in the International Monetary Fund (IMF) was US$ 4,790 million, the only component to rise (by US$ 3 million). The data come from the RBI Bulletin's Weekly Statistical Supplement extract of 06 November 2026.
  • RBI released provisional results of the 2025-26 Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA) census on 06 November 2026. The census covers 43,123 Indian entities with inward and/or outward direct investment: companies, limited liability partnerships (LLPs), Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) and partnership firms. About 97% were unlisted at end-March 2026, and non-financial companies held over 90% of Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) equity at face value. The inward-to-outward direct investment ratio in rupee terms moderated to 4.2 times from 5.5 times a year earlier. Total inward direct investment at market value was ₹70,71,945 crore (US$ 747,134 million), up 8.5%, and outward was ₹16,71,463 crore (US$ 176,586 million).
  • FLA census: country and sector patterns. The top sources of outstanding FDI were the United States (16.5%), Mauritius (14.5%), Singapore (13.7%) and the United Kingdom (10.8%), followed by the Netherlands (10.2%) and Japan (9.0%). For Overseas Direct Investment (ODI), Singapore, the United Arab Emirates and the United States together took more than 50%. Manufacturing held the largest share of FDI equity at market value (50.1%), against 45% for services. Other-investment liabilities grew 11.9% year-on-year and assets with unrelated non-residents grew 16.1%. FLA flows differ from Balance of Payments (BoP) flows because they include valuation changes from price and exchange-rate movements.
  • Government securities auction on 06 November 2026 raised ₹36,000 crore. Two re-issues were sold: 7.06% GS 2041 (₹23,000 crore; cut-off price ₹97.07, yield 7.3881%) and 7.43% GS 2076 (₹13,000 crore; cut-off price ₹95.70, yield 7.7708%). There was no devolvement on Primary Dealers. The Government of India (GoI) had the option to retain additional subscription of up to ₹2,000 crore against each security. Bids were placed on the RBI's e-Kuber Core Banking Solution using the multiple-price method.
  • Underwriting auction results fixed commissions for Primary Dealers. For 7.06% GS 2041 the Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) cut-off commission was 0.74 paise per ₹100, and for 7.43% GS 2076 it was 1.14 paise. Minimum Underwriting Commitment per Primary Dealer was ₹548 crore and ₹310 crore respectively. This follows the underwriting scheme notified on 14 November 2007.
  • Treasury Bill (T-bill) auctions: result of 04 November and notice for 11 November. In the 04 November auction, the 91-day bill cut off at ₹98.5511 (yield 5.8970%) and the 364-day at ₹93.7820 (yield 6.6485%), with no acceptance in the 182-day segment. The next auction on 11 November 2026 (settlement 12 November) is for ₹8,000 crore (91-day), ₹8,000 crore (182-day) and ₹7,000 crore (364-day), a total of ₹23,000 crore. Retail non-competitive allocation is capped at 5% of the notified amount, and bids go through e-Kuber or the Retail Direct portal.
  • RBI constituted the Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets (TCCFM) on 04 November 2026. It follows the Statement on Developmental and Regulatory Policies dated 07 October 2026. The Deputy Governor in charge of the Financial Markets Regulation Department chairs it. Its external members include Ananth Narayan, Shamsher Singh (Chairman, Fixed Income Money Market and Derivatives Association of India, FIMMDA), Neeraj Gambhir, Shailendra Jhingan, Aditya Bagree, Nand Kishore (Clearing Corporation of India Limited, CCIL) and Ridham Desai. It covers money, government securities and foreign exchange markets and their derivatives and infrastructure, and meets at least once a quarter.
  • RBI launched the November 2026 rounds of three household surveys on 03 November. The Rural Consumer Confidence Survey covers 31 states/UTs and is conducted by Ipsos Research Private Ltd., Gurugram. The Urban Consumer Confidence Survey and the Inflation Expectations Survey of Households each cover 19 cities and are conducted by Hansa Research Group Pvt. Ltd., Mumbai. Results feed into monetary policy, and the inflation expectations survey asks about price changes three months and one year ahead.
  • RBI Citizen's Charter status for October 2026. Opening pendency was 2,666 applications and 15,725 were received, with 707 referred back, giving a total of 17,684. Of 15,186 applications processed, 15,164 (99.9%) met the timeline. Pending applications at month-end were 2,498, of which 15 (0.6%) were beyond timeline. The number of services under the Charter is 206, and online filing is through the PRAVAAH portal.
02

Banking & Financial Sector

  • Scheduled commercial bank deposits and credit grew sharply year-on-year as of 15 October 2026. Aggregate deposits were ₹2,78,75,910 crore (up 16.7% year-on-year) and bank credit was ₹2,27,42,777 crore (up 18.2%). Broad money M3 stood at ₹3,33,00,300 crore (up 16.0%). Time deposits rose 17.1% and currency with the public rose 14.1%. Under the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025, a fortnight is now defined as the 15th and last calendar day of a month, effective 15 December 2025, replacing alternate Fridays.
  • ★ NBFC credit grew 15.4% year-on-year in September 2026, against 13.3% a year earlier. The RBI's sectoral deployment data cover non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) in the Upper and Middle Layers plus housing finance companies (HFCs), about 87% of total credit per the Report on Trend and Progress of Banking in India 2024-25. Agriculture and allied credit grew 17.8% (against 5.2%), industry slowed to 7.4% (from 10.2%) with infrastructure subdued, and services moderated to 17.3% (from 23.0%). Retail loans accelerated to 21.9% (from 16.8%), led by housing, loans against gold jewellery and vehicle loans.
  • ★ RBI released draft consolidated Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Directions for five regulated-entity types. The five drafts cover Commercial Banks, Small Finance Banks, Local Area Banks, Regional Rural Banks and Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs), each titled "Priority Sector Lending – Targets and Classification) Directions, 2026". Instructions not included in the consolidated drafts will be repealed once the Directions are issued. Comments are due by 26 November 2026 via the "Connect 2 Regulate" section of the RBI website or by e-mail. This continues the RBI's ongoing consolidation of its instructions regulated-entity-wise.
  • ★ RBI extended Section 35A directions on UCBs under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (read with Section 56). The Pusad Urban Co-operative Bank Ltd. (Yavatmal, Maharashtra) was extended by three months, from 07 November 2026 to 07 February 2027. Urban Co-operative Bank Limited, Dehradun was extended by three months, from 10 November 2026 to 10 February 2027. Sri Guru Raghavendra Sahakara Bank Niyamitha, Bengaluru was extended by six months, to 10 May 2027, having first been placed under directions on 02 January 2020. In each case the RBI states that the extension does not mean it is satisfied with the bank's financial position.
  • State Development Loan auction of 03 November 2026 allotted ₹27,805.607 crore against ₹29,900 crore notified. Cut-off yields ran roughly from 7.75% (Maharashtra SGS 2031) to 8.17% (Telangana SGS 2046). Maharashtra accepted nothing in the re-issue of 7.70% Maharashtra SGS 2049. Gujarat and Punjab each accepted an additional ₹500 crore in specific re-issues, and West Bengal accepted partial amounts. Another auction of ₹29,050 crore (face value) across 12 States/UTs is scheduled on 09 November 2026 on e-Kuber, with interest on new stock paid half-yearly on 11 May and 11 November. State Government securities count as eligible Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) investments.
  • The Competition Commission of India (CCI) approved Kotak Mahindra Bank's acquisition of Deutsche Bank AG's India retail, private banking and wealth management business. The deal is a slump sale, with Kotak as acquirer and Deutsche Bank Aktiengesellschaft as seller, as announced on 04 November 2026. The CCI also approved a Gentari Renewables India Pte. Ltd. and Sapphire US HoldCo (USA), LLC transaction, the target being a Petronas-group renewable power and battery storage business in India.
  • ★ The PMAY-G Reform Samvad in Lonavala explored institutional financing for rural housing. Organised by the Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD) and the National Housing Bank (NHB), it brought together HFCs, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Small Finance Banks (SFBs), Micro Finance Institutions (MFIs), the World Bank and the International Finance Corporation (IFC). It discussed financing for at least 4.95 crore pucca houses by March 2029 through loans of ₹2–5 lakh with repayment aligned to seasonal incomes. Recommendations included a pilot NHB-supported model, a partial guarantee, interest subvention, NHB refinance, mortgage and stamp duty reforms, and delivery via Gram Panchayats and DAY-NRLM self-help groups. It also asked for regulatory treatment of rural borrowers on non-performing asset (NPA) and provisioning norms.
  • The Small Industries (SRI) Fund workshop on women-led MSMEs reported its progress as of 30 October 2026. Commitments stood at ₹8,305 crore against a ₹10,000 crore corpus, with ₹48,000 crore of private capital leveraged. Investments reached 824 MSMEs, of which 74% were micro and 77 had moved to a larger category. Six listed on stock exchanges, and 32 exits were profitable. Udyam and Udyam Assist registrations exceed 10 crore, of which 37.6% are women-owned, and the MSME NPA ratio was cited as 3.5%.
  • Digital Life Certificate (DLC) Campaign 5.0 is run by the Department of Pension and Pensioners' Welfare (DoPPW) with the State Bank of India (SBI) and other banks. It runs from 06 to 30 November 2026 across more than 2,000 cities and towns via the Jeevan Pramaan app, which uses face authentication. The aim is to cut in-person visits for pensioners. A 02 November mega-camp targeted over 2 crore DLCs nationwide, and SBI handled about 25 lakh in the previous campaign.
03

Government Schemes & Budget

  • ★ Cabinet approved continuation of the Fast Track Special Courts (FTSC) scheme from FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31 with an outlay of ₹3,788.04 crore. The central share of ₹2,353.15 crore comes from the Nirbhaya Fund and the State/UT share is ₹1,434.89 crore. The scheme has operated since 02 October 2019 and had been extended to FY 2025-26. Functional FTSCs now number 779 (399 exclusive POCSO courts) across 29 participating States/UTs, with 4,02,457 cases disposed since inception. The plan is 850 FTSCs, each with one judicial officer, one Special Public Prosecutor and six support staff.
  • ★ Cabinet approved VISTAR (Viksit Bharat Initiative for Statistical Transformation, Analytics & Research) with an outlay of ₹4,371.10 crore for FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31. It has three components: comprehensive statistical operations, Support for Statistical Strengthening (SSS 2.0) for States/UTs, and the 8th Economic Census. The plan is 59 surveys in the 16th Finance Commission cycle against 43 earlier, with AI/ML embedded in statistical workflows. The 8th Economic Census will give non-farm establishment data down to Gram Panchayat and ward level and a baseline for a National Statistical Business Register.
  • ★ The Capex Support Scheme for Critical Mineral Processing carries an outlay of ₹2,814 crore for FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31. It gives a 30% capital subsidy on eligible investment, paid in three milestone-linked tranches. It targets two lithium plants (at least 30 kt each, maximum support ₹500 crore) and two nickel plants (at least 50 kt each, maximum ₹900 crore), with cobalt as a by-product. Selection is two-stage, technical qualification then least-cost financial bidding. The aim is to cut import dependence for batteries, electric mobility, defence and steel.
  • ★ Viability Gap Funding (VGF) for grid-scale energy storage totals ₹14,500 crore (FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31). It targets 92 GWh of uncommissioned capacity: 30 GWh battery energy storage (BESS), 60 GWh pumped storage and 2 GWh alternative technologies. Estimated investment is ₹97,600 crore, BESS must meet a 40% domestic content requirement, and projects are allocated by tariff-based competitive bidding.
  • ★ The National Green Hydrogen Mission (NGHM), approved in January 2023, has an outlay of ₹19,744 crore up to 2029-30. Its Strategic Interventions for Green Hydrogen Transition (SIGHT) programme carries ₹17,490 crore. The standard requires emissions of no more than 2 kg CO₂-equivalent per kg of hydrogen, the Green Hydrogen Certification Scheme was launched in April 2025, and green hydrogen and ammonia plants get a 25-year inter-state transmission charge waiver. At the Bharat Renewable Energy Summit, green hydrogen was cited at ₹279/kg and green ammonia at ₹49.75/kg, described as the lowest globally.
  • ★ The Prime Minister highlighted solar and renewable schemes at the Bharat Renewable Energy Summit & Expo 2026 (02–05 November, Bharat Mandapam). PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (launched February 2024) has reached 65 lakh households, about 1 lakh every six days. The Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana has an outlay above ₹5,000 crore for 5,000 MW of floating solar, and Green Energy Corridor Phase-III is cited at ₹1.86 lakh crore. PM-KUSUM, with central assistance of ₹34,422 crore, reaches over 30 lakh farmers. The summit coincided with the 9th International Solar Alliance Assembly and the 4th Conference on Green Hydrogen.
  • ★ Regular tax devolution of ₹1,09,019 crore to States was released on 05 November 2026. This was ahead of the usual 10 November date. Among the visible State allocations, Bihar received ₹10,845 crore, Madhya Pradesh ₹8,010 crore, Maharashtra ₹7,022 crore and Rajasthan ₹6,460 crore, with Sikkim at ₹365 crore and Goa at ₹398 crore. Devolution flows from the Union divisible pool to States under Finance Commission recommendations.
  • The Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme has an outlay of ₹1,856.67 crore. It supports investment, manufacturing zones, clusters, production incentives, testing, skilling, start-ups and branding to build a domestic sports-goods ecosystem and raise exports. A Khelo Bharat quiz titled "From Manufacturing to Podium Finish" runs from 04 to 20 November 2026, with Fit India merchandise for the top 100.
  • A pathway for Agniveers was outlined under a Cabinet proposal. Under it, 25% would be offered regular merit-based employment in the Defence Forces, and the remaining 75% would get another two-year engagement. After that, all Agniveers would be eligible to enrol as regular soldiers, subject to willingness and selection. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh referred to this as a "second tour of duty", with better remuneration and Seva Nidhi promised for the extended term.
  • Soreng became India's first notified Organic Fisheries Cluster under the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY). Union Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh (Lalan Singh) inaugurated projects worth ₹375 lakh in Sikkim, including hatcheries, feed mills, cold storage, Biofloc and Recirculatory Aquaculture System (RAS) technologies. A Standard Operating Procedure for organic fisheries certification in the North-East was launched, along with an MoU with the Indo-Tibetan Border Police for procuring local Rainbow Trout. The North District Cooperative Milk Producers' Union was launched under White Revolution 2.0, and a Siri cattle conservation programme and Centre of Excellence were announced.
  • The Ministry of Rural Development notified Grievance Redressal Rules, 2026 under the VB-G RAM G Act. They set up a three-tier mechanism at Gram Panchayat, Block and District levels, with enquiry in 7 working days and resolution in 15 days. A joint advisory by the Department of Rural Development and the Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying (04 November) lists 22 permissible fodder and livestock works, with 5% of programme expenditure reserved for them. The Act took effect on 01 July 2026.
  • The Department of Science and Technology and other ministries announced the Rs 1 lakh crore Research Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund. It is structured as 50:50 equity and was announced on 27 October 2026 under the Anusandhan framework. Dr Jitendra Singh also outlined the Deep Ocean Mission goal of a first crewed deep-sea dive to 6,000 m by 2028 using the Matsya submersible.
04

Economy & Trade

  • The Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) 2024-25 shows registered manufacturing Gross Value Added (GVA) up 9.28% year-on-year. Net Value Added per factory rose 6.59% and GVA per person engaged rose 2.25%. The top five industries (food products, textiles, basic metals, motor vehicles, wearing apparel) account for over 41% of manufacturing employment, and the top five States (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh) for over 54% of GVA. The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) published the results.
  • Indian Railways freight loading in October 2026 was 145.07 million tonnes against 133.93 million tonnes a year earlier, growth of 8.32%. Food grains rose 26.3% and domestic coal 16.0% (about 469.8 rakes a day against 393.2). Cumulative freight for April–October 2026 was 978.78 million tonnes (up 4.67%). Passenger traffic was 64.72 crore in October 2026 and 443.8 crore for April–October.
  • Finished steel consumption in April–October 2026 rose 6.6% to 14.3 Mt, and India remained a net importer. Imports were 0.76 Mt (up 65.8%) while exports fell 32.0% to 0.44 Mt. Crude steel production was 14.1 Mt (up 0.7%), finished steel production 13.4 Mt (up 2.8%), and hot metal production fell 2.9%. The public-sector share of finished steel production dropped from 15.5% to 13.9%.
  • Coal output data for October 2026 show mixed trends. Captive and commercial mines produced 22.03 MT (up 42% year-on-year) and dispatched 21.11 MT (up 36%). Total April–October production was 105.33 MT (down 11.30%) with dispatch of 111.95 MT (down 11.53%). The Lohari mine in Jharkhand opened, bringing operational mines to 87. Western Coalfields Limited (WCL) reached 102.47% of its April–October production target, with 32.96 MT produced.
  • The National Land Monetization Corporation (NLMC) completed Phase II of the Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL) land e-auction on 03 November 2026. 114 parcels fetched ₹218 crore against a reserve of ₹184.81 crore (a 17.96% premium). Combined with Phase I (12 October), 264 parcels fetched ₹584.33 crore against a reserve of ₹497.29 crore, a 17.5% premium.
  • ★ India–European Union (EU) Free Trade Agreement talks featured in the Commerce Ministry's engagements. Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal met EU officials in Prague on trade facilitation and customs procedures, and discussed the Antwerp diamond ecosystem linking Mumbai, Surat and Antwerp. Minister of State Jitin Prasada visited Poland on 2–3 November with 12 Indian companies and met Poland's Secretary of State Ignacy Niemczycki on India–EU FTA opportunities. A India-Poland Business Forum was held at the Polish National Chamber of Commerce in Warsaw on 3 November.
  • The Gem & Jewellery Export Promotion Council (GJEPC) signed an MoU with the African Diamond Producers Association (ADPA) on 04 November 2026. It aims at capacity building and skills in sorting, valuation and manufacturing, and trade and investment links across the diamond value chain.
  • NPPA slashed prices of anti-cancer medicines with effect from 09 November 2026. The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority revised 1,225 formulations under notification SO 5690(E) dated 17 October 2026, with the highest reduction at 96.45% and estimated annual savings of ₹3,502.31 crore. Trade margins are capped at 30%. For example, Bevacizumab 100 mg falls from ₹25,995 to ₹3,318 and Abiraterone acetate 250 mg from ₹41,250 to ₹3,218.
  • E20 petrol quality tests found all 893 samples across 27 States/UTs compliant with IS 17021 (ethanol 19–21%). Karnataka's 56 samples averaged 19.30% ethanol, and the Petroleum Ministry said claims of 26–31% ethanol there were not supported. Samples were drawn by Indian Oil, BPCL and HPCL.
  • The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) seized 2.080 kg of gold jewellery worth ₹2.73 crore in Coimbatore on 04 November 2026. The jewellery lacked the 6-digit Hallmarking Unique Identification (HUID). The action was under Section 28 of the BIS Act, 2016 and gazette orders of 23 June 2021 and 3 March 2023, with penalties of up to one year's imprisonment or fines up to five times the value of goods under Section 29.
  • Jaipur became the first State capital to house all 46 Commerce and Industry organisations under one roof (Vanijya Bhawan and Udyog Bhawan). All licences, permits and SCOMET approvals are to be online by 31 March 2027, with no physical visit needed for any service from 1 April 2027.
  • Agriculture GVA for 2025-26 is cited at ₹52.08 lakh crore. Agrivoltaics now has 36 operational projects totalling 37.54 MW (August 2025). India's space economy is about US$ 9 billion with a target of US$ 40–45 billion over the next decade, backed by about 450 space start-ups.

International Affairs & Organizations

  • India held the inaugural India–Canada Defence Dialogue virtually on 03 November 2026. It was co-chaired by Dr Amit Telang of the Ministry of Defence (International Cooperation) and Scott Millar, Canada's Assistant Deputy Minister. It covered defence industrial cooperation, military-technical cooperation and research and development, and refers to the Joint Statement of 2 March 2026 by PM Modi and PM Carney.
  • India chairs the Kimberley Process (KP) in 2026 and held a stakeholder consultation in Luanda on 03 November. The chair is Yashvir Singh (Additional Secretary, Commerce), and India's theme is "Confidence, Compliance and Credibility". India also chaired in 2008 and 2019, and the KP was set up under UN General Assembly Resolution 55/56 (2000). The KP 2026 Plenary is scheduled for 30 November to 4 December 2026 at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi.
  • ADPA marks its 20th anniversary on 27–28 November 2026. It was established on 04 November 2006 and is headquartered in Luanda. It has 15 effective members and 8 observers, including non-African observers Russia and India, and its members produce more than 60% of global diamonds.
  • India released Guidelines for Other Effective Area-based Conservation Measures (OECMs) at CBD COP-17 in Yerevan, Armenia, on 03 November 2026. The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change and the National Biodiversity Authority framed them to support the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework Target 3 (30x30) and the National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan 2024–2030. The linked project, running 15 May 2026 to 15 May 2032, has USD 60.79 million of funding and aims to add 3% of India's land and marine area to the conservation estate.
  • The Indo-Pacific Regional Dialogue (IPRD) 2026, the Indian Navy's apex conference, was held in New Delhi on 3–5 November. It drew participants from 20 countries, with Defence Minister Rajnath Singh speaking on maritime supply chains and the MAHASAGAR concept, and reinforced India's maritime vision. India's Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative dates to 2019.
  • Indian naval ships were active abroad. INS Sahyadri took part in MALABAR 2026 (India, US, Australia, Japan) at Yokosuka, Japan, on 04 November. INS Tarkash reached Port Louis, Mauritius, on 03 November for Aapravasi Diwas 2026 events and joint EEZ surveillance. INS Sudarshini returned to Kochi on 02 November after Lokayan 26, a 10-month, 22,000 nm voyage with 17 port calls in 11 countries.
  • Climate and Global South partnerships. NITI Aayog and GIZ signed the NDC-TIA II agreement on 03 November to decarbonise India's road freight. The Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) signed a Letter of Association with DAKSHIN on 03 November for Global South research and capacity building. The Bharat Renewable Energy Summit had nine country partners: Australia, Denmark, the EU, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the UK.
  • MoSPI and UN Women held a national conference on gender data on 02 November 2026. It discussed the "Women and Men in India 2025" publication across four sessions: health and survival, girls' education, women's work and wages, and livelihoods and financial inclusion.

Sports / Environment / Science & Tech

  • India won 114 medals at the Asian Para Games 2026 in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan (20–31 October). The tally was 30 gold, 35 silver and 49 bronze. India swept all podium places in four events, and the Prime Minister interacted with the contingent on 05 November 2026.
  • India has 18 notified Biosphere Reserves, of which 13 are in UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves (WNBR). Examples include Nilgiri (5,520 sq km), Nanda Devi (6,407.03 sq km, Uttarakhand), Gulf of Mannar (10,500 sq km), Sundarbans (9,630 sq km), Great Rann of Kutch (12,454 sq km, Gujarat), Cold Desert (Himachal Pradesh) and Panna (Madhya Pradesh). Grants-in-aid run 60:40 Centre:State and 90:10 for the North-East and Himalayan States. Pilot five-year Integrated Management Plans cover Nanda Devi, Pachmarhi, Dehang-Dibang and Agasthyamalai.
  • The first two geothermal wells were commissioned at Puga Valley, Ladakh, in July 2026. Each is 1,000 m deep and they support India's first 1-MW demonstration geothermal plant. The National Policy on Geothermal Energy was noted in September 2025. India's theoretical geothermal resource is about 10.6 GW, with 42 sites identified and 381 hot springs mapped.
  • Pumped storage hydropower capacity stood at 7.43 GW across 10 projects in August 2026. Another 15.87 GW is under construction across 11 projects, and total potential is 345.38 GW. The Central Electricity Authority's roadmap targets 100 GW by 2031-32 and 100.81 GW by 2035-36, with an inter-state transmission charge waiver for projects commissioned before 30 June 2028.
  • The Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) successfully flight-tested the quasi-ballistic missile Pralay on 05 November 2026.
