RBI & Monetary Policy

  • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) absorbed surplus liquidity through Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions, all clearing at a 5.24% cut-off rate, between 24 and 28 August 2026. RBI accepted ₹91,980 crore in a 7-day auction (₹2,50,000 crore notified) and ₹49,206 crore in a second overnight auction (₹1,50,000 crore notified) on 24 August. It then accepted ₹98,516 crore in a 2-day auction (₹1,75,000 crore) on 25 August, ₹1,42,622 crore in an overnight auction (₹2,00,000 crore) on 27 August and ₹1,52,537 crore in a 3-day auction (₹3,00,000 crore) on 28 August. VRRR is a Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) tool, and every offer was accepted because bids fell short of the notified amount. The guidelines remain those of Press Release 2019-2020/1947 dated 13 February 2020.
  • RBI announced a 15-day VRRR for Monday, 31 August 2026 with a notified amount of ₹6,00,000 crore. The window is 9:30 AM to 10:00 AM and the reversal date is 15 September 2026 (Tuesday). This is the largest notified amount and the longest tenor in the week's series, extending liquidity absorption beyond the overnight to 3-day operations.
  • ★ The RBI Bulletin for August 2026 (released 25 August) carries the bi-monthly monetary policy statement of the 3–5 August 2026 Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meeting, six speeches and one article on the State of the Economy. The article describes a resilient domestic economy despite geopolitical fragility and trade uncertainty. It cites buoyant domestic demand, a south-west monsoon that picked up in July after a June deficit, and fractionally higher headline Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation driven by food, with stable core inflation. It also notes easier liquidity and a rebound in foreign capital inflows. The supplied material does not quote the repo rate, so check it in the 5 August resolution; the inflation target is 4% with a 2% band on either side, and a breach of the 6% upper limit for three consecutive quarters counts as a failure.
  • India's foreign exchange reserves were US$729,328 million (₹69,81,330 crore) on 21 August 2026, up US$12,422 million over the week. Foreign Currency Assets were US$591,333 million, gold US$114,218 million, Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) US$18,852 million and the Reserve Position in the International Monetary Fund (IMF) US$4,925 million. Reserves are up US$38,221 million since end-March 2026 and US$38,608 million year-on-year. Gold added US$2,801 million in the week but is US$1,177 million lower than at end-March 2026.
  • Broad money (M3) stood at ₹3,22,94,503 crore on 15 August 2026, up 14.2% year-on-year, in data released on 28 August. M3 rose 0.1% in the fortnight and 2.7% in the financial year so far (from ₹3,14,53,126 crore on 31 March 2026). Currency with the public grew 12.9% year-on-year to ₹42,07,115 crore and time deposits grew 14.2%. The Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025 redefined a fortnight from alternate Fridays to the 15th and last calendar day of a month, effective 15 December 2025.
  • Daily LAF data show net absorption easing from ₹3,72,469 crore on 17 August to ₹1,53,390 crore on 23 August 2026. On 17 August the VRRR took ₹1,95,859 crore and the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) ₹1,76,657 crore. By 22–23 August no VRRR was outstanding and absorption relied on the SDF (₹1,60,047 crore and ₹1,62,921 crore). Use of the Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) rose to ₹9,531 crore on 23 August.
  • The 6.94% Government Stock (GS) 2036, maturing 11 May 2036, was auctioned on 28 August 2026 for ₹34,000 crore at a cut-off yield of 6.9136%. The cut-off price was ₹100.17 and the weighted average yield 6.9122% (price ₹100.18). Of 383 competitive bids worth ₹82,151.762 crore, 117 bids worth ₹33,995.602 crore were accepted, with 97.9374% partial allotment for 47 bids. Three non-competitive bids worth ₹4.398 crore were accepted and devolvement on Primary Dealers (PDs) was NIL. The auction used the multiple price method on the RBI Core Banking Solution (E-Kuber) with settlement on 31 August, and the Government of India (GoI) could retain ₹2,000 crore extra.
  • In the underwriting auction for the same security, the Minimum Underwriting Commitment (MUC) was ₹810 crore per PD, with an equal minimum bid under Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU). The total MUC was ₹17,010 crore, ACU accepted was ₹16,990 crore and total underwritten was ₹34,000 crore. The ACU commission cut-off was 0.39 paise per ₹100. The scheme dates from the circular of 14 November 2007.
  • GoI announced a ₹30,000 crore buyback of four securities, with the auction on 3 September 2026 and settlement on 4 September. The securities are the 7.33% GS 2026 (30 October 2026), 5.74% GS 2026 (15 November 2026), 8.15% GS 2026 (24 November 2026) and 8.24% GS 2027 (15 February 2027). There is no security-wise notified amount, and the multiple price method applies. GoI may accept more or less than ₹30,000 crore.
  • The Treasury Bill (T-bill) auction results of 27 August 2026 showed cut-off yields of 5.2640% (91-day), 5.6181% (182-day) and 5.7968% (364-day). Cut-off prices were ₹98.7046, ₹97.2750 and ₹94.5350 against notified amounts of ₹9,000, ₹8,000 and ₹7,000 crore. Weighted average yields were 5.2579%, 5.5884% and 5.7708%. Non-competitive bids accepted were ₹14,861 crore, ₹4,946.350 crore and ₹350 crore respectively.
  • RBI notified the next T-bill auction for ₹24,000 crore on 2 September 2026, with settlement on 3 September. The split is ₹9,000 crore (91-day), ₹8,000 crore (182-day) and ₹7,000 crore (364-day), using the multiple price method. Retail non-competitive allocation is capped at 5% of the notified amount, and States, Union Territories with legislature, eligible Provident Funds and designated Foreign Central Banks bid outside the notified amount.
  • The State Government Securities (SGS) auction of 25 August 2026 raised ₹20,100 crore for seven States. The States were Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Haryana, Maharashtra, Punjab, Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu. Cut-off yields ranged from 7.09% (Maharashtra 5-year) to 7.7498% (Punjab SGS 2039 re-issue). RBI received 1,029 competitive bids worth ₹53,804.500 crore.
  • Fifteen States will raise ₹27,000 crore through SGS auctions on 1 September 2026 (Tuesday), with payment on 2 September. The States are Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Manipur, Odisha, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and West Bengal. Up to 10% of each stock's notified amount is reserved for non-competitive bids, with a 1% cap per bid, and retail investors can use the Retail Direct portal. New stock pays interest half-yearly on 2 March and 2 September, and SGS count towards the Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
  • The RBI's USD-INR Swap Facility, launched on 8 June 2026, had drawn US$73 billion of foreign exchange inflows by 21 August 2026. Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) [FCNR(B)] deposits are cited at US$65.40 billion. The release compares this with the 2013 scheme, which raised US$26 billion over about three months, making it a useful reference on RBI's forex-mobilisation tools.
02

Banking & Financial Sector

  • ★ RBI placed Ashok Sahakari Bank Ltd., Ahmednagar under all-inclusive directions from the close of business on 28 August 2026. Under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (BR Act), the bank cannot grant loans, make investments, accept fresh deposits or allow any withdrawal from savings, current or other accounts without RBI's written approval. It may set off loans against deposits and meet essential expenses such as salaries and rent. The directions run for six months, and eligible depositors can claim up to ₹5,00,000 from the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC); RBI clarifies this is not cancellation of the licence.
  • ★ The Citizens Co-operative Bank Ltd., Jammu was brought under similar directions from the close of business on 27 August 2026 (directive dated 25 August), with withdrawals capped at ₹1,25,000 per depositor. The bank cannot grant or renew loans, make investments or accept fresh deposits without RBI approval. The directions are valid for six months and the DICGC cover is ₹5,00,000. RBI cited a lack of concrete efforts by the bank to address supervisory concerns.
  • ★ Desaiganj Nagari Cooperative Bank Maryadit, Desaiganj was placed under directions from the close of business on 27 August 2026 (directive dated 25 August), with no withdrawals allowed. The restrictions, valid for six months and subject to review, again rest on Sections 35A and 56 of the BR Act. Together with the Ashok Sahakari and Citizens actions, they continue RBI's pattern of tight supervisory directions on weak co-operative banks, with DICGC cover of ₹5,00,000.
  • ★ RBI penalised two Karnataka co-operative banks for asset-classification and related lapses under its Urban Co-operative Bank (UCB) directions. Vikas Souharda Co-operative Bank Limited, Hosapete was fined ₹1 lakh for failing to classify certain loan accounts as Non-Performing Assets (NPAs). Shri Vijay Mahantesh Co-operative Bank Limited, Hungund was fined ₹2.50 lakh for the same lapse and for sanctioning director-related loans. Both orders are dated 20 August 2026 under Section 47A(1)(c) read with Sections 46(4)(i) and 56 of the BR Act, based on inspection of the position as on 31 March 2025.
  • ★ The Pragathi Co-operative Bank Limited, Karnataka was fined ₹2 lakh by an order dated 20 August 2026. RBI found contravention of Section 20 read with Section 56 of the BR Act and breach of UCB directions on loans to directors and their relatives, exposure norms and the investment portfolio. The charges were sanctioning director-related loans, breaching the single borrower exposure limit, and breaching the prudential inter-bank (gross) exposure limit and the inter-bank counter-party limit.
  • ★ Jalna District Central Co-operative Bank Ltd., Maharashtra was fined ₹50,000 by an order dated 25 August 2026. The charges were failing to report borrowers' credit information to all Credit Information Companies (CICs) and failing to review risk categorisation of accounts at least once in six months. The penalty was imposed under Section 25(1)(iii) read with Section 23(4) of the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005 and the BR Act, and covers directions on CIC membership and Know Your Customer (KYC) norms. The statutory inspection was conducted by the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD).
  • Scheduled commercial banks' aggregate deposits were ₹2,69,31,346 crore on 15 August 2026, up 14.7% year-on-year, while bank credit was ₹2,20,07,764 crore, up 18.3%. Credit growth therefore exceeded deposit growth by 3.6 percentage points. Time deposits were ₹2,36,28,953 crore against demand deposits of ₹33,02,392 crore, and food credit was ₹1,14,136 crore.
  • ★ Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) posted a record net profit of ₹10,176 crore in FY 2025-26, against ₹6,820 crore in FY 2024-25. Their business crossed ₹13.5 lakh crore, with a Gross Non-Performing Assets (GNPA) ratio of 5.3% and a net NPA ratio of 2.1%. The credit-deposit ratio was 75.2%, and more than 54.98 lakh Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY) accounts were opened in FY 2025-26.
  • A conclave on the Small Industries Development Bank of India (SIDBI)–RRB MSME Co-lending arrangement brought together 28 RRB chairmen and Department of Financial Services (DFS) Secretary Sanjay Lohiya. The digital platform aims at faster sanction and disbursement and a paperless credit journey with digital documentation. It extends co-lending to rural banks, deepening MSME credit delivery.
  • ★ The Unified Payments Interface (UPI) completed ten years, with 24,162 crore transactions worth ₹314 lakh crore in FY 2025-26 against 1.78 crore transactions worth ₹0.07 lakh crore in FY 2016-17. Volume grew 30% and value 21% in FY 2025-26. UPI had 741 live banks in July 2026 (44 in April 2016), recorded a record 2,366 crore transactions in July 2026 and averages 66 crore transactions a day. It accounts for 84% of India's digital payments in FY 2025-26 and 49% of global real-time payment volume in 2025.
  • UPI product limits and reach were highlighted alongside the anniversary. The UPI-123Pay limit is ₹10,000 and the UPI-Lite wallet limit is ₹5,000, with UPI-Circle among the milestones. Cross-border UPI is stated as operational in 11 countries, among them Bhutan, Nepal, Singapore, the UAE, France, Sri Lanka, Mauritius, Qatar, Greece and the Maldives. The Prime Minister invited citizens to share their UPI experiences.
  • India Post's Dak MITRA mobile interface now supports 1.4 lakh Branch Post Offices. It enables paperless, real-time transactions, OTP-based delivery, core banking, Aadhaar e-KYC, insurance and cash operations. The Dak Sewa App offers 24×7 tracking, booking, complaints and a PIN locator in 23 Indian languages through BHASHINI. Fourteen renovated post offices were inaugurated on 24 August 2026, and the remaining 26 of the 40-office phase are due by 31 August 2026.
  • The Competition Commission of India (CCI) approved two acquisitions. Cyient Ltd. will acquire 100% of Tao Digital Solutions Inc., and Yancoal Australia Ltd. will acquire Kestrel Coal Group Pty Ltd. in a 100% stake purchase from certain sellers. Both reflect routine merger control under the Competition Act.
  • Parishrama Adalat in Hyderabad resolved 65 Micro and Small Enterprise (MSE) delayed-payment cases worth ₹8.29 crore. The conciliation conclave involved MSE Facilitation Councils, public-sector buyers, the Ministry of MSME and the Government of Telangana. The Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform, launched in 2025, has seen more than 800 cases filed in Telangana, with about 85 resolved by August 2026.
03

Government Schemes & Budget

  • ★ Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) has an allocation of ₹12,200 crore for 2026-27. Since Kharif 2016 until Rabi 2025-26, 92.46 crore farmer applications were insured and 26.33 crore received claims exceeding ₹2.06 lakh crore. Farmers pay 2% for Kharif foodgrain and oilseed crops, 1.5% for Rabi foodgrain and oilseed crops and 5% for commercial and horticultural crops. Central and State shares are 50:50, and 90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan States and Union Territories.
  • ★ PMFBY coverage in Kharif 2026 is 241.38 lakh farmers on 278.12 lakh hectares, up from 229.77 lakh farmers and 269.38 lakh hectares in Kharif 2025. Claims paid for Kharif 2025 were ₹9,837.61 crore to 60.89 lakh eligible farmers, and 1.44 crore tenant and share-cropper farmers have enrolled since 2018. Technology tools include YES-TECH (remote-sensing yield estimation), WINDS (weather network), Digi-Claim (over ₹55,000 crore paid through the Public Financial Management System) and the CCE-Agri App.
  • ★ Per Drop More Crop (PDMC) funds drip and sprinkler irrigation, with ₹8,123.24 crore of central assistance released over the last three years. Support covers up to 5 hectares per beneficiary, at 55% for small and marginal farmers and 45% for others. The scheme began as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme (Micro Irrigation) in 2006 and became the National Mission on Micro Irrigation (NMMI) in 2010-11. It was part of the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana in FY 2014-15 to 2021-22 and has sat under the Pradhan Mantri Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (PM-RKVY) since FY 2022-23.
  • ★ PDMC's 2025-26 figures were ₹3,226.36 crore within a PM-RKVY sanction of ₹8,957.72 crore. Micro-irrigation covered 115 lakh hectares in July 2026 (about 8.11% of net sown area), and 12.30 lakh farmers benefited in 2025-26, about 20% of them women. Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat and Rajasthan have the highest coverage. New flexibility lets States raise water-storage and conservation funding above the earlier caps of 20% (40% for the North-East, Himalayan States, Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh).
  • ★ PM SVANidhi, launched on 1 June 2020, was restructured on 27 August 2025 and extended to 31 March 2030. The target is 1.15 crore vendors, including 50 lakh new beneficiaries. In the year after restructuring, 21.86 lakh loans went to 10.56 lakh new beneficiaries, totalling ₹6,294 crore. Since inception, 78.68 lakh vendors have accessed 1.18 crore loans worth ₹19,171 crore, with interest subsidy of ₹421 crore released.
  • ★ PM SVANidhi's newer components include a UPI-linked RuPay Credit Card with a limit up to ₹30,000 (27,077 cards sanctioned). Fifty Street Food Hubs are planned with assistance up to ₹4 crore per hub plus ₹25 lakh for vending-zone notification. Digital transactions by 57.09 lakh vendors total ₹908 crore, and 6 lakh vendors have received Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) training. The Mann Ki Baat of 30 August noted 35 lakh women beneficiaries (46% share).
  • ★ The Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY) has 59.09 crore accounts as on 19 August 2026, up from 14.72 crore in 2015. Deposits are ₹3,16,514 crore (₹15,670 crore in March 2015), with an average of ₹5,356 per account. RuPay cards issued total 41.29 crore, and women hold 55.7% of accounts, with 32.92 crore female beneficiaries. Rural and semi-urban accounts are 77.8%, and PMJDY holds a Guinness record of 1,80,96,130 accounts opened in a single week of January 2015.
  • ★ PMJDY benefits cited include an accident insurance cover of ₹1 lakh, raised to ₹2 lakh from August 2018, and an overdraft of ₹10,000. The overdraft is available to one account per household after six months of satisfactory operation. These features explain why PMJDY is frequently combined with RuPay, direct benefit transfer and insurance questions.
  • ★ Union Budget 2026-27 introduced a ₹600 crore Integrated Scheme in Skilling Architecture (ISSA). The "Reimagining Skilling for Viksit Bharat@2047" report identifies 5 learner segments and notes 8.7 crore youth not in education, employment or training (NEET), cautioning that this group should not be read as simply unemployed.
  • ★ Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation (AMRUT) 2.0 has ₹71,133 crore allocated for sewerage and septage management. A review chaired by Minister of State Tokhan Sahu noted 394 projects worth ₹47,230 crore under implementation across 500 cities. Expected outcomes are 48.76 lakh new sewerage connections and upgrades benefiting 60.59 lakh households. The same review covered the Gurugram Metro (Millennium City Centre to Cyber City, with a spur to the Dwarka Expressway) and CITIIS 2.0, for which no figures were released.
  • PM-SETU funds Industrial Training Institute (ITI) upgrades, with a Hub ITI and four Spoke ITIs attracting ₹241 crore. The split is 50% Central, 33% State and 17% industry partners, using a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) with 51% industry ownership and 24.5% each for the Centre and the State. Thirty-two ITIs in 8 clusters are identified, and ₹1,500 crore of industry-led investment is committed across 7 clusters in 5 States. The National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC) and the Lucknow Cantonment Board signed an MoU for a "Shaurya Academy".
  • The PAIMANA portal flash report covers 1,775 central sector infrastructure projects with a total revised cost of ₹37.11 lakh crore. Cumulative expenditure is ₹19.26 lakh crore (about 51.91%), with 735 mega projects (₹28.77 lakh crore) and 1,040 major projects (₹4.93 lakh crore). Transport and logistics account for 1,246 projects (₹19.81 lakh crore) and energy for 205 projects (₹10.66 lakh crore). Thirty-six projects were added in July 2026, and the next release is due on 25 September 2026.
  • The Prime Minister chaired the 53rd PRAGATI meeting on 25 August 2026, reviewing six infrastructure projects in the Railway, Road and Power sectors. The projects span 9 States with a cumulative investment above ₹30,000 crore. He also reviewed AgriStack (the Digital Agriculture Mission) and cyber-fraud response, and urged treating projects as an integrated whole.
  • The e-Shram portal reached five years on 26 August 2026 with 31.89 crore registered workers, each issued a unique 12-digit Universal Account Number (UAN). It integrates welfare, jobs, skilling, apprenticeship and pension services, and links schemes such as PM-SYM, PMSBY, PMJJBY, NSAP, ONORC and PMAY-G.
  • The Pradhan Mantri Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises (PMFME) Conclave, held on 24–25 August 2026 at the India Habitat Centre, drew 500+ stakeholders. Exhibitors from 25 States and Union Territories showcased products, a coffee-table book of 30 success stories and the quarterly magazine FoodNova were launched, and MoUs were signed with APEDA, ONDC and NABKISAN. The credit-linked capital subsidy under the scheme is 35% up to ₹10 lakh per unit.
  • NAMASTE-SUY supports mechanised sanitation enterprises, with a typical project cost of ₹10,70,400. Kapil (Ghaziabad) received a ₹6,77,800 loan and ₹3,92,600 capital subsidy and now earns ₹30,000–35,000 a month. Beneficiaries Sushil Kumar Sharma (Rajasthan) and Himanshu (Delhi) report similar earnings after buying tractor-mounted suction units.
  • Road-safety schemes were highlighted at the Regional Manthan Conference in Mumbai under the "Zero Fatality District" theme. PM Rahat provides cashless treatment up to ₹1.5 lakh for up to 7 days, and Rah-Veer pays ₹25,000 to a person who saves a life. The goal is a 50% cut in road fatalities by 2030, supported by Sadak Suraksha Mitra volunteers and data-driven hyper-local interventions.
  • The Integrated Fertilizer Management System (iFMS) enters a new phase from 30 August 2026. It covers over 14 crore Aadhaar-linked buyers, over 2.5 lakh retailers and about 7 crore MT of annual sales, administering roughly ₹2 lakh crore of subsidy. New integrations include Meri Fasal Mera Byora (Haryana), AgriStack and a Vehicle Location Tracking System, and digital bill processing began on 1 January 2026.
  • PMGSY-IV Batch-1 (2026-27) sanctioned 110 roads of 598.455 km at ₹1,503.53 crore in Jammu & Kashmir. It benefits 46,972 people, with the largest shares in Udhampur (44 roads, 235.040 km, ₹554.90 crore) and Kathua (24 roads, 128.925 km, ₹307.12 crore). Waste plastic is used in many of the roads.
  • The Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) 2.0, approved on 10 March 2026, and the Swachh Bharat Mission-Gramin (SBM-G) 2.0 are to be converged under the VB-G RAM G Act, 2025. The Act names water security as one of four thematic focus areas. Under DAY-NRLM, about 10 crore rural women are mobilised into Self-Help Groups, described as the world's largest community-institutional network.
  • A decade backgrounder shows India's universities rising from 760 (2014-15) to 1,289 (2023-24), a 69.6% increase. Colleges rose from 38,498 to 48,246, and enrolment from 3.42 crore to 4.50 crore (+31.5%). The Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) rose from 23.7% to 30%, female enrolment from 1.57 crore to 2.24 crore, and the Gender Parity Index from 0.92 to 1.08.

Economy & Trade

  • Kharif sowing stood at 1,104.45 lakh hectares on 21 August 2026, against 1,056.70 lakh hectares a year earlier (+47.75). Rice was lower at 405.10 lakh hectares (418.29 last year) and maize at 80.77 (89.51), while cotton jumped to 125.51 (108.45). Total pulses were 113.63 (112.14), total oilseeds 200.08 (188.37), soybean 128.71 and Shree Anna and coarse cereals 182.63. Sugarcane fell to 54.20 from 58.44.
  • The Service Producer Price Indices (base 2022-23) showed Air (Passenger) inflation of +31.94% year-on-year in Q1 2026-27, with the index at 126.4 against 106.9 in Q4 2025-26. Banking Service inflation was −3.35% (index 100.9), while Banking Service Contribution was +6.90% (134.8). Securities Transaction fell 1.32% to 89.9, Management of Pension Funds rose 5.20% to 113.3, and Insurance (+0.98%), Telecom (+0.72%) and Railway (+1.27%) were modestly positive.
  • RBI's study of 3,247 listed non-government non-financial companies shows sales growth accelerating to 19.4% year-on-year in Q1 2026-27, from 13.9% in Q4 2025-26. Manufacturing sales (1,827 companies) rose 21.4%, Information Technology (IT) 14.8% and non-IT services 19.7%. Manufacturing operating profit growth improved to 21.3% from 9.4%. Raw material costs rose 27.5%, but the raw-material-to-sales ratio eased to 58.1%.
  • The Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR) of manufacturing companies improved to 10.2 in Q1 2026-27 and that of non-IT services to 2.6. An ICR of 1 is the minimum for viability. Staff cost grew 12.4% in manufacturing, 7.6% in IT and 11.2% in non-IT services, and the data source is the Capitaline database.
  • The All-India House Price Index (HPI, base 2022-23, 18 cities) rose 1.1% over the previous quarter to 117.5 in Q1 2026-27. Annual growth was 3.6%, the same as in Q1 of the previous year. Chandigarh, Lucknow and Thiruvananthapuram led the quarterly rise, while Chandigarh, Jaipur, Kanpur, Lucknow and Thiruvananthapuram drove annual growth. The HPI uses transaction-level data from registration authorities.
  • India's steel output for April–July 2026 was 14.4 million tonnes (Mt) of crude steel (+1.9%), 8.4 Mt of hot metal (+6.3%) and 14.0 Mt of finished steel (+3.9%). In July 2026 TMT (10 mm) was ₹56,698 per tonne (+2.3% month-on-month) and HR coil ₹69,828 (+0.9%). Alloy steel output was 982.6 thousand tonnes (+22.7%) and stainless steel 352.6 thousand tonnes (−9.4%). The Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Act 2026 was notified on 17 August 2026.
  • The NA-allocated Talabira II & III coal mines recorded their highest annual production of 19.14 Mt in FY 2025-26, up 11.28% year-on-year. The peak day was 21 March 2026 (1,26,138.07 tonnes) and the peak month March 2026 (3.39 Mt). Highest annual dispatch was 17.69 Mt, and cumulative production up to 14 August 2026 is 72.23 Mt. The mines generated 3,277 jobs (1,277 direct) and spent ₹5.32 crore on Corporate Social Responsibility in FY 2025-26.
  • Retail onion is being sold at ₹35 per kg from buffer stock through NCCF, NAFED and Kendriya Bhandar outlets and mobile vans. Onion production was 307.37 lakh metric tonnes (LMT) in 2025-26 against 307.67 LMT in 2024-25. The Price Stabilisation Fund (PSF) target for Rabi onion procurement is 2.00 LMT, with 1.21 LMT procured since 15 May 2026, and the Central Warehousing Corporation (CWC) is sole storage agency. In 2025-26, 86 rail rakes moved about 88,000 MT to 16 cities (12,000 MT to 5 cities in 2024-25).
  • Sugar-sector data shared at UNCCD CoP17 gave 2026-27 sugarcane Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) of ₹365 per quintal (basic recovery 10.25%). Estimated sugarcane production for 2025-26 is 500 million metric tonnes (MMT), and ethanol diversion fell from 12% in 2022-23 to 9% in 2025-26. Global sugar prices rose from US$474 per tonne (30 June 2026) to US$552 per tonne (20 August 2026).
  • The first Sagar Samvad on Maritime India Vision 2030 and Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047 set a target of adding 100 vessels within five years. Five enablers were listed: fiscal reform, assured cargo support, competitive financing, regulatory streamlining and ease of doing business. Indian-flagged ships are 16%–20% costlier to run than foreign vessels, which the programme seeks to address.
  • Defence production reached ₹1.80 lakh crore in FY 2025-26, against ₹46,000 crore in 2014, with record exports of ₹39,000 crore. A ₹472 crore tank overhaul project in Jabalpur will overhaul 80 T-72 and T-90 tanks a year. The Army needs 230 tanks a year and the Heavy Vehicles Factory (HVF) Avadi has capacity for 150.
  • Khadi and Village Industries turnover rose from ₹31,000 crore in 2013-14 to ₹1.87 lakh crore in 2025-26, Union Home Minister Amit Shah said at the 72nd convocation of Gujarat Vidyapith on 27 August 2026. The Vidyapith was established on 18 October 1920, and 900 graduates received degrees, 11 with gold medals.

International Affairs & Organizations

  • ★ Prime Minister Narendra Modi's State Visit to Uzbekistan (29–30 August 2026) elevated the strategic partnership to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership, with a trade target of USD 5 billion by 2030. Talks with President Mirziyoyev noted bilateral trade above USD 1 billion and covered logistics, finance, digital, defence, health, minerals and space. The joint statement reaffirmed zero tolerance for terrorism and backed defence cooperation, including the Dustlik exercise. The visit is part of a 29 August–1 September trip that continues to Bishkek for the 26th Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) Summit and the 6th World Nomad Games.
  • ★ India–Uzbekistan outcomes include the Lal Bahadur Shastri Scholarship for Hindi (100 scholarships), an ICCR Sanskrit Chair at Tashkent State University of Oriental Studies and a USD 1 million grant for Aral Sea afforestation. NPCI International Payments Ltd (NIPL) and Uzbekistan's National Interbank Processing Centre (NIPC) signed a commercial agreement enabling UPI QR-scan payments (UZQR). The MoUs cover Ayurveda, geology and mineral resources, higher education, an economic and financial dialogue and a Cultural Exchange Programme 2026–30, along with a letter of intent on restoring Fayaz Tepa and Kara Tepa.
  • ★ Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal's Japan visit centred on the Japan–India Year of Shared Horizons 2026, marking 75 years of relations. He called for doubling the number of Japanese companies operating in India by the end of the decade, and addressed the Japan Business Federation and a 70-business round-table on capital goods, machinery and automotive. Meetings in Tokyo, Nagoya and Osaka covered over 30 companies and financial institutions, and Japan's private investment goal is JPY 10 trillion over the next decade. The 200+ strong business delegation was described as India's largest to Japan, and the Indian community there is nearly 60,000.
  • ★ The India–Japan Industry Roundtable on Semiconductors and AI (25 August 2026) involved 21 Japanese companies, including Tokyo Electron and NEC. Domestic semiconductor demand is projected at USD 150 billion by 2032, and the Government committed USD 10 billion under Semicon India 1.0 and USD 15 billion under 2.0. The six-pillar strategy covers chip design, machinery, fabrication, Assembly, Testing, Marking and Packaging (ATMP) or Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (OSAT), research and development, and talent.
  • ★ At the India–Japan Startup Roundtable in Tokyo (26 August 2026), India was presented as having 250,000+ startups, the world's third largest ecosystem. Two deep-tech funds of US$1 billion each were announced, and about 40 Japanese firms met a 222-member Indian delegation. The pillars were a Deep-Tech Capital Corridor, an Innovation Bridge, Manufacturing and Technology Integration, and Joint Pitching Platforms.
  • ★ India–Chile Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) talks in Santiago on 26 August 2026 aim to conclude the agreement by year-end. Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal highlighted healthcare, pharmaceuticals, energy, minerals, agriculture, machinery and engineering. Both sides were described as Global South partners. Chile is part of the Lithium Triangle with Argentina and Bolivia, which makes minerals central to the talks.
  • ★ The 4th India–Argentina Joint Trade Committee (JTC) met on 24 August 2026 at Palacio San Martín, Buenos Aires. Bilateral trade was USD 6.5 billion in 2025 (+17%), spanning lithium, energy, agriculture and pharmaceuticals. Khanij Bidesh India Limited (KABIL) reported completed Phase II drilling with prospects in Salta and Jujuy, and sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) issues were discussed for onions, milk, grapes, potatoes, bananas and pulses. Pharma regulation is moving towards Annex I from Annex II under an ANMAT–CDSCO MoU.
  • The 3rd India–Cambodia Joint Working Group on Trade and Investment (JWGTI) in Phnom Penh reported bilateral trade up 36% to USD 406.78 million in FY 2025-26, from USD 299 million. The April–June 2026 quarter was up 63.25% year-on-year. Cambodia is India's 8th largest ASEAN partner, with 0.31% of India–ASEAN trade, and a customs cooperation MoU and an investment treaty are pending.
  • Minister of State Jitin Prasada visited Morocco on 24–25 August 2026 for the 7th India–Morocco Joint Commission, co-chaired with Omar Hejira. An MoU on food safety was signed between ONSSA (Morocco) and FSSAI (India), along with a Cultural Exchange Programme 2026–2030. Bilateral trade was about USD 4 billion in 2025, mainly refined petroleum, pharmaceuticals and vehicles.
  • Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman is on an official visit to Canada (25–28 August) and the USA (28 August–2 September 2026). In Canada she held the inaugural Economic and Financial Dialogue with François-Philippe Champagne and investor roundtables referencing GIFT City-IFSC (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City, International Financial Services Centre). The visit also references the G20 Finance Ministers and Central Bank Governors (FMCBG) meeting on 31 August–1 September. In the USA she covers Chicago, Asheville and New York, including a NASDAQ bell-ringing and talks on fintech and venture capital.
  • Six envoys presented credentials to President Droupadi Murmu on 24 August 2026 at Rashtrapati Bhavan. They represented South Sudan, the United Arab Emirates, Nigeria, Moldova, Germany and Sweden, namely Sitona Abdalla Osman, Abdulla Saif Ali Slayem Al Nuaimi, Muhammad Saidu Dahiru, Inga Ionesii, Jasper Wieck and Petra Menander.
  • India–Mauritius and India–Seychelles cooperation featured capacity building and fisheries. Mauritian Minister Lutchmanah Raj Pentiah met Dr Jitendra Singh on scaling a 500-officer training programme under the National Centre for Good Governance (NCGG) and on iGOT-Karmayogi. India and Seychelles discussed a joint fisheries and aquaculture MoU, citing India's fish production of 19.7 million tonnes and USD 8 billion in seafood exports.
  • India–New Zealand held the 2nd Defence Strategic Dialogue on 24–25 August 2026 (Amitabh Prasad and Richard Schmidt) on maritime security and a 2030 Strategic Partnership Road-map. The Indian and Vietnam Coast Guards held their 7th High-Level Meeting in Chennai on 25 August, with Director General Paramesh Sivamani and Major General Luong Dinh Hung. The SAHYOG-HOPTAC sea exercise was scheduled for 27 August.
  • Union Home Minister Amit Shah issued an alert for a suspected flash flood or glacial lake outburst flood on the Trishuli river at Furke Khola in Nepal. Impact could propagate through the Gandak river in India, so the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and local administrations were put on high alert, with Central Water Commission advisories noting falling water levels in Nepal. Hotlines for Indian nationals are +977 985 131 6807 and +977 970 910 7500.
  • ★ The Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List at the 48th Session of the World Heritage Committee in Busan, South Korea, under criteria (iii) and (v). India now has 45 properties (37 cultural, 7 natural and 1 mixed) and ranks sixth globally and second in Asia-Pacific. The UNESCO list has 1,273 properties in total.
  • Indian defence cooperation and capability news included the commissioning of the Nistar-class Diving Support Vessel (DSV) NIPUN on 31 August 2026 at Naval Dockyard, Mumbai. It was built by Hindustan Shipyard Limited, Visakhapatnam, and delivered on 30 July 2026. Larsen & Toubro delivered the vessel Samarthak (over 75% indigenous content), and Defence Minister Rajnath Singh approved transferring all conventional DRDO missile technologies to Indian industry.

Sports / Environment / Science & Tech

  • National Sports Day is observed on 29 August, the birth anniversary of Major Dhyan Chand (121st in 2026). At the Khelo India Dialogue on 27 August, the Prime Minister said India participates in only half of the Olympic sports (13 sports in 2012) and announced a national talent hunt for children aged 5–15 with the 2036 Olympics in mind. He cited 2.4 crore youth on the MyBharat platform and the National Sports Governance Act, and Sports Minister Mansukh Mandaviya launched "Ek Ghanta Khel Ke Maidan Mein". A Paris 2024 Paralympics haul of 29 medals was also cited by Dr Jitendra Singh.
  • India's second Bonn Challenge progress report says 21.76 million hectares were restored during 2011–2020, generating 1.22 billion person-days of employment. The Bonn Challenge target for 2030 is 26 million hectares. It is a through-line for India's land-restoration commitments under the UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD).
  • At the 17th Conference of the Parties (CoP17) of the UNCCD in Ulaanbaatar, Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav urged treating drought as a predictable, preventable risk. India highlighted rangelands, including Himalayan pastures, Central Indian savannas, the Thar, Kachchh and Southern sholas. About 25,000 community-protected sacred groves cover roughly 0.6 million hectares, and India also pushed a blended green-finance framework (Sovereign Green Bonds, Green Credit Programme).
  • The National Biodiversity Authority disbursed ₹5.68 crore under Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) on 30 August 2026, taking cumulative ABS funds above ₹191 crore. The funds are linked to okra (139 varieties, 28 hybrids), watermelon (662 varieties) and cucumber (1,009 varieties), and flow to State Biodiversity Boards for conservation and community livelihoods.
  • The India–Russia partnership on snow leopard conservation, between the Interregional Association "Irbis" and the International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA), was signed in December 2025. A roundtable at the A.N. Severtsov Institute in Moscow on 26 August 2026 covered population estimation, radio telemetry and genetic analysis.
  • The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) inspected 28 industrial units at Loni (Ghaziabad) under "Operation Clean Air". Sixteen units were sealed, 21 lacked a valid Consent to Operate, 14 used unapproved fuels and 8 had furnaces without pollution control devices.
  • The Jawaharlal Nehru Centre for Advanced Scientific Research (JNCASR) developed ADxFluor, a smartphone-integrated Alzheimer's diagnostic platform. It uses the fluorescence-responsive probe TZ-48 to detect amyloid-β in blood and cerebrospinal fluid.
  • The South Asian MicroBiome ARray (SAMBAR) is the first baseline gut-microbiome dataset for South Asia. It covers 575 healthy adults from 10 communities in India and Sri Lanka, studied by the Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences with the University of Chicago.
  • CSIR-National Aerospace Laboratories (CSIR-NAL) presented indigenous micro and small gas turbine engines, the NJ-05, NJ-50 and NJ-100 (5, 50 and 100 kg thrust). The Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission launched seven nitrosamine reference standards: NDMA, NDEA, NEIPA, NDBA, NMPA, NDIPA and NMBA.
  • The Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026, notified on 27 August 2026, make Indian Standard Time the sole time reference for legal, administrative, commercial and digital use. They take effect 180 days after publication, with time disseminated via the NavIC satellite system under a "One Nation, One Time" approach.
  • The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology's GENESIS Entrepreneur-in-Residence (EIR) Cohort-3 offers up to ₹10 lakh per selected innovator. Applications run from 4 August to 3 September 2026 and are open to those in Tier-II and Tier-III cities with under two years of incorporation. IIT Madras Global's "Bharat Ascend" aims to scale 100 ideas over 365 days (targets include 10 investment-ready ventures).
  • Mission ShaktiSAT will teach satellite technology to 12,000 girls from 108 countries, with its second phase launched on 23 August 2026. It was highlighted in the 137th Mann Ki Baat, alongside the "Nasha-mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat" campaign.
