01

RBI & Monetary Policy

  • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) kept absorbing surplus liquidity through overnight Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions, all clearing at a 5.24 per cent cut-off. Under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF), it notified ₹2,00,000 crore on each of 28, 29 and 30 September 2026 and accepted ₹1,11,711 crore, ₹1,19,048 crore and ₹85,424 crore respectively. On 29 September it also held a second overnight VRRR (₹1,00,000 crore notified, 11:30 AM–12:00 Noon), accepting ₹33,770 crore at the same 5.24 per cent. A VRRR drains excess liquidity at a rate discovered in the auction, and the operational guidelines remain those of the press release dated 13 February 2020.
  • A 4-day VRRR auction was held on 1 October 2026, with reversal on 5 October 2026 (Monday). Of ₹2,00,000 crore notified, offers of ₹1,68,055 crore were received and fully accepted, with cut-off and weighted average rate both at 5.24 per cent. The RBI then announced an overnight VRRR for 5 October (₹2,00,000 crore, reversal 6 October). The Weekly Statistical Supplement shows net daily absorption of roughly ₹1.8–3.1 lakh crore during 21–27 September 2026, which is the liquidity backdrop to these operations.
  • An Open Market Operation (OMO) sale on 28 September 2026 also withdrew liquidity. The RBI notified an aggregate ₹25,000 crore (no security-wise amounts), received bids of ₹67,655 crore and accepted ₹25,000 crore across six Government Stocks (GS). The largest acceptance was ₹14,350 crore in 6.54% GS 2032 at a cut-off yield of 7.0810 per cent (partial allotment of 50.00 per cent at cut-off), followed by ₹6,450 crore in 7.26% GS 2032 at 7.1021 per cent. OMO sales are outright sales of government securities used to absorb liquidity.
  • The Government of India (GoI) auctioned four dated securities worth ₹33,000 crore on 1 October 2026, with settlement on 5 October. These were 6.20% GS 2029 (₹9,000 crore), 6.57% GS 2033 (₹12,000 crore), 7.63% GS 2056 (₹9,000 crore) and 7.50% GOI SGrB 2056 (₹3,000 crore, a Sovereign Green Bond). Cut-off yields were 6.7688, 7.1680, 7.6895 and 7.6539 per cent, and devolvement on Primary Dealers (PDs) was NIL in every case. Bids were submitted on e-Kuber, the RBI's Core Banking Solution, and GoI retained the option to take up to ₹2,000 crore extra per security.
  • The underwriting auction fixed the Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) commission for the same four securities. Cut-off rates were 1.97, 0.85, 0.64 and 0.87 paise per ₹100 respectively. The Minimum Underwriting Commitment (MUC) per PD was ₹215 crore, ₹286 crore, ₹215 crore and ₹72 crore, with the same amounts as minimum ACU bidding commitment. ACU bids were submitted on e-Kuber between 09:00 and 09:30 AM, under the underwriting scheme notified on 14 November 2007.
  • Treasury Bill (T-bill) auction results of 30 September 2026 showed an upward-sloping yield curve. Cut-off yields were 5.5199 per cent (91-day, price ₹98.6425), 5.9601 per cent (182-day, ₹97.1139) and 6.1798 per cent (364-day, ₹94.1949), against notified amounts of ₹9,000 crore, ₹8,000 crore and ₹7,000 crore. The next auction (7 October 2026, settlement 8 October) is ₹8,000 crore, ₹8,000 crore and ₹7,000 crore, a total of ₹23,000 crore. Retail non-competitive allocation is capped at 5 per cent of the notified amount and is available through the Retail Direct portal.
  • The coupon on Government of India Floating Rate Bond (FRB) 2028 was reset to 6.45 per cent per annum for 4 October 2026–3 April 2027. The coupon is the average Weighted Average Yield (WAY) of the last three 182-day T-bill auctions plus a fixed spread of 0.64 per cent. This is the routine half-yearly reset of an existing instrument.
  • State Government Securities (SGS/SDL) auctions raised ₹19,200 crore against ₹22,200 crore notified on 29 September 2026. Tamil Nadu accepted nothing in its 10-year security and the re-issue of 7.62% Tamil Nadu SGS 2041, which explains the shortfall. Cut-off yields were mostly in the 7.8–8.0 per cent range; Sikkim's 4-year paper cleared at 7.55 per cent. A further auction of ₹25,100 crore was announced for 6 October 2026 (results the same day, payment 7 October).
  • ★ The Benchmark Issuance Strategy (BIS) for State borrowings was extended again from Q3 of FY 2026-27 (October–December 2026). BIS began as a pilot in Q1 FY 2026-27 with nine States: Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Keralam, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Telangana and Uttar Pradesh. Nine more States and the Union Territory (UT) of Delhi joined in Q2, and Assam, Goa, Haryana, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura and the UT of Jammu and Kashmir join from Q3. Total State/UT market borrowing for October–December 2026 is expected to be ₹3,60,820 crore, subject to approval under Article 293(3) of the Constitution.
  • The RBI released the Report of the Advisory Committee on Ways and Means Advances (WMA) to State Governments on 29 September 2026. The committee was chaired by Shri I S N Prasad, former Additional Chief Secretary, Government of Karnataka. Its terms of reference included reviewing State-wise WMA limits and other financial accommodation facilities, and the RBI will examine its recommendations. WMA is the temporary overdraft-type facility the RBI gives to Centre and States.
  • Shri Sudhakar Malli was appointed Executive Director (ED) of the RBI with effect from 1 October 2026. He was earlier Chief General Manager-in-Charge of the Department of Supervision and will now look after the Department of Supervision (Supervisory Assessment). A career central banker with about three decades of experience, he holds a B.Tech in Mechanical Engineering and is a Certified Associate of the Indian Institute of Bankers (CAIIB).
  • The RBI's Citizen's Charter status for September 2026 showed 99.9 per cent of applications processed within timeline. Of 19,510 applications (3,011 pending at the start plus 17,200 received, minus 701 referred back), 16,844 were processed, of which 16,822 were within timeline. The number of services under the Charter is now 206, after one service was added under Financial Markets, eight were deleted under Foreign Exchange Management because powers were delegated to Authorised Dealers, and one Financial Markets timeline was modified. Applicants are directed to the PRAVAAH portal.
  • Foreign exchange reserves stood at US$747,557 million (₹71,64,287 crore) on 25 September 2026, down US$18,343 million over the week. The components were Foreign Currency Assets US$615,411 million, Gold US$108,701 million, Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) US$18,642 million and Reserve Position in the International Monetary Fund (IMF) US$4,804 million. Gold and currency valuation changes drive most weekly swings in the reserves.
02

Banking & Financial Sector

  • Lending rates eased marginally in the RBI's September 2026 data release. The Weighted Average Lending Rate (WALR) on outstanding rupee loans of Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs) fell to 8.96 per cent in August 2026 from 8.97 per cent in July. WALR on fresh rupee loans was 8.61 per cent (8.52 per cent in July), and the 1-year median Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate (MCLR) fell to 8.61 per cent in September from 8.70 per cent in August. The data cover SCBs excluding regional rural banks and small finance banks.
  • External-benchmark loans keep gaining share over MCLR-linked loans. The share of External Benchmark based Lending Rate (EBLR) linked loans in floating-rate rupee loans was 68.2 per cent at end-June 2026 (67.6 per cent at end-March 2026), while MCLR-linked loans fell to 29.6 per cent (30.2 per cent). This shows faster transmission of policy rate changes to borrowers.
  • Deposit rates also declined. The Weighted Average Domestic Term Deposit Rate (WADTDR) on outstanding rupee term deposits fell to 6.56 per cent in August 2026 from 6.58 per cent in July. On fresh deposits it fell more sharply, to 5.67 per cent from 5.85 per cent.
  • Bank credit growth accelerated sharply in the Sectoral Deployment of Bank Credit data for August 2026. Non-food credit grew 18.8 per cent year-on-year (y-o-y) against 10.2 per cent a year earlier. Agriculture and allied activities grew 17.2 per cent (7.6 per cent), industry 18.2 per cent (7.0 per cent), services 24.3 per cent (10.3 per cent) and personal loans 16.9 per cent (11.9 per cent). Services were supported by NBFCs (Non-Banking Financial Companies), trade, professional services and commercial real estate, while credit cards and gold-jewellery loans decelerated. The data cover 41 select SCBs (about 95 per cent of non-food credit).
  • Weekly data confirm strong balance-sheet expansion. As on 15 September 2026, aggregate deposits of SCBs were ₹2,76,23,066 crore (y-o-y growth 17.3 per cent), bank credit ₹2,23,29,258 crore (18.1 per cent) and broad money M3 ₹3,30,67,446 crore (16.6 per cent). The fortnight definition now follows the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 2025 (15th and last calendar day of a month, effective 15 December 2025).
  • The Scheduled Banks' Statement of Position as on 15 September 2026 lists the scheduled banking universe. It shows 120 Scheduled Commercial Banks, 26 Scheduled State Co-operative Banks and 55 Scheduled Primary (Urban) Co-operative Banks. Food credit outstanding of SCBs was ₹1,09,721 crore against ₹45,284.14 crore a year earlier.
  • ★ The RBI extended Section 35A directions on two Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs) to 4 January 2027. The Industrial Co-operative Bank Ltd., Guwahati, and Innovative Co-operative Urban Bank Limited, New Delhi, were each extended by three months from close of business on 4 October 2026 to close of business on 4 January 2027, subject to review. The directions are issued under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, and continue earlier six-monthly orders of July 2025. The RBI stresses that an extension does not imply satisfaction with the bank's financial position.
  • ★ The RBI simplified approvals for further acquisitions of major shareholding in banks by mutual funds, insurers and pension funds. Final Amendment Directions took immediate effect on 1 October 2026 for Commercial Banks, Small Finance Banks, Payments Banks and Local Area Banks. They followed the draft issued on 14 July 2026, with comments invited until 4 August 2026 and feedback incorporated.
  • Cross-border financial investments were highlighted in the India–UAE task force. Emirates NBD acquiring RBL (a US$3 billion investment) and the International Holding Company (IHC) investing US$1 billion in Sammaan Capital were cited as examples of deepening financial-sector ties. These sit alongside the 14th High-Level Joint Task Force discussion in Mumbai.
  • An IIT Madras study found that highly educated professionals fall for cyber-fraud mainly through psychological manipulation. The study covered IT, medical, banking and management professionals. It found that urgency tactics, fake investment offers and "digital arrest" threats worked more often than technical loopholes. It recommends targeted awareness instead of blanket warnings, better fraud-detection systems and inter-agency coordination.

Government Schemes & Budget

  • ★ The PM E-DRIVE Scheme has an outlay of ₹10,900 crore and operates until 31 March 2028. ₹780 crore is earmarked for testing agencies, and the Global Automotive Research Centre (GARC) has been allocated ₹221.13 crore. Upgrades cover EV and battery testing under AIS-156 and AIS-004 Part 3 Rev-1, power-train, electromagnetic compatibility (EMC), fatigue, safety and ADAS testing. This builds the testing infrastructure behind India's electric mobility push.
  • ★ PM-SETU, the scheme to transform Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs), completed one year on 4 October 2026. The total outlay is ₹60,000 crore (₹30,000 crore Centre, ₹20,000 crore State, ₹10,000 crore Industry), covering 1,000 Government ITIs as 200 Hubs and 800 Spokes. So far 850 ITIs (172 Hubs, 678 Spokes) are identified and 14 Skill India Programme (SIP) proposals worth ₹3,446 crore are approved. 32 new-age courses (AI, cyber-security, semiconductors, drones, green hydrogen and others) were added, and India now has 13,888 ITIs with 14.70 lakh enrolled.
  • ★ Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana–Gramin (PMAY-G) 2.0 was launched for 3 lakh houses in Andhra Pradesh on 4 October 2026. The allocation is ₹3,600 crore, raised from the original 76,000 houses. The launch at Burripalem, Guntur, was by Union Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan and Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu. A pilot of 177 houses built with 3D concrete-printing technology costs ₹14 crore and is expected in 6 months. A Saras Mela, the first outside Delhi, will be inaugurated by President Droupadi Murmu on 15 October 2026.
  • The Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) has assisted 8.12 lakh micro-entrepreneurs from FY 2014-15 to FY 2025-26. Margin money subsidy of ₹24,398.61 crore has supported 73.08 lakh jobs. Success stories include Skinkaanti Ayurveda (Himachal Pradesh; ₹25 lakh project, ₹30 crore turnover, 300 employees, 95 per cent women) and Ess Pee Quality Products (Punjab; ₹21 lakh project, ₹38 crore turnover, exports to 26 countries).
  • The Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC) launched a Gandhi Jayanti discount drive from 2 October to 16 November 2026. Discounts are up to 20 per cent on Khadi and 10 per cent on village-industry items. FY 2025-26 turnover was ₹1.87 lakh crore with employment for 2.04 crore people, and the FY 2026-27 revenue target is ₹2.51 lakh crore.
  • Agriculture ministry announcements at the Rabi Campaign 2026 conference carried concrete subsidy and growth figures. Fertiliser subsidies of ₹2 lakh crore were cited, with urea at ₹266 per bag and DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate) at ₹1,350 per bag. The "Khet Bachao Abhiyan" (22 October–30 November 2026) promotes balanced fertiliser use. Agriculture growth was cited at 4.5 per cent (3 per cent in 2015), with 100 per cent assured procurement of tur, urad and masoor at Minimum Support Price (MSP) and payment within 48 hours. Sanctions under PM-RKVY (Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana) included ₹290 crore for West Bengal and ₹198 crore for Haryana.
  • The PM-MKSSY (Pradhan Mantri Matsya Kisan Samridhi Sah-Yojana) offers aquaculture insurance incentives. Approved on 8 February 2024, it gives an incentive of up to 40 per cent of premium. Component-1B provides up to ₹25,000 per hectare (maximum ₹1 lakh for up to 4 ha), with an extra 10 per cent for SC/ST/women. Claims are to be settled within 30 days (shrimp) or 45 days (others), and 127 of 316 applications were approved under the OTI component (₹40.03 lakh disbursed).
  • A new livestock insurance portal was launched at rashtriyapashubima.dahd.gov.in. Union Minister of State Shri Rajiv Ranjan Singh (alias Lalan Singh) launched it. It handles policy issuance, premium management, claim processing and real-time monitoring, and more than 30 lakh animals were insured in the year.
  • The Pradhan Mantri Anusuchit Jaati Abhyuday Yojana (PM-AJAY) reported progress as of 8 September 2026. This Centrally-sponsored scheme (FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26) has served 47,243 Adarsh Grams, prepared 27,032 Village Development Plans, declared 18,093 villages A-Grams and completed 785 student hostels. Central assistance of ₹61.46 crore was released in FY 2026-27 for two hostels.
  • Panchayat finance and local planning got a major push on Gandhi Jayanti (2 October 2026). 2.42 lakh special Gram Sabhas were held, launching the People's Plan Campaign 2026-27 ("Sabki Yojana, Sabka Vikas") for FY 2027-28 plans. ₹4,35,236 crore is allocated to Rural Local Bodies for FY 2026-31 (about 84 per cent higher), and per-capita grant rises from ₹400 to ₹950.
  • Export-support schemes were extended and tweaked. RoDTEP (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products) was extended till 31 December 2026 with rates and caps unchanged (Notification 41/2026-27). Under the RELIEF logistics scheme (Notification 37/2026-27), Component II gives 95 per cent risk coverage for FCL, LCL and reefer containers for policies taken on or after 16 March 2026.
  • The Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) extended income-tax deadlines. For FY 2026-27, the return due date of 31 October 2026 moves to 21 November 2026 for persons covered under section 139(1) Explanation-2, and the audit report due date of 30 September 2026 moves to 21 October 2026.
  • Special Campaign 6.0 runs from 2 to 31 October 2026 and continues the earlier Special Campaign 5.0. It focuses on cleanliness, pendency reduction, record management and e-waste disposal, after preparatory work from 15 to 30 September. The Department of Personnel reported ₹5,102.91 crore generated from e-waste and scrap, 1,049.54 lakh sq ft of space freed, 180.48 lakh files weeded out and 24.20 lakh sites cleaned.
  • Labour-code outreach and employment-linked schemes continued. The 90-day "Shram Samvad" campaign (launched 28 September 2026) will cover 316 districts across 36 States/UTs. Dr. Mansukh Mandaviya said social security coverage rose from 19 per cent in 2015 to 68 per cent, and highlighted AI skills for one crore youth.
  • Delhi connectivity projects worth over ₹1,941 crore were inaugurated or foundation-laid by Shri Amit Shah. The largest is Barapullah Elevated Road Phase 3 (₹1,635 crore), alongside the Modi Mill–Savitri Cinema Flyover and Loni Underpass.

Economy & Trade

  • India's net international investment position (IIP) improved against a year ago but weakened over the quarter in June 2026. Net claims of non-residents on India rose US$16.5 billion in Q1:2026-27 to US$220.3 billion (Net IIP −220.3, against −203.8 in March 2026). The ratio of international assets to liabilities moderated to 84.6 per cent from 85.7 per cent, and reserve assets were 55.1 per cent of international financial assets. Portfolio equity liabilities fell US$14 billion, while direct investment (US$15.7 billion) and other investment (US$4.2 billion) rose. The share of debt liabilities in external liabilities reached 56.9 per cent.
  • India's external debt reached US$778.2 billion at end-June 2026, up US$15.4 billion from end-March. The external debt-to-GDP ratio moderated to 20.8 per cent (20.9 per cent). Long-term debt was US$624.7 billion, the short-term share rose to 19.7 per cent (19.6 per cent), and short-term debt (original maturity) was 23.0 per cent of forex reserves (21.6 per cent). The US dollar had a 54.8 per cent share, followed by the rupee (29.8 per cent), Yen (6.9 per cent), SDR (4.1 per cent) and Euro (3.5 per cent). Loans (34.3 per cent) were the largest instrument, and debt service was 5.6 per cent of current receipts.
  • Services trade stayed strong in August 2026. Service exports were US$35,471 million (13.7 per cent y-o-y growth) and imports US$18,934 million (21.4 per cent). The RBI also released India's Invisibles data for April–June 2026-27 in the IMF's BPM6 format.
  • ★ The 14th India–UAE High-Level Joint Task Force on Investments met in Mumbai on 28 September 2026. It was co-chaired by Shri Piyush Goyal and Sheikh Hamed bin Zayed. It reviewed 2025-26 bilateral trade of US$101.25 billion (US$76.2 billion non-oil) and Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) progress. Investments flagged were US$3 billion (Emirates NBD acquiring RBL), US$1 billion (IHC in Sammaan Capital) and US$11.5 billion for an integrated aluminium complex in Odisha.
  • The G20 Trade Ministers' Meeting in Milwaukee (30 September–1 October 2026) saw Shri Piyush Goyal visit the United States. He held bilateral meetings with his US counterpart focused on farmers, MSMEs (Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises) and startups. This is a G20 forum visit combined with bilateral trade engagement.
  • The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) released the Index of Services Production (ISP) for July 2026 on base year 2024-25. Growth over July 2025 was strongest in Administrative and Support Services (+20.9), Retail Trade (+18.5, index 140.0) and Real Estate (+14.4), while Accommodation and Food stood at 144.0 (+12.6). Only Repair Services (−5.0) and Air Transport (87.8, −8.4) contracted.
  • Telecom subscription data for end-August 2026 show 1,101.21 million broadband subscribers (up 0.61 per cent). Jio led with 538.03 million, followed by Airtel (386.39 million), Vodafone Idea (130.88 million), BSNL (27.82 million) and Atria (2.48 million). Wireline subscribers were 48.45 million (up 0.93 per cent), with 15.83 million mobile number portability requests and 1,207.59 million active wireless subscribers (peak VLR).
  • The 5th Kautilya Economic Conclave (3 October 2026) highlighted financial inclusion figures. Vice-President C.P. Radhakrishnan cited 590 million Jan Dhan accounts (August 2026), 16.5 million GST registrations (May 2026, up from 6.65 million in 2017), 9.2 million Self-Help Groups and 34.6 million Lakhpati Didis. The theme was "Resilience in an Age of Flux."
  • Agricultural exports were promoted through the Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority (APEDA). A 1 MT consignment of Bihar makhana went to Greece (Mundra to Piraeus port), and Haryana frozen food (24 MT, valued at CAD 35,140) went to Canada. Bihar contributes 40 per cent of national makhana production.
  • The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) intensified action on smuggling. It seized 6.61 kg of foreign-origin gold (about ₹10.36 crore) in the courier crackdown of 23–24 September, and 8.564 kg of 24-carat gold (about ₹12.64 crore) plus 1,168.97 carats of diamonds at Mumbai airport on 30 September. Over nine months, 318 kg of gold worth ₹475 crore had moved via courier networks from West Bengal and Assam.

Sports / Environment / Science & Tech

  • India won a string of athletics and shooting medals at the Asian Games 2026 on 28–29 September. On 28 September the medals were Vithya Ramraj (bronze, women's 400 m hurdles), Gulveer Singh (silver, men's 1500 m, personal best), Parul Chaudhary (bronze, women's 5000 m), Yashvir Singh (silver, javelin, personal best), Rohit Yadav (bronze, javelin), Murali Sreeshankar (bronze, long jump), Pinkcy Balhara (bronze, women's 78 kg Kurash) and Esha Singh (silver, women's 25 m pistol). On 29 September came the men's 4×400 m relay bronze (Dharmveer Chaudhary, Manu TS, Jay Kumar, Vishal TK), Pooja (bronze, women's high jump), Gulveer Singh (bronze, men's 5000 m), the women's trap team silver (Manisha Keer, Neeru, Aashima Ahlawat) and Neeru Dhanda's women's trap gold.
  • India's boxers, wrestlers and hockey teams delivered gold on 2–3 October 2026. Lovlina Borgohain (women's 75 kg), Ankush (men's 80 kg) and Parveen (women's 65 kg) won boxing gold, and Sujeet won men's 65 kg freestyle wrestling gold on debut. The women's hockey team won gold (first in over four decades), and the men's team retained its title, so both teams won gold in the same Games for the first time. India also won its first-ever men's quadrant Sepaktakraw bronze and a women's dinghy sailing bronze (Nethra Kumanan).
  • More firsts came in wrestling, golf and archery. Aman Sehrawat won men's 57 kg freestyle gold (India's first Asian Games gold in this event), and Deepak Punia took men's 97 kg bronze. Pranavi Sharath Urs won India's first-ever women's individual golf gold, and Kumkum Anil Mohod became the first Indian woman to win recurve archery gold. The women's golf team (Aditi Ashok, Pranavi Sharath Urs, Diksha Dagar) and Antim Panghal (53 kg wrestling) won silver. Indian players were also congratulated for their performance at the 46th FIDE Chess Olympiad.
  • ★ Great Indian Bustard (GIB) re-wilding advanced at Desert National Park, Jaisalmer. Two captive-bred male birds were released at Sam on 4 October 2026 (re-wilding was initiated on 3 October by Shri Bhupender Yadav and Chief Minister Shri Bhajan Lal Sharma). The stock is 97 birds (40 wild-laid eggs, 57 captive-bred): 11 are earmarked for release and 86 kept for breeding. Captive breeding yielded 24 chicks in 2025 and 29 in 2026, while the jump-start method (captive eggs in wild nests) produced 6 chicks in Rajasthan and 2 in Gujarat. About 200 sq km of grassland is fenced, and the Ramdevra rewilding aviary was sanctioned at ₹9.25 crore.
  • Tigress T138 from Valmiki Tiger Reserve was released into Buxa Tiger Reserve (West Bengal) on 2 October 2026. It is the first phase of a phased Tiger Reintroduction Programme under the Tiger Augmentation and Recovery Project (Government of West Bengal, National Tiger Conservation Authority, Wildlife Institute of India). Buxa has 380 ha of restored grassland, 800 chital released and 2,055 ha of connectivity to Bhutan and Assam.
  • COFO 28 highlighted India's forest and restoration numbers. Beyond the rankings, India cited the Aravalli Green Wall (6.45 million ha of degraded land), the MISHTI mangrove restoration initiative and over 3.5 billion saplings planted under "Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam."
  • Delhi air-quality enforcement continued under the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM). In the reporting period 8–24 September 2026 there were 197 inspections and 85 violations, with 4 construction-and-demolition projects and 1 industrial unit closed and 28 DG sets sealed. Cumulatively there are 1,831 closure directions and 1,488 resumption orders. Separately, Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav and Chief Minister Rekha Gupta reviewed the action plan (37 hotspots, "No PUCC, No Fuel" at 500 ANPR-enabled stations), and a 3 October drive by four flying squads found 17 violations on Ring Road.
  • ★ India's National Supercomputing Mission (NSM, launched April 2015, outlay about ₹4,500 crore) now has 40 supercomputers with 68 PF capacity. 13 systems exceed 1 PF, and the target is 50 systems with 123 PF. PARAM Rudra servers are deployed (6,000, with 1,500 more under manufacture), and applications include genomics, flood prediction in the Mahanadi basin and seismic imaging.
  • Health science and AI news included robotics, genomics and BHASHINI. Dr. Jitendra Singh said the Genome India Programme has sequenced 10,000 individuals, with a goal of newborn genomic sequencing for 1.4 billion people. BHASHINI (CEO Shri Amitabh Nag) reported over 9 billion AI inferences across 36 Indian text and 23 voice languages. A 24-week trial of 1,200 adults found ashwagandha safe after COVISHIELD vaccination (published in Frontiers in Medicine, 20 August 2026).
  • Jawahar Dweep Marine Oil Terminal (Mumbai Port Authority) was declared India's first plastic-free oil terminal on 2 October 2026. The announcement was made during Swachhata Abhiyan 2026 with Union Minister Sarbananda Sonowal. Separately, the Coast Guard's ICGS Vishwast handled the distress of the Belize-flagged tanker MT Seagull 9 (9,000 t palm oil, 23 crew) off Paradip, and the palm-oil sheen was cleared by 28 September.
