01

RBI & Monetary Policy

  • RBI's Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions all cleared at a cut-off and weighted average rate of 5.24% between 07 and 11 September 2026. ★ VRRR is a Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) tool the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) uses to absorb surplus liquidity from banks. Operational guidelines remain those of Press Release 2019-2020/1947 dated February 13, 2020. The identical 5.24% rate across 1-day, 4-day, 26-day and 30-day tenors indicates that offers stayed below the notified amounts throughout the week.
  • Seven VRRR auctions absorbed funds, and in each one every offer received was accepted. On Monday 07 Sep, the 30-day auction (notified ₹7,00,000 crore) took ₹2,59,276 crore. The second, overnight auction (notified ₹5,00,000 crore, window 11:00–11:30 AM, reversal 08 Sep) took ₹3,53,390 crore. On 08 Sep the 1-day auction took ₹4,17,609 crore (window 9:30–10:00 AM), and on 09 Sep ₹4,61,586 crore. On 10 Sep the notified amount was raised to ₹6,00,000 crore and ₹3,96,694 crore was accepted. On Friday 11 Sep the 26-day auction took ₹60,449 crore (reversal Wednesday 07 October 2026), and a second 4-day auction (11:00–11:30 AM) took ₹3,44,864 crore (reversal Tuesday 15 Sep).
  • RBI announced an overnight VRRR for Tuesday 15 September 2026 and set rules for premature reversal of the 26-day VRRR. The overnight auction is notified at ₹5,00,000 crore, with a 09:30–10:00 AM window and reversal on 16 Sep. For the 26-day VRRR, premature reversal requests must go through the E-Kuber portal between 09:00 and 17:00 hrs on Mumbai working days. Partial reversal of a bid is allowed, and the request must reach the Financial Markets Operations Department by e-mail as well. It must be placed at least two working days before the original reversal date, with settlement at start of day on the next working day.
  • Net liquidity absorption stayed heavy but fell sharply by 05–06 September 2026. ★ Per the Weekly Statistical Supplement (WSS), net absorption rose from −₹6,71,690 crore (Aug 31) to −₹8,30,561 crore (Sep 04), then eased to −₹2,64,000 crore (Sep 05) and −₹2,45,922 crore (Sep 06). On Aug 31 the components were VRRR ₹3,84,465 crore, Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) ₹551 crore and Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) ₹2,87,776 crore. On Sep 04 they were VRRR ₹6,02,394 crore, MSF ₹123 crore and SDF ₹2,28,290 crore. The formula used is Net Injection (+) / Absorption (−) = (1+3+5+7+9−2−4−6−8).
  • India's foreign exchange reserves stood at ₹7,424,473 crore (US$785,706 million) on September 04, 2026. ★ The weekly rise was ₹357,815 crore (US$44,903 mn), the rise over end-March 2026 was US$94,599 mn, and the year-on-year rise was US$87,438 mn. Foreign Currency Assets were ₹6,124,821 crore (US$648,168 mn), Gold ₹1,075,494 crore (US$113,816 mn), Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) ₹177,703 crore (US$18,806 mn) and the Reserve Position in the International Monetary Fund (IMF) ₹46,455 crore (US$4,916 mn). Foreign Currency Assets exclude SDR holdings, investment in bonds issued by IIFC (UK), amounts lent under SAARC and ACU currency swap arrangements, and RBI's contribution to funding of Nexus Global Payments.
  • Scheduled commercial banks' (SCBs') deposits grew 17.8% and bank credit 19.1% year-on-year as on 31 August 2026. ★ Aggregate deposits were ₹27,871,530 crore (demand ₹3,448,742 crore, time ₹24,422,788 crore), and growth so far in FY2026-27 is 6.3%. Bank credit was ₹22,387,567 crore (food credit ₹112,042 crore, non-food ₹22,275,525 crore), with FY2026-27 growth so far at 4.8%. Credit growing faster than deposits is a key liquidity-and-funding theme for exam analysis.
  • Money Stock (M3) rose to ₹33,221,648 crore on 31 August 2026, up 16.7% year-on-year. M3 was ₹31,453,126 crore on 31 March 2026. Fortnight growth was 2.9% (reference fortnight Aug 15, 2026) and FY2026-27 growth so far is 5.6%. Time deposits with banks were ₹25,301,766 crore, currency with public ₹4,194,110 crore and bank credit to the commercial sector ₹23,240,519 crore (up 18.6%).
  • Under the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025, a "fortnight" now means the 15th and last calendar day of a month. This replaced the earlier alternate-Friday definition and applies with effect from December 15, 2025. It changes how reporting-fortnight data in the WSS are dated, which is why the M3 reference date is Aug 15, 2026.
  • RBI's loans and advances to State Governments were ₹27,940 crore on Sep 04, 2026, against ₹3,116 crore on Aug 28, 2026. The year-ago figure (Sep 05, 2025) was ₹44,070 crore, a week variation of 24,824 and a year variation of −16,130. Loans and advances to the Central Government were nil.
  • RBI auctioned ₹32,000 crore of dated government securities (G-Secs) on 11 September 2026, with nil devolvement on Primary Dealers. ★ The auction covered 6.20% GS 2029 (₹11,000 crore, maturity Aug 17, 2029), 6.57% GS 2033 (₹11,000 crore, Aug 17, 2033) and a New GS 2056 (₹10,000 crore, Sep 15, 2056), with settlement on Sep 15. Cut-off yields were 6.4031%, 6.8703% and 7.6300% (cut-off price ₹100.00 for the new security). The 6.20% GS 2029 was only partly accepted at ₹4,505.529 crore, while the other two were fully accepted at ₹11,000 crore and ₹10,000 crore.
  • Underwriting details of the 11 September auction highlight the Minimum Underwriting Commitment (MUC) system. Each Primary Dealer's MUC was ₹262 crore for the two older securities and ₹239 crore for the New GS 2056. Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) cut-off commissions were 2.00, 1.80 and 1.29 paise per ₹100, and ACU bids were taken on e-Kuber between 09:00 and 09:30 AM. The framework follows the "Revised Scheme of Underwriting Commitment and Liquidity Support" (circular RBI/2007-08/186 dated November 14, 2007).
  • A ₹28,000 crore re-issue auction is set for 18 September 2026, and general auction rules were restated. The securities are 7.06% GS 2041 (₹17,000 crore, maturing Jul 27, 2041) and 7.43% GS 2076 (₹11,000 crore, maturing Jan 19, 2076), with settlement on Monday 21 Sep. Auctions use the multiple price method and are yield-based for a new security and price-based for re-issues. The non-competitive segment is up to 5% of the notified amount, and the minimum bid is ₹10,000 in multiples of ₹10,000. Retail investors can bid via the Retail Direct portal, and non-resident investment is governed by the Fully Accessible Route (FAR).
  • RBI announced an Open Market Operations (OMO) sale of G-Secs worth ₹1,00,000 crore in three tranches, plus ₹24,000 crore of Treasury Bills. The OMO tranches are ₹50,000 crore on Sep 17, ₹25,000 crore on Sep 21 and ₹25,000 crore on Sep 28, 2026, using a multi-security multiple-price auction. The Sep 17 list is 7.59% GS 2029, 6.79% GS 2029, 7.61% GS 2030, 5.77% GS 2030, 6.68% GS 2031 and 8.28% GS 2032, with bids on E-Kuber from 9:30–10:30 AM. The 16 Sep Treasury Bill auction covers 91-day ₹9,000 crore, 182-day ₹8,000 crore and 364-day ₹7,000 crore, settling on Sep 17.
02

Banking & Financial Sector

  • RBI issued the Draft RBI (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026 on money mules and temporary debit holds. ★ Released on Sep 11, 2026 (Press Release 2026-2027/1109), it amends the "Operations of Bank Accounts and Money Mules" instructions in the KYC Directions, 2025. It follows a Supreme Court order dated August 4, 2026 directing RBI to adopt and circulate a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for temporary debit holds on amounts or accounts linked to money-mule activity and cyber-enabled fraud. One consolidated draft covers commercial banks (including Small Finance, Payments, Regional Rural and Local Area Banks) and Urban Cooperative Banks (UCBs), and comments are due by October 2, 2026 via the "Connect 2 Regulate" section.
  • RBI recognised the Unified Fintech Forum (UFF) as a Self-Regulatory Organisation in the FinTech Sector (SRO-FT) on Sep 10, 2026. The first SRO-FT was the FinTech Association for Consumer Empowerment (FACE), recognised by a Press Release dated August 28, 2024. The UFF recognition therefore continues RBI's framework of industry-led self-regulation for fintechs.
  • RBI penalised Asset Care & Reconstruction Enterprise Limited ₹27.30 lakh under the SARFAESI Act. ★ The order dated September 08, 2026 (announced Sep 11) used Section 12 read with Section 30A(1) of the Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002. The default was non-compliance with directions on "Income Recognition", specifically the rules on charging management fees. The inspection was based on the financial position as on March 31, 2025, and this is a regulatory-compliance action that does not rule on the validity of any customer transaction.
  • RBI penalised Jilla Sahakari Bank Ltd., Azamgarh, Uttar Pradesh ₹1.15 lakh. ★ The order dated September 09, 2026 cites non-compliance with directions on "Membership of Credit Information Companies (CICs) by Co-operative Banks" and on KYC. The bank failed to report borrowers' credit information to all CICs and did not upload customer KYC records to the Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR) on time. Powers used were Section 25(1)(iii) read with Section 23(4) of the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005 and Section 47A(1)(c) read with Sections 46(4)(i) and 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, with the statutory inspection conducted by NABARD.
  • RBI extended Section 35A directions on five co-operative banks for three months each, subject to review. ★ The directions are under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949. Nasik Jilha Mahila Vikas Sahakari Bank Ltd. now runs to Dec 9, 2026 (from Sep 9, 2026). Loknete R.D. (Appa) Kshirsagar Sahakari Bank, Niphad runs to Dec 16, 2026, The Gujarat Rajya Karmachari Co-op. Bank, Ahmedabad to Dec 11, 2026, and Kanaka Pattana Sahakara Bank Niyamita, Davangere to Dec 12, 2026. The U.P. Civil Secretariat Primary Co-operative Bank, Lucknow runs to Dec 11, 2026 and The Amanath Co-operative Bank Ltd., Bangalore to Dec 12, 2026. RBI states that an extension does not imply it is satisfied with the bank's financial position.
  • NBFC credit grew 14.9% year-on-year in July 2026, against 10.6% a year earlier. ★ The RBI release (Press Release 2026-2027/1058, Sep 07, 2026) covers Upper and Middle Layer Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) and Housing Finance Companies (HFCs), about 87 per cent of total credit based on the Report on Trend and Progress of Banking in India 2024-25. Data are provisional and relate to the month ended July 31, 2026. This is the recurring NBFC/HFC sectoral deployment series that questions often draw on.
  • Within NBFC credit, agriculture (18.0%) and retail loans (21.4%) accelerated while industry (7.4%) moderated. ★ Agriculture and allied activities grew 18.0% against 5.4% a year ago, and retail loans 21.4% against 13.7%. Industry slowed from 9.3% because of subdued infrastructure growth, and services slowed to 15.2% from 24.5%. Housing and loans against gold jewellery accelerated, vehicle loans grew steadily, commercial real estate expanded buoyantly, and trade and transport operators decelerated.
  • The Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 2026 replaces the 1891 Act and comes into force on 1 October 2026. It received assent on 13 Aug 2026 and was notified in the Gazette on 10 Sep 2026. It gives technology-neutral recognition to physical, electronic, virtual and cloud-based records, simplifies certification (manual, digital or electronic signatures), clarifies the "special cause" requirement for summoning bank officials, and allows extension to specified financial entities.
  • The Finance Minister agreed to keep the Performance Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme dated 19.11.2024 for FY 2025-26 in abeyance. The decision came after a delegation of bank employees (Bharatiya Mazdoor Sangh) raised concerns about public sector bank (PSB) staff incentives. The issue will be discussed in the ongoing Bipartite Settlement / Joint Note process.
  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated Global FinTech Fest 2026 in Mumbai (8–11 September 2026). The theme was "Potential to Impact: Agentic AI, Tokenisation, Quantum-Trusted, Connected, Global Systems for Inclusive Finance". It signals a policy focus on agentic artificial intelligence, tokenisation and quantum-secure systems for financial inclusion.

Government Schemes & Budget

  • The 9th meeting of the Central Sanctioning and Monitoring Committee (CSMC) under PMAY-U 2.0 approved housing sanctions of 18.77 lakh houses. ★ Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana-Urban 2.0 continues the urban housing mission, and the meeting was chaired by Satendra Singh. Beneficiaries included women 17.19 lakh, senior citizens 1.80 lakh, SC 3.25 lakh, ST 1.02 lakh and OBC 8.22 lakh. Additional approvals of 29,000 houses under Beneficiary Led Construction (BLC) and Affordable Housing in Partnership (AHP) covered Assam, Bihar, Gujarat, Telangana, Tripura and Uttar Pradesh.
  • PM Modi dedicated three sections of the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor (326 km, ₹20,700 crore) at Vadodara on 08 September 2026. ★ The event covered projects worth more than ₹35,000 crore, with sections including New Sanand, New Makarpura, New Umbergaon–New Saphale and New Saphale–New JNPT. He also laid foundation stones for three railway projects (329 km, over ₹9,700 crore) and three National Highway projects (about ₹1,780 crore), plus a 110 MW floating solar project at Kadana Dam. The railway works include Vadodara–Ratlam third and fourth lines, Miyagam Karjan–Choranda–Malsar gauge conversion and Vishwamitri–Dabhoi doubling.
  • The National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) covers 130 cities, and 108 improved their PM10 levels in FY 2025-26 against FY 2017-18. ★ Of these, 72 cities cut PM10 by more than 20%, 26 cities by more than 40%, and 14 cities met the National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS). Performance-linked gap funding is quoted as ₹16,425 crore in one release and ₹16,423.55 crore (FY 2019-20 to FY 2025-26, 70.5% utilised, ₹11,575.54 crore) in another. The programme runs 1,646 Air Quality Index (AQI) stations across 603 cities in 28 States and 7 Union Territories (UTs).
  • The Defence Acquisition Council (DAC) granted Acceptance of Necessity (AoN) for proposals worth ₹1,10,000 crore. ★ The Army list includes CBRN Recce Vehicles, HMV, MML, ALH, Trawl Tanks and the Sarvatra Bridge System. The Navy list covers Arudhra Radars and Marine Gas Turbines, and the Air Force list the Ground-Based Multi-Purpose Jammer and the Defence Forces Secure Access Card. Around 98% of procurement is from Indian industry, which fits the self-reliance push.
  • Defence Minister Rajnath Singh cited defence production rising from ₹4,000 crore before 2014 to ₹1.80 lakh crore in 2026. Exports grew 56-fold to ₹38,000 crore, with a target of ₹50,000 crore by 2029. He said defence PSUs are all loss-free, 16,000 MSMEs are involved, and ₹70,000 crore of investment proposals exist (₹10,000 crore already invested).
  • The 9th Rashtriya Poshan Maah 2026 was launched at Varanasi on 9 September 2026 under the theme "Hamari Anganwadi Hamari Jimmedari". ★ It is a 10-month campaign with five themes: Dietary Diversity & Adequate Protein, Fresh Hot-Cooked Meals, Community Ownership & Accountability, Nutrition/Early Stimulation & Education, and 10 Years of Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY). Revised Supplementary Nutrition Programme (SNP) Guidelines (May 2026) define hot-cooked meals and mandate menu boards, and Anganwadi Management Committee guidelines were issued 25 Jun 2026. Home-visit counselling via Navchetana tracks 34 developmental milestones, and PMMVY celebrates 10 years with a 100% saturation drive.
  • The incentive scheme for domestic PNG connections was launched on 18 August 2026 and took effect on 1 September 2026. Piped Natural Gas (PNG) had 1.74 crore domestic connections on 18 Aug 2026. The scheme runs six months in two tranches and gives 200 SCM of Administered Price Mechanism (APM) gas, which replaces costlier LNG, per incremental billed connection above a threshold. It aims to cut the payback period from about 10 years to about 3 years, backed by an Accelerated Approval Framework, uniform right-of-way charges, VAT cut to 5% in several states and a unified PNG registration portal.
  • Pradhan Mantri Kisan Maandhan Yojana (PM-KMY) offers small and marginal farmers an assured pension of ₹3,000 per month after age 60. The scheme was launched on 12 Sep 2019, and the spouse receives 50% (₹1,500 per month) on the subscriber's death. As on 06 Feb 2026 there were 24,96,252 enrollees, led by Haryana (about 5.75 lakh) and Bihar (about 3.46 lakh), and ₹540.66 crore was utilised. Monthly contributions are ₹55–₹200 by age, with the Government matching 1:1, and enrolment is via Common Service Centres.
  • Under Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY), India adopted 9,467 Recirculatory Aquaculture Systems and 4,573 Bio-floc units. Investment under PMMSY on this technology was ₹4,120 crore. Fish production rose from 95.79 lakh tonnes (2013-14) to 198 lakh tonnes (2024-25), a 147% increase, and shrimp exports from ₹30,213 crore to ₹73,890 crore (2025-26). The Honey Mission (2017-26) distributed 2,46,000 bee boxes and colonies and produced 5,500 metric tonnes of honey in 2025-26.
  • NSKFDC has sanctioned ₹3,365.93 crore in concessional loans to 6,10,902 beneficiaries up to 10 September 2026. The National Safai Karamcharis Finance & Development Corporation was established on 24 Jan 1997 and became operational in Oct 1997, under the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment. Loans go up to ₹30 lakh per individual and ₹75 lakh per group at 6%–9%, with 3–10 years' repayment and rebates of 1% for women and 0.5% for timely instalments.
  • A cluster of social-welfare releases quantified sanitation, senior-citizen and de-addiction support. Of sewer and septic tank workers, 90,942 were profiled (89,248 validated), 87,037 received PPE kits and 76,247 health insurance. Senior-citizen schemes benefit over 2.88 lakh people through 142 new homes, and 23.93 lakh assistive devices were distributed. Individual success stories under NAMASTE-SUY, the National Fellowship for Scheduled Caste Students (NFSC) and NAPDDR (de-addiction) illustrate the schemes' impact.
  • Other rural and agriculture initiatives were also announced. Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan set a target to upgrade 300 Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs) by March 2027. The 7–8 Sep 2026 workshop at NASC, Pusa, closed documentation of more than 2,000 DDU-GKY 1.0 projects. At Gender Samvaad, 10 crore women are in the SHG movement and 3.46 crore Lakhpati Didis earn over ₹1 lakh a year.
  • Vice-President C. P. Radhakrishnan laid foundation stones for projects worth ₹1,010.95 crore in Meghalaya on 07 September 2026. The New Shillong Water Supply Scheme Phase II, Part A costs ₹850.58 crore (Urban Challenge Fund), and the Maternity & Children's Hospital, Nongstoin (₹31.05 crore, National Health Mission) was inaugurated. He also noted 46,000 km of rural roads built under PMGSY in the North-East with ₹47,000 crore spent over the last decade. At Gauhati University he laid foundation stones for ₹110 crore of works.

Economy & Trade

  • India's Q1 GDP growth was cited at 7.8% by Union Home Minister Amit Shah. ★ He cited the figure at the 52nd India Gems & Jewellery Awards, Mumbai, on 13 Sep 2026, and it is the latest growth number in this week's material. The sector is worth ₹2.44 lakh crore, is 6.3% of merchandise exports and employs about 75 lakh people. Surat handles more than 90% of the world's cut-and-polished diamonds, and the India Jewellery Park is planned with about ₹50,000 crore of investment and about 1 lakh jobs.
  • Union Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal said India has 9 FTAs covering about $60 trn of GDP, rising to about $75 trn. ★ He spoke on health and medtech, pushing India as a medical value tourism destination with a target of 10 million patients a year and a ₹1 lakh crore investment drive for R&D and innovation. His five-point caregivers plan includes training and certifying caregivers, the Heal-in-India gateway for hospitals, and only NABH-enabled hospitals at exhibitions. It also covers Tier-2/Tier-3 expansion with state participation.
  • The Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) launched an Open API for Certificates of Origin (Trade Notice No. 25/2026-27, 07 Sep 2026). ★ Exporters can apply for preferential and non-preferential CoO directly from their ERP systems through three APIs: Authentication Token, CoO File and Certificate Verification. Security uses SHA-256 RSA signatures, PBKDF2 hashing, IP whitelisting and 60-minute token validity. Covered pacts include India–Japan CEPA, India–Korea CEPA, SAFTA, SAPTA, ASEAN–India FTA, India–Singapore CECA, India–Malaysia CECA, India–Chile PTA and India–Mercosur PTA.
  • At the BRICS Business Forum, Goyal said BRICS accounts for about 25% of global trade. He cited UPI at 250 billion transactions a year, accepted in 11 countries, and said over 45% of 250,000 recognised startups have at least one woman partner or director. Digital payment linkages, market access and supply-chain diversification were the highlighted priorities.
  • Indian Railways' freight loading rose 5.4% year-on-year to 137.9 million tonnes in August 2026 (against 130.9 million tonnes). Freight revenue rose 6.27%. Commodity growth was led by iron ore (12.1%), clinker (10.4%), domestic container (9.2%), coal (6%), finished steel (4.9%), mineral oil (3.1%) and fertilizers (1.6%). Five new Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals (Shivlakha, Nalli, Shaktinagar, Deoragram and Brajrajnagar) took the total to 149, and 14 Amrit Bharat services were regularised, taking the total to 86.
  • Railway passenger traffic was 67.27 crore in August 2026, against 64.43 crore a year earlier. Suburban passengers grew 9.02% to 38.01 crore from 34.89 crore. Railways ran 144 Rakshabandhan special trains (25–31 Aug 2026) against 48 in 8–14 Aug 2025.
  • Domestic coal production exceeded 1 billion tonnes for a second consecutive year, with 1,039 MT in FY 2025-26. Captive mines produced 184 MT, up 10.10% from 167.44 MT, and commercial mines about 26 MT. Nine new captive and commercial mines (20.67 MT peak capacity) are slated for FY 2026-27, with captive output forecast above 190 MT. SECL alone produced 176.28 MT, and Coal India restored output after the monsoon dip from 1.36 MT/day to 1.83 MT/day by 6 Sep (a 35% increase).
  • The revised Tara coal block was auctioned in the 15th round of commercial coal mine auctions at a 37.50% revenue share. It was excluded from the 7th tranche in 2023 because of overlap with the Lemru Elephant Corridor, until a Chhattisgarh Government letter dated 11 Dec 2025 said it lay outside the corridor. Nine bids were received, annual revenue to the state exceeds ₹1,200 crore and over 8,000 jobs are expected.
  • Jute production in FY 2025-26 was 76 lakh bales of raw jute and 12.80 lakh MT of jute goods. Exports were 22,700 MT of raw jute (₹225.11 crore) and 1,71,300 MT of jute goods (₹3,155.27 crore), while domestic consumption was 12.05 lakh MT (94% of production). The MSP for FY 2026-27 is ₹5,925 per quintal (about 147% above FY 2014-15). Jute Corporation of India procures through about 110 centres, and the National Jute Development Programme was allotted ₹485.58 crore (FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26).
  • India's bioeconomy reached USD 195.3 billion in 2025, up 18% year-on-year, and India supplies over 60% of global vaccine production. The biopharma sector holds about 30% of the bioeconomy, and the startup ecosystem has 11,855 startups (1,780 new in 2025). India also supplies 65% of WHO routine immunisation requirements and is shifting from affordable manufacturing to advanced biologics.
  • Maritime and industrial partnerships advanced. Cochin Shipyard Ltd. and Drydocks World agreed a 50:50 joint venture to operate and expand the International Ship Repair Facility in Cochin, the first PPP of its kind in India's ship-repair sector. At Lothal, the National Maritime Heritage Complex Phase 1A is about 82% complete, with a target of 31 Oct 2026 and 1,492 artefacts ready for installation. The ITRA, DGFT and CIL notes fit the wider Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047.
  • Bharat Innovates showcased 37 Indian deep-tech innovators at Bharat Mandapam, linked to the BRICS Business Forum. It reported 20,000 participants, 30 MoUs and USD 254.5 million in funding secured. Separately, the National Conference on Building & Other Construction Workers (BOCW) in Mumbai (11–12 Sep 2026), chaired by Dr. Mansukh Mandaviya, pushed the BOCW Digital Labour Chowk, Labour Chowk-cum-Facilitation Centres and an Online BOCW Cess Collection Portal.
  • Fish, honey and bamboo statistics show the rural-economy push. Bamboo was removed from tree classification in forest law in 2017, and the National Bamboo Mission targets value addition rising from 10% to 70%. Engineered bamboo covers over 42 lakh sq ft of structures, and the Gadchiroli project involves 32 centres and 1,100 people (90% women).

International Affairs & Organizations

  • India hosted the XVIII (18th) BRICS Summit in New Delhi on 12–13 September 2026. ★ The theme was "Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability", and the 20th anniversary of BRICS was marked with a stamp inscribed "BRICS: Two Decades of Cooperation, 2006–2026". More than 350 meetings in nearly 30 cities were cited in the PM's remarks (another release says more than 400). The BRICS New Delhi Declaration reaffirms the three pillars: Political & Security, Economic & Financial, and Cultural & People-to-People.
  • PM Modi's proposals at BRICS rest on four pillars and a series of new mechanisms. The pillars are Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability. Initiatives include a BRICS Integrated Early-Warning System for infectious diseases, a Logistics Supply-Chain Cooperation Framework, a BRICS Incubator Network, a Startup Innovation Fund and a Network on Digital Agriculture. Others are a BRICS MSME Cooperation Portal, an Urban Mobility Hub and a Digital Centre of Excellence for Smart Grids & Energy Storage.
  • On global governance, India proposed a Seafarers Emergency Support Network and a BRICS Continuity & Implementation Mechanism. He described the Global South as being in the "front row of crises, back row of decision-making" and urged a shift from a "pyramid of privilege" to a "platform of partnership". Ten reform proposals are to become a BRICS Reform Roadmap, with emphasis on United Nations Security Council reform. The Declaration also backs a BRICS Expert Working Group on Traditional, Complementary and Integrative Medicine (TCIM).
  • The Prime Minister held bilateral meetings with a long list of leaders around the summit. ★ Before the summit (11 Sep), he met UN Secretary-General António Guterres, Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian and Russia's President Vladimir Putin, and visited INNOPROM India (9–11 Sep 2026). On 12–13 Sep he met China's President Xi Jinping on border peace, trade imbalance and supply chains. He also met Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed of Abu Dhabi, Vietnam's PM Lt-Gen Le Minh Hung, Malaysia's PM Anwar Ibrahim and Ethiopia's PM Abiy Ahmed Ali. On 13 Sep he met the Vice-Presidents of Nigeria (Kashim Shettima Mustapha) and Uganda (Jessica Rose Epel Alupo), Philippines President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. and Kazakhstan's President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev.
  • The UAE meeting produced a USD 11.5 billion integrated greenfield aluminium project in Odisha. ★ The project is described as the largest integrated aluminium investment and builds on the India–UAE Comprehensive Strategic Partnership. Vietnam is a BRICS partner country and is moving to an Enhanced Comprehensive Strategic Partnership after the May 2026 state visit of To Lam. India also supported Uganda's Vision 2040.
  • The Philippines (ASEAN Chair) agreed to join the Coalition for Disaster-Resilient Infrastructure, building on the Strategic Partnership of August 2025. Marcos welcomed India's Strategic Partnership across defence and security, trade, space, railways, fintech, education, tourism and science.
