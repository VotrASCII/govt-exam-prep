01

RBI & Monetary Policy

  • ★ The RBI Bulletin for September 2026 was released on 25 September 2026. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) issue carries seven speeches, three articles and current statistics. The articles are "State of the Economy", "Credit-Deposit Divergence: A Balance Sheet Decomposition" and "Private Corporate Investment: Growth in 2025-26 and Outlook for 2026-27". Bulletin articles carry the authors' views, not the RBI's official position, which is a standard disclaimer worth remembering.
  • ★ India's GDP grew 7.8 per cent in Q1:2026-27. The State of the Economy article says robust growth came despite re-escalating geopolitical tension in West Asia, which is raising energy prices and financial-market volatility. High-frequency indicators showed the economy stayed resilient through August. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth is a perennial exam topic, so note both the figure and the quarter.
  • ★ Headline inflation inched up to 4.8 per cent in August 2026. The Bulletin attributes the rise to the food and beverages group, plus a pickup in fuel and core components. The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) framework has a 4 per cent target with a 6 per cent upper tolerance limit; inflation above 6 per cent for three consecutive quarters counts as a failure. The releases for this week contain no policy-rate decision.
  • The external sector stayed supportive. The Bulletin says strong export growth narrowed the merchandise trade deficit. A moderate current account deficit in Q1:2026-27 and strong Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) flows helped, and foreign exchange reserves reached an all-time high. System liquidity surplus also surged following Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) [FCNR(B)] deposit inflows.
  • ★ Foreign exchange reserves stood at US$765,901 million (₹7,342,985 crore) on 18 September 2026. The RBI's Weekly Statistical Supplement splits this into foreign currency assets US$630,980 million, gold US$111,292 million, Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) US$18,739 million and Reserve Position in the International Monetary Fund (IMF) US$4,890 million. Reserves fell US$14,881 million over the week but are up US$74,793 million since end-March 2026 and US$63,331 million year-on-year.
  • ★ The special USD-INR Forex Swap facility has drawn US$1,43,596 million so far. The RBI introduced it on 8 June 2026 for FCNR(B) deposits, External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) and Overseas Foreign Currency Borrowings (OFCBs). Inflows reported by Authorised Dealer banks till 18 September 2026 were FCNR(B) US$1,32,980 million (mobilised till 31 August 2026), OFCBs US$5,320 million and ECBs US$5,296 million. The FCNR(B) window closed on 31 August 2026, while ECBs and OFCBs remain open up to 31 December 2026. These inflows explain the liquidity surplus.
  • ★ The RBI absorbed surplus liquidity through daily Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF). Overnight auctions were:
    • 21 September: ₹1,00,000 crore notified, ₹75,058 crore accepted.
    • 22 September: ₹75,000 crore notified, ₹71,971 crore accepted.
    • 23 September: ₹75,000 crore notified, offers of ₹87,993 crore, ₹75,026 crore accepted with 75.93 per cent partial acceptance.
    • 24 September: ₹1,25,000 crore notified, ₹1,01,399 crore accepted.
    • 25 September: a 3-day auction of ₹1,50,000 crore notified, ₹90,280 crore accepted, reversing on 28 September.
    • 28 September: an overnight auction of ₹2,00,000 crore announced.

The cut-off and weighted average rate were 5.24 per cent in every auction, and the operational guidelines of press release 2019-2020/1947 (13 February 2020) were unchanged.

  • Daily net liquidity absorption was large. The Weekly Statistical Supplement shows net absorption ranging from ₹89,513 crore (19 September) to ₹6,71,097 crore (15 September). Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) and VRRR operations dominated, with no repo injection in the days shown. This confirms the surplus-liquidity regime behind the 5.24 per cent VRRR cut-off.
  • ★ The Ways and Means Advances (WMA) limit for the Government of India for October 2026–March 2027 is ₹50,000 crore. The RBI may trigger fresh floatation of market loans when 75 per cent of the limit is used. Interest on WMA is charged at the Repo Rate and on overdraft at two per cent above the Repo Rate. The RBI can revise the limit at any time in consultation with the Government.
  • The Floating Rate Bond 2033 (FRB 2033) coupon is 6.87 per cent for 22 September 2026 to 21 March 2027. The coupon equals the average Weighted Average Yield of the last three 182-day Treasury Bill auctions plus a fixed spread of 1.22 per cent. It resets every half-year, so this continues an existing floating-rate mechanism.
  • The RBI ran Open Market Operation (OMO) sales of Government securities to drain liquidity. On 21 September 2026 it notified ₹25,000 crore, received bids of ₹84,982 crore and accepted ₹25,000 crore. The largest acceptances were 6.10% GS 2031 (₹11,512 crore) and 7.95% GS 2032 (₹8,758 crore), while no bids were accepted for 8.28% GS 2027. A further ₹25,000 crore OMO sale was scheduled for 28 September 2026, offering 7.59% GS 2029, 6.45% GS 2029, 7.61% GS 2030, 5.85% GS 2030, 6.54% GS 2032 and 7.26% GS 2032.
  • ★ Broad money (M3) was ₹33,067,446 crore on 15 September 2026. This is 16.6 per cent higher year-on-year, though it fell 0.5 per cent over the fortnight. Its components include currency with the public (₹4,210,170 crore) and time deposits with banks (₹25,242,433 crore). The RBI also released Reserve Money and Money Supply data for the fortnight ended 15 September 2026.
  • The Bulletin's credit-deposit (CD) article argues deposits need not be a binding constraint on lending. Since FY2023, bank credit growth has outpaced deposit growth and pushed the CD ratio above 80 per cent. The authors (Saurabh Ghosh, Prabhat Kumar, Madhuresh Kumar and Monica) say deposits are created when banks lend or invest. They conclude the CD ratio alone is not an appropriate gauge of funding vulnerability. The high ratio at end-March 2026 reflected liability-side adjustments such as cheaper borrowings and higher capital.
  • Private corporate capex is estimated at ₹3.2 lakh crore in 2026-27. The article by Purnendu Kumar, Snigdha Yogindran, Sukti Khandekar and Bhavyashree K uses banks' and financial institutions' project-sanction data and capex phasing plans. The number and total cost of sanctioned projects rose in 2025-26, and infrastructure drew the largest share, led by Power. The pipeline suggests sustained investment momentum.
02

Banking & Financial Sector

  • ★ PSBs and RRBs will open on Sunday, 27 September 2026. The RBI granted approval for all Public Sector Banks and Regional Rural Banks to work on that day. The aim is to mitigate the impact of the proposed three-day strike of 28–30 September 2026 called by the United Forum of Bank Unions (UFBU).
  • ★ The RBI issued final Basel III market risk Directions for commercial banks on 21 September 2026. The Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Minimum Capital Requirements for Market Risk) Directions, 2026 follow a draft of 17 February 2023 that proposed the Simplified Standardised Approach. They take effect from 1 April 2027, and transition scalars have applied since 1 April 2024. Final changes include deleting the trading-book definition in favour of the Investment Directions, aligning interest-rate specific risk tables with the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS), revising treatment of debt mutual funds and exchange traded funds, and covering positions hedged by total return swaps.
  • ★ The RBI released the Supervisory Data Quality Index (sDQI) for Scheduled Commercial Banks for June 2026 on 22 September 2026. It measures Accuracy, Timeliness, Completeness and Consistency of returns and microdata submitted for supervision. It tests adherence to the Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Supervisory Returns) Directions, 2026 and the equivalent Directions for Small Finance Banks.
  • ★ Hero Fincorp Limited was fined ₹10 lakh by an order dated 22 September 2026. The Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) breached the 'Fair Practices Code for Lenders – Charging of Interest' by collecting excess interest from certain loan accounts. The penalty was imposed under Section 58G(1)(b) read with Section 58B(5)(aa) of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, after a statutory inspection with reference to its position as on 31 March 2025.
  • ★ KLM Axiva Finvest Limited was fined ₹2.70 lakh by an order dated 22 September 2026. It failed to pay certain borrowers the surplus realised from auctioning pledged gold over and above the outstanding loan, violating RBI directions on the 'Auction' procedure. This is a recurring gold-loan NBFC compliance theme.
  • Ola Financial Services Private Limited was fined ₹3.10 lakh by an order dated 24 September 2026. The breach concerned Know Your Customer (KYC) directions, specifically failure to carry out risk categorisation of certain customers. The penalty came under Section 30(1) read with Section 26(6) of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, not the RBI Act, because it is a payment-system entity.
  • ★ The 6.94% GS 2036 (maturing 11 May 2036) was re-issued for ₹34,000 crore on 25 September 2026. The cut-off price was ₹98.58 (yield 7.1450 per cent) and the weighted average yield 7.1421 per cent. The RBI received 402 competitive bids worth ₹83,880 crore and accepted 143 worth ₹33,915.822 crore, with 19.5619 per cent partial allotment. Non-competitive bids of ₹84.178 crore were fully accepted and there was no devolvement on Primary Dealers. Underwriting showed a Minimum Underwriting Commitment (MUC) of ₹17,010 crore, Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) of ₹16,990 crore and a commission cut-off of 0.54 paise per ₹100.
  • ★ The RBI released the indicative dated-securities calendar for October 2026–March 2027 totalling ₹7,86,000 crore over 23 weekly auctions. The 30-year Sovereign Green Bond (SGrB) slots of ₹3,000 crore each in five weeks add up to ₹15,000 crore. By maturity mix, the 10-year share is 26.3 per cent, the 15-year 17.6 per cent and the 5-year 12.1 per cent. A greenshoe option allows retaining up to ₹2,000 crore per security, switches are held on the third Monday of each month, and 5 per cent of each auction is reserved for non-competitive retail bidding.
  • ★ The Treasury Bill calendar for the quarter ending December 2026 totals ₹2,99,000 crore. Thirteen weekly auctions from 7 October to 30 December 2026 notify ₹8,000 crore (91-day), ₹8,000 crore (182-day) and ₹7,000 crore (364-day), or ₹23,000 crore a week. Quarterly totals are ₹1,04,000 crore, ₹1,04,000 crore and ₹91,000 crore. The 23 September auction cut-off yields were 5.3900 per cent (91-day), 5.8196 per cent (182-day) and 6.0895 per cent (364-day). The 30 September auction notifies ₹24,000 crore (₹9,000 crore, ₹8,000 crore and ₹7,000 crore).
  • State Government Securities (SGS) auctions raised ₹16,750 crore on 22 September and ₹22,200 crore is planned for 29 September 2026. In the 22 September auction (Andhra Pradesh, Goa, Gujarat, Jammu and Kashmir, Maharashtra, Punjab and Rajasthan) cut-off yields ranged from 7.3286 per cent (Maharashtra SGS 2031) to 7.91 per cent (Punjab 15-year). The 29 September auction involves 12 states on the RBI's e-Kuber Core Banking Solution, with results the same day and payment on 30 September. Interest on new stock is paid half-yearly on 30 March and 30 September, and SGS qualify as Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) investments under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
  • A conversion/switch auction of Government securities was held on 21 September 2026. Source securities such as 8.26% GS 2027 (into 7.19% GS 2060) and 7.06% GS 2028 (into 6.79% GS 2034) were exchanged for longer destination securities. No offers were accepted for 7.88% GS 2030. Switches lengthen the maturity profile and smooth redemption pressure.
  • ★ Scheduled Commercial Banks' (SCBs) deposits were ₹27,623,066 crore and credit ₹22,329,258 crore on 15 September 2026. Year-on-year growth was 17.3 per cent for deposits and 18.1 per cent for credit. Credit growth exceeding deposit growth links to the CD ratio debate. Under the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025, a "fortnight" now means the 15th and last calendar day of a month, effective 15 December 2025, instead of alternate Fridays.
  • The Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE) guarantee cover was extended to Trade Receivables Discounting System (TReDS) platforms. The covered platforms are M1xchange, RXIL and DTX (KredX). The cover is 75 per cent of the amount in default, with maximum exposure of ₹10 crore for buyers and ₹2 crore for sellers on a revolving basis. Invoice discounting grew from ₹40,000 crore in FY 2021-22 to ₹3.5 lakh crore in FY 2025-26.
  • The Competition Commission of India (CCI) reviewed two acquisitions. L'Oréal India Private Limited is acquiring 100 per cent of Onesto Labs Private Limited, a skincare and haircare manufacturer. FIH Mauritius Investments Ltd, wholly owned by Fairfax India Holdings Corp., is acquiring equity in IIFL Capital Services Limited through a preferential issue, an open offer and secondary purchase from promoters.

Government Schemes & Budget

  • ★ The EPF wage ceiling was raised from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month. A Gazette Notification under Chapter III of the Code on Social Security, 2020 took effect on 17 September 2026. The Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) now covers a wider set of employees, and employers must recalibrate payroll systems and enrol newly eligible staff.
  • ★ Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) marked its eighth anniversary. Launched on 23 September 2018, it gives cashless cover up to ₹5 lakh per family for 1,961 procedures across 27 specialties. Funding is shared 60:40 between Centre and States, with 90:10 for the North-Eastern States, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand and Jammu and Kashmir, and 100 per cent Centre for Union Territories without a legislature. The 2026-27 estimate is ₹9,500 crore, roughly 48.51 crore people hold Ayushman cards, and senior-citizen cards exceed 1.36 crore since 11 September 2024. Hospital admissions total 13.25 crore worth ₹2.03 lakh crore.
  • Arogya Manthan 2026 was held at Vigyan Bhawan on 25 September 2026. It celebrated 8 years of AB PM-JAY (more than 60 crore beneficiaries) and 5 years of the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM), with more than 97.8 crore ABHA IDs and 121 crore health records. It launched the National Healthcare Providers Registry (NHPR) Mobile App, a Secure Data Environment and the National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX).
  • ★ Modified UDAN for 2026-27 to 2035-36 has an outlay of ₹28,840 crore. It targets 100 airports and 200 helipads, continuing the Ude Desh ka Aam Naagrik (UDAN) regional connectivity scheme. On 22 September 2026 Civil Aviation Minister Ram Mohan Naidu oversaw 21 Memoranda of Understanding (MoUs) for UDAN-2026. Since launch it has operated 687 routes and 95 aerodromes, carried 1.71 crore passengers on 3.66 lakh flights, and a Challenge-Mode Portal for aerodrome development was launched.
  • ★ The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes for pharmaceuticals show strong results as of June 2026.
    • The Bulk Drug scheme has an outlay of ₹6,940 crore for 41 critical APIs/KSMs/DIs and 48 approved projects. It saw ₹5,210.74 crore invested against ₹4,330 crore committed, and 39 projects were commissioned.
    • The Pharmaceutical scheme has an outlay of ₹15,000 crore. Actual investment is ₹46,744 crore against a ₹17,275 crore target, with 1,21,294 jobs and cumulative sales of ₹4,02,869 crore, of which ₹2,57,370 crore are exports.
    • The Medical Devices scheme has an outlay of ₹3,420 crore and gives a 5 per cent incentive on incremental sales; 57 devices are in production.
  • Five greenfield bulk-drug projects were highlighted on 25 September 2026. Lyfius Pharma (Aurobindo) makes Penicillin G at Kakinada SEZ with ₹2,270.05 crore investment and 15,000 MT per year capacity. Kinvan Private Ltd makes Clavulanic Acid at Nalagarh (₹504.68 crore, 400 MT). Andhra Organics Ltd (Virchow) makes Sulfadiazine, Telmisartan and Olmesartan. Meghmani LLP makes Para-Amino Phenol at Dahej (₹60.46 crore, 13,500 MT). Centrient Pharmaceuticals makes Atorvastatin API at Nawanshahr (₹161.13 crore, 206 MT).
  • The Technology Development Board (TDB) of the Department of Science and Technology (DST) backed two start-ups through the Research Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund. Ubifly Technologies (Chennai) gets ₹285 crore in Optionally Convertible Debentures on a ₹570 crore project to take its three-seater e200X ePlane from Technology Readiness Level 6 to 9. Agnikul Cosmos gets ₹200 crore to take the reusable Agnibaan launch vehicle from TRL-4+ to TRL-8.
  • Venture capital support for marginalised groups was reviewed as of May 2026. The Venture Capital Fund for Scheduled Castes (VCF-SC) has a ₹750 crore corpus at 4 per cent interest, with ₹613.02 crore approved for 145 companies. The Venture Capital Fund for Backward Classes (VCF-BC) has a ₹200 crore corpus at 6 per cent, with ₹142.88 crore approved for 31 companies. Central assistance of ₹46.16 crore has been released under the Support for Marginalised Individuals for Livelihood and Enterprise (SMILE) scheme.
  • NAMASTE has validated 90,186 sewer and septic-tank workers and profiled 2,51,756 waste pickers. PPE kits went to 87,008 workers and capital subsidy for mechanisation reached 983 beneficiaries (₹34.16 crore). The scheme targets mechanised sanitation and worker safety.
  • Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Grameen Kaushalya Yojana (DDU-GKY) 2.0 skilling starts in October 2026. About 70,000 rural youth in 2,015 batches are the first cohort across 24 States/UTs. The overall target for 2026-28 exceeds 9 lakh youth across 31 States/UTs, with training lasting 3 to 12 months.
  • The Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (DILRMP) showed high digitisation. As of 23 September 2026, 99.90 per cent of Records of Rights are computerised and 97.43 per cent of cadastral maps digitised. More than 40.58 crore parcels have the 14-digit Bhu-Aadhaar (Unique Land Parcel Identification Number, ULPIN), and 94.11 per cent of Revenue Courts are computerised.
  • The PAIMANA portal tracks 1,731 central sector infrastructure projects costing ₹33.60 lakh crore as of August 2026. Expenditure is ₹16.33 lakh crore (48.59 per cent). Road Transport and Highways has 982 projects (₹9.52 lakh crore) and Railways has 147 (₹3.14 lakh crore).
  • Two social-sector milestones were announced. ELDERLINE 14567 has received more than 29 lakh calls since 1 October 2021, with 8.67 lakh actionable calls. MY Bharat has crossed 3 crore registered youth since its launch on 31 October 2023.
  • A one-time interest-free loan of ₹50,000 per barn was announced for Flue-Cured Virginia (FCV) tobacco growers. It covers about 44,000 growers, a financial intervention of about ₹220 crore.
  • ★ Tourism ministry schemes were reviewed on World Tourism Day.
    • Swadesh Darshan: 76 projects costing ₹5,295.24 crore, with 75 completed.
    • Swadesh Darshan 2.0: 53 projects costing ₹2,207.08 crore.
    • Challenge-Based Destination Development (CBDD): 37 projects costing ₹687.99 crore.
    • PRASHAD: 54 projects costing ₹1,726.18 crore.
    • Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment (SASCI): 40 projects across 23 states costing ₹3,295.76 crore in FY 2024-25, fully funded by the Centre.
  • The 11th Ayurveda Day (23 September 2026) had the theme "Ayurveda for a Healthier Tomorrow". The national event at Nagpur launched Safety Dossiers for Gokshura and Chandrasura, Standard Treatment Guidelines for skin disorders, a report on the Ayush Services Sector and Ayush Vision 2030. As of 19 September 2026, 12,260 Ayush Aarogya Mandirs are functional against a target of 12,500, and 593 Ayush colleges operate.
  • Cleanliness and governance campaigns run through October. Swachhata Hi Seva 2026 runs 17 September–2 October 2026 with the theme "Swachhata Mein Sahbhag, Swachh Bharat, Viksit Bharat". Special Campaign 6.0 implementation is 2–31 October 2026, with an evaluation phase of 16–30 November 2026 per the Ministry of Textiles. The Ministry of Cooperation held a National Cooperative Training Framework 2026 consultation on 24 September 2026, and She-Mart stores (300 planned across Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka) were launched on 26 September.

Economy & Trade

  • The National Single Window System (NSWS) completed five years on 22 September 2026. Launched on 22 September 2021 by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), it integrates approvals of 32 Central Ministries/Departments and State approvals. It uses PAN as the Single Business ID, with authentication through DSC and DigiLocker. It gives access to 327 Central and 3,452 State approvals, and more than 5.69 lakh business entities are onboarded.
  • ★ World Tourism Day, 27 September 2026, brought new initiatives and fresh sector statistics. The Tourism Minister announced a National Digital Tourism Stack and Dekho Apna Desh 2.0. Tourism contributed 5.22 per cent of GDP (2023-24) and 8.46 crore jobs. Domestic tourist visits were 428.55 crore and foreign tourist visits 2.53 crore in 2025, with foreign exchange earnings of ₹2,76,831 crore. Globally, tourism contributed US$10.9 trillion (about 10 per cent of world GDP) and 357 million jobs in 2024.
  • Digital tourism infrastructure expanded. e-Visa is available to nationals of 172 countries and accounts for 78 per cent of visas, with about 95 per cent of applications processed within 72 hours. The NIDHI+ database lists 63,740 accommodation units, 54 convention centres, 215 food-business operators, 1,450 online travel agents and 15,531 tourism service providers. A Memorandum of Understanding with Google India was signed in June 2026.
  • The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) published a Domestic Tourism Expenditure Survey for July 2025–June 2026. It covered 24,778 first stage units (14,371 rural, 10,407 urban) and 2,46,113 households. Pilgrimage and religious trips had the highest share (48 per cent) of overnight trips. Uttar Pradesh had the highest visitor-trip share (15.7 per cent), followed by Tamil Nadu (13.4 per cent) and Rajasthan (8.0 per cent).
  • India Cine Hub is the National Film Development Corporation (NFDC) single-window portal for film shooting permits. It covers permits, visas and co-production approvals across 34 states and UTs. Consumer content revenue was US$13.1 billion in FY 2024, projected at US$17.5–19.7 billion by FY 2029. 5G reaches 99.9 per cent of districts.
  • Industrial and trade facilitation moves were announced. The Gwalior Telecom Manufacturing Zone phase I gets ₹493 crore from the Department of Telecommunications, with 24 companies promising ₹5,500 crore and about 18,000 jobs. A Trade Connect platform (product-wise and HSN-code-wise) was announced, alongside a 15-fold expansion plan for the roller-chain sector (₹500 crore, 5,000 jobs).
  • GI-tagged Gulbarga Tur Dal was exported to the Maldives. The Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority (APEDA) arranged a 1 MT consignment on 24 September 2026, giving farmers ₹82 per kg against ₹60 per kg in the domestic market. Its Geographical Indication (GI) was registered in 2019.
  • Onion price management continued through the "Kanda Express". Onions reached Kolkata on 24 September 2026 from 1,400 MT of stock in West Bengal sold via NAFED and NCCF at ₹35 per kg. Calibrated releases began on 24 August 2026 and have reached more than 100 cities.
  • Coal sector milestones were reported. Central Coalfields Limited production rose from 50 MT (FY 2013-14) to 87.5 MT (FY 2024-25), about 75 per cent. Coal India Limited's cumulative CSR spend for FY 2014-15 to FY 2025-26 was ₹7,276 crore against a statutory requirement of ₹5,795 crore.
  • The National Land Monetisation Corporation (NLMC) is preparing a land e-auction for Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL). It covers 459 parcels (93,572.21 sq yd): 456 in HB Colony and 3 in Auto Nagar, Gajuwaka. Day 1 on 12 October 2026 offers 243 plots and Day 2 on 16 October 2026 offers 216 plots. The Earnest Money Deposit is ₹2 lakh per auction, and this fits the National Monetization Pipeline theme.
  • Transport infrastructure advanced. The 17.5 km Taroki–Rowghat section of the 95 km Dallirajhara–Rowghat new line was inspected, mainly to move iron ore to Bhilai Steel Plant. The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) signed an MoU with Sardar Sarovar Narmada Nigam Limited for crossing approvals. Three foot over bridges on the Delhi–Saharanpur–Dehradun Expressway cost ₹12.8 crore.
  • Retail and traders' platforms. Bharat Taxi, a cooperative ride platform, launched in Uttar Pradesh on 23 September 2026 in five cities. The National Traders' Welfare Board held its 11th meeting on 25 September 2026 under Shri Sunil J. Singhi, discussing GST rationalisation, ONDC and credit access.
