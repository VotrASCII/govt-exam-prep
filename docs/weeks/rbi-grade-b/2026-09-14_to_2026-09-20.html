RBI & Monetary Policy

  • Overnight Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions on 15 and 16 September 2026 all cleared at a 5.24% cut-off. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) used VRRR auctions under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) to absorb surplus liquidity. On 15 September ₹5,00,000 crore was notified, and ₹3,93,352 crore was offered and accepted. On 16 September the first auction notified ₹2,50,000 crore, drew ₹3,17,088 crore of offers and accepted ₹2,50,025 crore (66.9% partial acceptance at cut-off), while a second auction (11:00–11:30 AM, ₹1,00,000 crore) accepted all ₹40,302 crore offered. This week's releases carry no Monetary Policy Committee decision, so 5.24% is an auction outcome and not a policy rate.
  • VRRR on 17 and 18 September: ₹2,39,959 crore overnight and ₹2,22,629 crore for 3 days, both at 5.24%. The 17 September overnight auction notified ₹3,00,000 crore. The 18 September 3-day auction notified ₹2,25,000 crore, with reversal on 21 September (Monday). RBI then announced a further overnight VRRR of ₹1,00,000 crore for 21 September (9:30–10:00 AM, reversal on 22 September). Operational guidelines still follow Press Release 2019-2020/1947 dated 13 February 2020, so the 2020 framework continues to govern these auctions.
  • Open Market Operation (OMO) sale on 17 September 2026 accepted ₹50,000 crore against ₹66,590 crore of bids. Six Government of India securities (Government Stock, GS) were sold with cut-off yields rising with tenor: 7.59% GS 2029 (6.6007%), 6.79% GS 2029 (6.7023%), 7.61% GS 2030 (6.8191%), 5.77% GS 2030 (6.8589%), 6.68% GS 2031 (6.9080%) and 8.28% GS 2032 (7.0090%). The 8.28% GS 2032 saw the largest acceptance at ₹18,840 crore. OMO sales withdraw durable liquidity, complementing the VRRR auctions.
  • A further OMO sale of ₹25,000 crore is scheduled for 21 September 2026. It was first announced in Press Release 2026-27/1106 dated 11 September 2026. The securities offered are 8.28% GS 2027, 7.17% GS 2028, 6.75% GS 2029, 7.17% GS 2030, 6.10% GS 2031 and 7.95% GS 2032, with no security-wise notified amount. Bids go through RBI's Core Banking Solution (E-Kuber) between 9:30 and 10:30 am, and successful bidders must fund their accounts by noon on 22 September.
  • Government Stock auction of 18 September 2026 raised ₹28,000 crore across two securities. The 7.06% GS 2041 (₹17,000 crore) cleared at a cut-off price of ₹98.45 (yield 7.2305%), and the 7.43% GS 2076 (₹11,000 crore) at ₹96.73 (yield 7.6863%). Competitive bids were ₹36,467 crore and ₹28,172 crore respectively, and there was no devolvement on Primary Dealers (PDs). Cut-off yields of about 7.2% at the 15-year tenor and 7.7% at the 50-year tenor show the shape of the current yield curve.
  • Underwriting auction on 18 September 2026 fixed the Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) commission at 0.43 and 0.72 paise per ₹100. The 0.43 paise rate applied to 7.06% GS 2041 and the 0.72 paise rate to 7.43% GS 2076. The Minimum Underwriting Commitment (MUC) per PD was ₹405 crore and ₹262 crore, with total MUC of ₹8,505 crore and ₹5,502 crore. The scheme dates from the 14 November 2007 notification, and PDs bid electronically on E-Kuber between 9:00 and 9:30 AM.
  • Treasury Bill (T-bill) auction of 16 September 2026 produced cut-off yields of 5.2801% (91-day), 5.7500% (182-day) and 6.0386% (364-day). The notified amounts were ₹9,000 crore, ₹8,000 crore and ₹7,000 crore, with cut-off prices of ₹98.7007, ₹97.2128 and ₹94.3200. The next auction, on 23 September 2026 (settlement 24 September), again offers ₹9,000 crore, ₹8,000 crore and ₹7,000 crore, a total of ₹24,000 crore. It uses the multiple-price method, and retail allocation is capped at 5% of the notified amount.
  • State Government Securities (SGS) auction of 15 September 2026 sought ₹35,900 crore and accepted ₹34,645.055 crore. Kerala's 18-year paper had the highest cut-off yield at 8.07%, followed by Telangana's 2044 paper (8.0182%) and Bihar's 2044 re-issue (8.0166%). Odisha's re-issue of 7.16% SGS 2032 had the lowest cut-off at 7.6094%. Assam accepted only ₹445.055 crore and Uttarakhand accepted nothing in its 7.87% SGS 2044 re-issue.
  • A new SGS auction of ₹16,750 crore is set for 22 September 2026 on E-Kuber. Andhra Pradesh, Goa, Gujarat, Jammu and Kashmir, Maharashtra, Punjab and Rajasthan have offered stock. Up to 10% of the notified amount goes to non-competitive bidders, with a maximum of 1% per bid, and the Retail Direct portal is open to individuals. Results come the same day, payment is on 23 September, and new stock pays interest half-yearly on 23 March and 23 September. SGS are eligible Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) investments under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
  • Government securities switch auction of ₹30,000 crore on 21 September 2026. Seven source securities (for example 8.26% GS 2027 and 7.88% GS 2030) are exchanged into longer destination securities such as 7.19% GS 2060 and 7.50% GS 2034. The source price must equal the Financial Benchmarks India Pvt. Ltd. (FBIL) closing price of the previous working day. The auction is multiple-price, the minimum bid is ₹10,000, and settlement is T+1 (22 September). Switches lengthen the maturity profile of government debt and are broadly cash-neutral.
  • Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) 2019-20 Series IV premature redemption price fixed at ₹15,173 per unit. The series was issued on 17 September 2019, and premature redemption is allowed after the fifth year on an interest payment date, here 17 September 2026. The price is the simple average of the closing price of 999-purity gold over the previous three business days (16, 15 and 11 September 2026), as published by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association Ltd (IBJA).
  • Foreign exchange reserves stood at US$780,782 million (₹74,60,261 crore) on 11 September 2026. They fell US$4,924 million in the week, but are up US$89,674 million since end-March 2026 and US$77,816 million year-on-year. Foreign currency assets were US$645,796 million, gold US$111,225 million, Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) US$18,845 million and the Reserve Position in the International Monetary Fund (IMF) US$4,916 million. These come from the Weekly Statistical Supplement extract of 18 September 2026.
  • Money supply (M3) was ₹3,32,21,648 crore on 31 August 2026, growing 16.7% year-on-year. Time deposits with banks rose 17.1% year-on-year and currency with the public 12.8%. Net bank credit to government grew 8.4% year-on-year, and bank credit to the commercial sector 18.6%. Because the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025 redefined a banking "fortnight" as the period ending on the 15th and the last calendar day of a month (from 15 December 2025), reporting dates are now 15th and month-end, not alternate Fridays.
02

Banking & Financial Sector

  • ★ RBI extended Section 35A directions on two Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs). The Valsad Mahila Nagrik Sahakari Bank Ltd., Valsad (Gujarat) now has its directions run from 18 September to 18 December 2026. The Gauhati Co-operative Urban Bank Ltd., Guwahati has its directions extended from 17 September to 17 December 2026. Both are directions under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, originally issued on 17 December 2025 for six months and extended in three-month steps. RBI stresses that an extension does not mean it is satisfied with the bank's financial position.
  • ★ RBI cancelled the Certificate of Registration (CoR) of five Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) under Section 45-IA(6) of the RBI Act, 1934. The companies are Dar's Financial Services Pvt Ltd (3 August 2026), Rolta Holding and Finance Corporation Pvt Ltd (6 August), Vasudeo Securities Pvt Ltd (6 August), Parsoli Corporation Limited (18 August) and A. C. Choksi Financial Services Pvt. Ltd. (31 August). They may no longer transact NBFC business as defined in Section 45-I(a) of the Act.
  • ★ Eight NBFCs surrendered their CoR, which RBI cancelled under Section 45-IA(6). Four exited the NBFC business: Anupam Mercantile, Grand Motor and Finance, Sky Limit International Finance and ASA International India Microfinance. Anagram Industries Limited was cancelled as an unregistered Core Investment Company (CIC), and CDN Finance Private Limited as an unregistered Type I NBFC, one that takes no public funds and has no customer interface. Shivam Securities and April Investment and Finance ceased to be legal entities through amalgamation, merger, dissolution or strike-off.
  • Scheduled Commercial Bank (SCB) deposits and credit grew sharply year-on-year to 31 August 2026. Aggregate deposits were ₹2,78,71,530 crore (up 17.8% year-on-year) and bank credit ₹2,23,87,567 crore (up 19.1%). Demand deposits were ₹34,48,742 crore and time deposits ₹2,44,22,788 crore, while food credit was ₹1,12,042 crore. The Scheduled Banks' Statement of Position records 119 scheduled commercial banks, 26 scheduled state co-operative banks and 55 scheduled primary (urban) co-operative banks.
  • New Unified Payments Interface (UPI) framework keeps person-to-person (P2P) transfers free and sets a tiered Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) for merchant payments. P2P is completely free, and person-to-merchant (P2M) payments up to ₹2,000 carry zero MDR. Above ₹2,000 the nominal MDR is 0.4%, capped at ₹300 for ₹75,000 and above. Essential sectors (railways, telecom, insurance, fuel, agriculture) pay a flat ₹5 per transaction, and capital-market transactions 0.02% (capped at ₹300). About 96% of transactions are unaffected, and 5% of total MDR is earmarked for a fund promoting small-merchant UPI adoption.
  • NHAI's OneTag feature on the Rajmargyatra App will allow FASTag portability. The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) announced it for 17 September 2026 (4 PM). Users can change issuer without a new tag, with an inter-operability cooling period of 6 months. FASTag penetration is above 98%, about 13 crore users, so the change targets competition and service quality among issuing banks.
  • Pension Sakhis MoU brings the National Pension System (NPS) to rural households. The Department of Rural Development (DoRD) and the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA) signed it on 14 September 2026 (Shri Amit Shukla and Ms. Sumeet Kaur Kapoor, witnessed by Shri Rohit Kansal). Outreach runs through the Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana-National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM) network, especially women-led Self Help Groups and bank correspondent "Sakhi" cadres. The aim is awareness, enrolment and post-enrolment support.
  • Frontline financial-inclusion cadres in rural India were profiled. There are 1.48 lakh Business Correspondent Sakhis, 50,548 Bank Sakhis and 56,727 Financial Literacy Community Resource Persons. Together with SHG banking they have mobilised ₹13.41 lakh crore of credit since 2013-14, and 3.81 crore women SHG members received financial-literacy training in 2025-26. This shows how women-led cadres extend the formal financial system to villages.
  • India Post's re-invention was discussed with postal employee organisations. The meeting with Dr Jitendra Singh covered 24-hour delivery, Aadhaar-related services, PAN and Passport Seva, doorstep banking and digital pension services. The growth of India Post Payments Bank extends banking to villages, and postmen are shifting towards banking and citizen services. A proposal seeks greater use of the nationwide post network for government schemes and last-mile delivery.
  • The Department of Public Enterprises (DPE) held a credit-rating workshop for Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs). More than 80 senior finance executives from Maharatna and Navratna CPSEs attended on 16 September 2026. The participating rating agencies were CareEdge Global IFSC Ltd., CARE Ratings Ltd., CRISIL Ratings Ltd., ICRA Ratings Ltd. and Infomerics Valuation & Rating Ltd. The objective is transparency and enabling CPSEs to raise funds at competitive rates.
  • The Competition Commission of India (CCI) cleared three combinations on 17 September 2026. OMERS Infrastructure Asia Holdings Pte. Ltd. may acquire additional shareholding in Azure Power Global Limited from CDPQ Infrastructures Asia Pte. Ltd. Several investors, including ISAF III Onshore Fund, may acquire 50% of Great White Global Pvt Ltd. Westview Cricket Ltd. and Poonawalla Sports & Fitness Pvt Ltd were cleared to acquire Rajasthan Royals (India), Paarl Royals (South Africa) and Barbados Royals (Barbados).
  • RBI released Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) data for August 2026 on 18 September 2026. The data cover both the Automatic Route and the Approval Route. The press release itself carries no figures, so for study purposes only the fact and date of release matter.

Government Schemes & Budget

  • ★ Cabinet raised the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) wage ceiling from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month. The decision was taken on 16 September 2026 and takes effect from 17 September 2026 (Vishwakarma Jayanti and Seva Divas). More than 51 lakh additional employees earning ₹15,000–₹25,000 come under the Employees' Provident Fund, the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS) and Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance (EDLI). Expected expenditure is ₹56,696 crore over five years, with annual government outgo of about ₹11,339 crore versus ₹10,250 crore at present.
  • ★ PM Vishwakarma Scheme completed three years with 30 lakh artisans registered. Launched on 17 September 2023 with an outlay of ₹13,000 crore (FY 2023-24 to FY 2027-28) for 18 traditional trades, it rests on three pillars: Samman (recognition), Samarthya (skill) and Samriddhi (prosperity). The anniversary release cites 24.50 lakh trained, 18.15 lakh toolkits and 6.18 lakh loans totalling ₹5,297 crore. A fact sheet as of 15 September cites 24.37 lakh trained, 18.16 lakh toolkits and 6.19 lakh loans worth about ₹5,316 crore. Anniversary events were held in Kolkata (Dhono Dhanyo Auditorium), and West Bengal joined the scheme in May 2026.
  • ★ PM Vishwakarma benefit structure. Basic training lasts 5–7 days with a stipend of ₹500 a day, advanced training lasts 15 days or more, and toolkit support is up to ₹15,000. Collateral-free Enterprise Development Loans of up to ₹3 lakh come in two tranches, ₹1 lakh over 18 months and ₹2 lakh over 30 months, at 5% fixed interest with an 8% subvention borne by the government. A digital incentive of ₹1 per transaction applies for up to 100 transactions a month. Only one member per family is eligible.
  • ★ Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana-Urban (PMAY-U) and PMAY-U 2.0 housing progress. As of 9 August 2026, 1.25 crore houses had been sanctioned and over 1 crore delivered. Under PMAY-U 2.0 (relaunched September 2024, targeting 1 crore additional urban families in 2024-29) 18.38 lakh houses are sanctioned: 14.40 lakh Beneficiary-Led Construction (BLC), 2.48 lakh Affordable Housing in Partnership (AHP), 1.36 lakh Interest Subsidy Scheme (ISS) and 13,046 Affordable Rental Housing (ARH). Central assistance is up to ₹2.50 lakh per unit, and the income bands are Economically Weaker Section (EWS) up to ₹3 lakh, Low Income Group (LIG) ₹3–6 lakh and Middle Income Group (MIG) ₹6–9 lakh. The mission was extended to 30 September 2026, and the Technology Sub-Mission (Global Housing Technology Challenge India) shortlisted 54 technologies and 6 Light-House Projects.
  • ★ Semicon 2.0 approved with an outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore. The Cabinet approved it on 15 July 2026 as the successor to Semicon 1.0 (approved December 2021, ₹76,000 crore), whose 12 semiconductor units carry cumulative investment of ₹1.64 lakh crore. Its six pillars are design, machines and materials, new fabs, advanced packaging, research and talent development. The same approval round carried the IndiaAI Mission at ₹10,371.92 crore, and the Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS) was enlarged.
  • ★ ECMS outlay was raised to ₹40,000 crore in Union Budget 2026-27. The scheme was launched on 8 April 2025 with an initial outlay of ₹22,919 crore (another release rounds the earlier figure to ₹22,000 crore). By August 2026 it had approved 106 projects across 15 states with investment of ₹69,548 crore, 74,628 direct jobs and projected production of ₹5.34 lakh crore. Electronics production reached ₹13.11 lakh crore in 2025-26 versus ₹1.9 lakh crore in 2014-15.
  • ★ Data-centre policy milestones and a Budget tax holiday. Installed capacity rose from 375 MW (2020) to 1.57 GW (August 2026) and is projected at 8 GW by 2030, with USD 70 bn of investment underway and USD 90 bn announced. Data centres got infrastructure status in 2022-23. Union Budget 2026-27 gives eligible foreign cloud service providers a tax holiday till 2047 (tax years 2026-27 to 2046-47). The Central Electricity Authority (CEA) projects data-centre demand of 17 GW by 2031-32.
  • ★ A Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Scheme of ₹1 lakh crore for private-sector R&D and deep-tech was highlighted. Dr Jitendra Singh cited it alongside 25 National Mission Technology Innovation Hubs for Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems. It supports technology-driven competitiveness across mining and metals, from extraction through processing, logistics and downstream manufacturing.
  • Pre-Matric and Post-Matric Scholarships for Scheduled Castes (SC) and other groups. Central assistance from FY 2014-15 to FY 2025-26 was ₹4,893.03 crore (about 299.66 lakh beneficiaries) for Pre-Matric and ₹46,581.54 crore (about 612.98 lakh) for Post-Matric. In FY 2025-26 alone, Pre-Matric disbursed ₹562.36 crore to 26.79 lakh students and Post-Matric ₹6,186.95 crore to 47.53 lakh. Since FY 2021-22 payments go through Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) with de-duplication, e-KYC and linkages to DigiLocker, API SETU, the Unified District Information System for Education (UDISE) and the Public Financial Management System (PFMS).
  • SHREYAS spent ₹3,509.39 crore on 1,18,248 SC/OBC students from 2014-15 to 2025-26. The Scholarships for Higher Education for Young Achievers Scheme has four components. Free Coaching accounts for ₹145.10 crore (23,208 beneficiaries), Top Class Education ₹703.44 crore (35,237), the National Overseas Scholarship ₹461.57 crore (764 beneficiaries abroad) and the National Fellowship for SC Students ₹2,199.28 crore (59,039). Junior and Senior Research Fellows receive ₹31,000 and ₹35,000 per month.
  • PM-YASASVI disbursed ₹15,555.53 crore over 2014-25. It covers Pre-Matric (₹2,163.15 crore), Post-Matric (₹12,118.75 crore), hostels (₹346.79 crore for 28,865 seats) and Top Class Education for schools (₹116.69 crore, 45,228 students) and colleges (₹810.15 crore, 37,937 students). It sits alongside SHREYAS in the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment's scholarship portfolio.
  • Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (NMBA) mass pledge has reached 34.54 crore citizens. These include 13.70 crore youth and 10.64 crore women, with 1.67 lakh Nasha Mukt Mitras, 31.40 lakh outreach activities and 755 new de-addiction facilities in two years. A later release cites 34.55 crore, 13.71 crore and 10.65 crore. A 100-week Nasha Mukt Yuva campaign (National Service Scheme and MY Bharat) started on 18 September 2026 with the first national pledge on 20 September.
  • Kishau Multipurpose Project agreement involves six states. Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan, Delhi and Haryana participated, with Union Home Minister Amit Shah present. It is a 232.6-metre-high concrete gravity dam on the Tons River with storage of 1,562 million cubic metres, irrigation of 97,000 hectares and 1,476 million units of hydropower. It is a National Project with 90% central funding, and the formal agreement is dated 16 June 2026 against the original pact of 12 May 1994.
  • Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) Phase II statistics as of 17 September 2026. There are 5.69 lakh Open Defecation Free (ODF) Plus villages, 5.25 lakh ODF Plus Model villages, 5.38 lakh villages with solid-waste arrangements and 5.65 lakh with liquid-waste systems. 1,314 GOBARdhan biogas plants operate, and 10.28 crore toilets were built in 2014-2019. Swachhata Hi Seva 2026 (17 September–2 October, theme "Swachhata Mein Sahbhag, Swachh Bharat, Viksit Bharat") has a nationwide shramdaan on 1 October.
  • Special Campaign 6.0 targets e-waste. Preparation runs from 15 to 30 September 2026 and implementation from 2 to 31 October 2026, with a portal at scdpm.nic.in going live on 16 September. Past achievements across 2021-2025 were 24.20 lakh sites covered, 180.48 lakh files weeded and ₹5,102.91 crore revenue, and in 2025 alone ₹833.92 crore. It extends Special Campaign 5.0 (October 2025), and most ministries reported their own 5.0 results.
  • Pradhan Mantri Khanij Kshetra Kalyan Yojana (PMKKKY) status as of July 2026. 4,70,020 projects worth ₹1,09,938 crore were sanctioned, with 2,92,156 completed (₹49,973 crore) and 78,809 ongoing (₹30,512 crore committed). The scheme directs district mineral foundation funds to mining-affected communities.
  • State Finance Ministers and Secretaries conference, 18–19 September 2026. Held in New Delhi on "Financing India's Journey towards Viksit Bharat", it featured the Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) Secretary Smt. Anuradha Thakur and Shri N.K. Singh, chair of the XV Finance Commission. Centre-state fiscal coordination is the exam-relevant theme.
  • Jan Aushadhi and health-spending figures. The Pradhan Mantri Bharatiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana (PMBJP) has over 20,000 Kendras, 2,110 medicines and 315 surgical items, with sales of ₹7,873.85 crore and citizen savings of ₹37,200 crore. Health Minister J.P. Nadda cited Ayushman Bharat at about 11.5 crore beneficiaries and out-of-pocket spending falling from 62% (2014) to 43% (2026).

Economy & Trade

  • ★ Wholesale Price Index (WPI) inflation for August 2026 was 9.92% year-on-year. Primary Articles rose 7.76%, Fuel & Power 22.93% and Manufactured Products 8.37%. The All Commodities index was 110.8 versus 110.0 in July 2026, and the Food Index (weight 24.99%) rose 7.05% versus 6.65% in July. Major drivers were mineral oils, food articles, basic metals and chemicals, and the response rate was 84.4% for the provisional estimate.
  • Producer Price Index (PPI) data for August 2026. The provisional Output PPI All Commodities index was 110.8 versus 109.9 in July. Sector indices were Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing 116.5, Mining and Quarrying 122.0, Manufactured Products 109.7 and Electricity 90.9. The draft Input PPI for manufacturing was 104.2, up 1.6 points, led by tobacco (+8.86 points), basic metals (+7.43) and chemicals (+5.73).
  • ★ India's trade in April–August 2026-27 shows strong export growth. Total exports were US$399.27 bn versus US$345.55 bn (up 15.55%), with merchandise US$215.91 bn (up 17.85%) and non-petroleum exports US$180.61 bn (up 14.39%). Electronic goods (+89.82%), petroleum (+63.27%) and engineering (+24.86%) are listed among the top export drivers. NITI Aayog's Trade Watch Quarterly puts India's Q1 FY27 trade at US$506.9 bn (up 15.5% year-on-year).
  • August 2026 estimated trade: deficit narrowed to US$9.41 bn. Merchandise exports were US$43.81 bn versus US$34.74 bn a year earlier, merchandise imports US$70.67 bn versus US$61.96 bn, services exports US$38.87 bn versus US$31.19 bn and services imports US$21.42 bn versus US$15.59 bn. Total exports were US$82.68 bn and total imports US$92.09 bn, against a deficit of US$11.62 bn a year ago. The top export destinations listed were the USA, Singapore, Spain, China and Tanzania, and the top import sources the USA, Russia, China, Oman and Taiwan.
  • Balance of Payments (BoP) for July 2026 shows a current account deficit (CAD) of US$7.0 bn. This compares with US$3.2 bn in July 2025, and the April–July 2026 CAD is US$11.2 bn. The merchandise deficit was US$31.7 bn (exports US$45.1 bn, imports US$76.8 bn), and net services surplus US$17.6 bn. The capital account surplus was US$27.7 bn, with net Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) US$7.3 bn, net Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) US$4.1 bn and net External Commercial Borrowings (ECB) minus US$2.3 bn. The overall balance was US$20.8 bn, meaning an equal addition to reserves.
  • RBI's software and ITES export survey for 2025-26. Software services exports rose 8.2% to US$221.4 bn, and including foreign affiliates' services (commercial presence) rose 9.5% to US$239.3 bn. Computer services were 69.3% of exports and the United States took 54.1%, with Europe at 31.8% (United Kingdom 15.4%). The US dollar dominated invoicing at 72.6%, followed by the euro (9.6%), rupee (6.3%) and pound sterling (6.0%), and 91.7% of services were delivered off-site. The survey contacted 7,569 companies, and 2,363 responded, covering about 89% of estimated exports.
  • Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) indicators for August 2026. The overall Labour Force Participation Rate (LFPR, age 15+) was 55.6% (rural 58.2%, urban 50.4%) and the female LFPR 34.8%. The Worker Population Ratio was 52.8% and the unemployment rate 5.0%, with rural unemployment at 4.1% (lowest since January 2026) and urban at 6.8%. The sample was 3,70,160 persons. A separate release gave the first district-level estimates, with Surat the highest LFPR at 68.6%.
  • ★ The Prime Minister cited quarterly GDP growth of 7.8% and a sovereign rating upgrade at SEMICON India 2026. The 7.8% figure was presented alongside a 3,000 km Dedicated Freight Corridor, and the rating upgrade was attributed to a Japanese credit rating agency. It is the latest growth datapoint in this week's material, but it is a speech citation and not an RBI forecast.
  • Textile exports rose 16.1% in August 2026 to ₹29,776 crore. The previous August figure was ₹25,656 crore, and April–August exports were ₹1.43 lakh crore (up 10.3%). Handicrafts (excluding handmade carpets) grew 41.4%, cotton yarn and fabrics 24.1%, man-made yarn and fabrics 19.9%, carpets 15.0% and ready-made garments 6.1%.
  • Sugar policy was eased and the Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) set at ₹365 per quintal from 1 October 2026. Bulk consumers' stockholding limit goes from 15 to 30 days, but stock beyond 15 days must be imported sugar under the Advance Authorisation Scheme and the Tariff Rate Quota. Bulk consumers must disclose stock every Friday on the foodstock portal. Retail sugar prices fell about 10% (₹65 to ₹58.50) and ex-mill prices about 25%.
  • Export facilitation: Registration-cum-Membership Certificate (RCMC) exemption from 16 September 2026. Export consignments with Free on Board (FOB) value up to ₹3 lakh (about USD 3,000) are exempt, which helps MSMEs, artisans, first-time exporters and e-commerce exporters. The Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority (APEDA) also signed an MoU for Gulfood 2027 (15–19 March 2027, UAE), where India will be Official Partner Country for the second consecutive year.
  • Commercial coal mine auctions have run since 18 June 2020. Some 147 auctions have produced 44 new companies winning blocks, with the 16th round launched on 17 September 2026 offering 25 blocks (21 fully and 4 partially explored) across 9 states. The government estimates ₹47,500 crore annual revenue, ₹55,000 crore capital investment and 4.9 lakh jobs. Six mines were awarded in the round's first stage, with combined reserves of 1,504 million tonnes and peak capacity of 11.62 MTPA.
  • Fisheries and silk targets. At a deep-sea fishing workshop in Gandhinagar, fish production was cited at 197.75 lakh tonnes (2024-25) and seafood exports at ₹73,890 crore, with the Access Pass fee for cooperatives cut from ₹25,000 to ₹5,000. Silk production is 42,000 tonnes (26,000 in 2014), with a target of 60,000 tonnes by 2030-31 and the No. 1 global position by 2028-29.

The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) fined Roppen Transportation Services Pvt Ltd (Rapido) ₹10 lakh for dark patterns under the 2023 Dark Patterns Guidelines. Two CGST Delhi South cases involved fraudulent Input Tax Credit (ITC) of over ₹15.78 crore on ₹87.67 crore of bogus invoices and over ₹25.22 crore on ₹140.14 crore, with arrests under Section 69 of the CGST Act, 2017.   • India to export power to Nepal from 13 September to 31 December 2026. International Affairs & Organizations

  • ★ India and MERCOSUR signed a First Additional Protocol and began talks to expand their Preferential Trade Agreement (PTA). The protocol amends Article 16 of Annex III (Rules of Origin) so electronic Certificates of Origin are valid like paper ones. It was signed by Commerce Secretary Shri Rajesh Agarwal with the Ambassadors of Uruguay and Paraguay. The PTA was signed on 25 January 2004, took effect on 1 June 2009, and covers 450 Indian and 452 MERCOSUR tariff lines. Piyush Goyal and Uruguay's Foreign Minister Mario Lubetkin (pro-tempore MERCOSUR) launched expansion negotiations, and MERCOSUR is a PTA partner, not an FTA partner.
  • ★ India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement (FTA) gained force through New Zealand's legislation on 16 September 2026. The FTA was signed on 27 April 2026 and gives 100% duty-free access to all Indian exports, including textiles, apparel, leather, footwear, engineering goods, pharma and agriculture. New Zealand's exports of wood, wool, sheep meat and leather hides get preferential access. The investment facilitation target is USD 20 billion, with provisions on services and professional and student mobility.
  • G20 Energy Abundance Ministerial Meeting in Houston, 14–16 September 2026. Union Minister Manohar Lal put India's installed power capacity at 552 GW with over 50% non-fossil, and restated the net-zero goal of 2070. He committed to technology-neutral pathways and cooperation through the International Solar Alliance, One Sun One World One Grid (OSOWOG) and the Global Biofuels Alliance. He also toured NASA's Johnson Space Centre during the visit.
  • India holds the BRICS chair in 2026. Vice-President C.P. Radhakrishnan praised the "successful BRICS Summit" and the New Delhi Leaders' Declaration, and the UAE President called the PM to praise India's BRICS presidency. India's initiatives cited include Vaccine Maitri, Life (LiFE), the International Solar Alliance and the first IIT campus outside India (Zanzibar). The Women and Child Development Ministry's meeting with Nigeria was aligned with the BRICS-2026 chairship.
  • BIMSTEC cooperation: Third Conference on Traditional Medicine in Goa (16–17 September 2026). All seven members attended (Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Thailand), and the "Goa Conclusions" set four cooperation areas: institutional and policy, standards and trade, documentation and capacity building, and implementation mechanisms. A BIMSTEC Energy Centre programme (14–18 September, Bengaluru) was also held, and the Centre was launched on 1 April 2025.
  • Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) 25th Meeting of Ministers in Dushanbe, Tajikistan, on 17 September 2026. India's delegation was headed by Shri Yashvir Singh and highlighted Security, Connectivity and Opportunity. The meeting agreed an Action Plan 2026-2030 and Regulations on a Special Working Group for Creative Economy.
  • India–Nepal Inter-Governmental Sub-Committee (IGSC) met on 16–17 September 2026 in New Delhi. It covered customs and trade facilitation, standards, phytosanitary matters, border logistics, rail and road connectivity, and digitalisation such as e-verification and e-certificates. It sits alongside the power-export arrangement as part of Neighbourhood First economic ties.
  • Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar visited Bhutan from 14 to 16 September 2026. He met the King in Thimphu, was Chief Guest at National Voters' Day in Paro (15 September) and met Prime Minister Dasho Tshering Tobgay. The purpose was to strengthen cooperation between the Election Commissions of India and Bhutan.
  • India's parliamentary delegation attended the 69th Commonwealth Parliamentary Conference (CPC) in Cape Town (12–19 September 2026). It was led by Shri Harivansh, Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, with 8 MPs, 32 presiding officers and 18 secretaries. Bilateral meetings were held with South Africa's National Assembly Speaker Angela Thokozile Didiza and others. Topics included inclusive fair trade, digital payments, agricultural credit and World Trade Organization (WTO) dispute settlement.
  • NHRC ran the Fifth ITEC Executive Capacity-Building Programme (14–19 September 2026). The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC), under the Indian Technical and Economic Cooperation (ITEC) programme, hosted 38 officials from National Human Rights Institutions of 14 Global South countries. These were Algeria, Chile, Ecuador, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, Kenya, Kyrgyzstan, Malaysia, Oman, Samoa, South Africa, Tajikistan and Zimbabwe. NHRC Chairperson is Justice V. Ramasubramanian.
  • Bilateral military engagements. The Samudra Laksamana exercise (4th edition) took place at Lumut, Malaysia (9–12 September) with INS Mysore. SLINEX-26 (13th edition) with Sri Lanka runs 17–21 September at Visakhapatnam, and SLINEX began in 2005. Yudh Abhyas 2026 (22nd edition) with the United States involved about 600 personnel each side, and the Chief of the Army Staff visited Russia from 16 to 18 September.

Sports / Environment / Science & Tech

Held 15–18 September 2026 under the theme "Circular Economy: Transition for People and Prosperity", it saw participation of about 2,700 delegates from over 60 countries by one count (3,603 stakeholders from 62 countries by another). The Union Environment Minister launched a Soft Release Centre, reviewed 11 sq km of grassland restoration and 30 km of predator-proof fencing, and flagged off 33 vehicles. Between 24 August and 7 September 2026, 150 inspections were made (7 construction and demolition, 98 industrial, 45 diesel generator sets). In Hapur, 22 units were inspected on 15 September, with 6 compliant, 16 non-compliant and 12 show-cause notices issued. Cumulatively 28,547 units have been inspected, with 1,829 closure directions.

Its theme is "Silicon to Systems: Building the Ecosystem", with over 600 companies from 52 countries and about 50,000 visitors expected. Five semiconductor plants are in commercial production, 70,000 chip-design engineers have been trained (target 85,000), and 105 chip-design startups accessed Electronic Design Automation tools. Odisha's pavilion showed ₹23,600 crore of proposals and a planned 870-acre Silicon Valley at Naraj.

India's first soil-carbon payments of ₹2.9 crore reached 2,550 farmers in Punjab and Haryana, with 50,000 carbon credits issued. Researchers at the Jawaharlal Nehru Centre for Advanced Scientific Research (JNCASR) reported thallium copper selenide (TlCu₅Se₃) with a thermoelectric figure of merit (zT) of 1.7, published in Science Advances.

The 4th Hanle Dark Sky Reserve Star Party (7–12 September 2026) trained 25 local villagers as astronomy ambassadors. CSIR-NIScPR's SARAL AI turns research papers into short videos.

The Ayush Visa was launched on 27 July 2023, and NITI Aayog released a roadmap in July 2026 to make Ayurveda global by 2047.
