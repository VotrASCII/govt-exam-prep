RBI & Monetary Policy

  • ★ RBI ran a week-long series of Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions, and every one cleared at a 5.24% cut-off rate. Between 31 Aug and 4 Sep 2026 the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) absorbed surplus liquidity through 1-day, 3-day, 7-day and 15-day VRRRs. In each case the cut-off rate equalled the weighted average rate of 5.24%, and the full amount offered was accepted (no partial acceptance). This is the auction-clearing rate, not the policy repo rate, which did not feature in this week's releases. All operations follow the guidelines in Press Release 2019-2020/1947 dated 13 Feb 2020.
  • Overnight (1-day) VRRRs drew large but uneven demand. The second overnight auction of 31 Aug (11:00–11:30 AM) notified ₹4,00,000 crore and accepted ₹2,49,840 crore, reversing on 1 Sep. The second overnight auction of 1 Sep notified ₹4,00,000 crore and accepted ₹2,59,846 crore, reversing on 2 Sep. On 2 Sep, ₹5,00,000 crore was notified and ₹4,59,843 crore accepted. On 3 Sep the main window notified ₹6,00,000 crore and accepted ₹5,18,742 crore, while the second window (12:00–12:30 PM) notified ₹1,50,000 crore and accepted only ₹34,652 crore.
  • The 15-day, 7-day and 3-day VRRRs were also run. The 15-day VRRR of 31 Aug notified ₹6,00,000 crore and accepted ₹1,34,625 crore. The 7-day VRRR of 1 Sep (reversal 8 Sep) notified ₹6,00,000 crore and accepted ₹1,14,320 crore. On 4 Sep the 3-day VRRR notified ₹7,00,000 crore and accepted ₹5,41,975 crore, and a second 3-day window notified ₹1,50,000 crore and accepted ₹60,419 crore. Both 3-day auctions reversed on Monday 7 Sep because of the weekend.
  • ★ A 30-day VRRR of ₹7,00,000 crore was announced for Monday 7 Sep 2026, with reversal on Wednesday 7 Oct 2026. The auction window is 9:30–10:00 AM. Premature reversal (partial reversal of a bid is allowed) is possible only through the E-Kuber portal, 09:00–17:00 on Mumbai working days. The request must also be emailed to the Financial Markets Operations Department and placed at least two working days before the original reversal date. Settlement is at "start of day" on the next working day, crediting principal plus accrued interest to the participant's RBI current account and debiting the collateral from its reverse repo constituent Subsidiary General Ledger (SGL) account.
  • Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) data for 24–30 Aug 2026 (Weekly Statistical Supplement) showed net absorption every day, a surplus-liquidity signal. The largest net absorption was ₹4,02,124 crore on 28 Aug and the smallest was ₹1,90,718 crore on 26 Aug. The Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) peaked at ₹2,63,990 crore on 29 Aug, and VRRR absorption was ₹1,52,537 crore on 28 Aug. Borrowing under the Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) stayed in a narrow range of ₹284–₹2,401 crore.
  • ★ RBI auctioned ₹32,000 crore of Government of India (GoI) dated securities on Friday 4 Sep 2026. The offer was New GS 2031 (repayment 7 Sep 2031) for ₹21,000 crore and 7.71% GS 2066 (repayment 18 May 2066) for ₹11,000 crore. Settlement was on Monday 7 Sep, using the multiple price method, with bidding on E-Kuber (the RBI Core Banking Solution). GoI could retain additional subscription of up to ₹2,000 crore per security. The specific notification was F.No.4(1)-B(W&M)/2026 dated 31 Aug 2026, and "When Issued" trading ran 1–4 Sep.
  • Auction results: New GS 2031 cleared at a cut-off yield to maturity (YTM) of 6.5300% and 7.71% GS 2066 at 7.6618%. New GS 2031 received 137 competitive bids for ₹49,886 crore at cut-off price ₹100.00. It accepted 76 bids for ₹20,996.823 crore, with 35.1939% partial allotment (28 bids) and a weighted average yield (WAY) of 6.5163%. 7.71% GS 2066 received 241 bids for ₹34,980 crore at cut-off price ₹100.58, accepting 72 bids for ₹10,986.680 crore, with 84.8966% partial allotment (11 bids) and a WAY of 7.6570%. Devolvement on Primary Dealers (PDs) was NIL for both.
  • Underwriting and bidding rules matter for exam questions. PDs bid in Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) at 09:00–09:30 AM. The Minimum Underwriting Commitment (MUC) and minimum ACU bidding commitment per PD was ₹500 crore for New GS 2031 and ₹262 crore for 7.71% GS 2066. The ACU commission cut-off was 0.27 paise per ₹100 for New GS 2031 and 0.62 paise for 7.71% GS 2066. New-security auctions are yield based, re-issues are price based, a new Floating Rate Bond auction is spread based, and the minimum bid is ₹10,000 in multiples of ₹10,000.
  • Up to 5% of the notified amount is reserved for non-competitive bidders, including via the Retail Direct portal (rbiretaildirect.org.in). Non-competitive allotment is at the weighted average rate of successful competitive bids. Non-resident investment is under the Fully Accessible Route (FAR). Repo eligibility follows the Master Direction – RBI (Repurchase Transactions (Repo)) Directions, 2025.
  • Treasury Bill (T-bill) auction of 2 Sep 2026 raised the full ₹24,000 crore. The 91-day bill (notified ₹9,000 crore) had a cut-off yield of 5.2599% and WAY of 5.2525%. The 182-day bill (₹8,000 crore) had a cut-off yield of 5.6588% and WAY of 5.6189%. The 364-day bill (₹7,000 crore) had a cut-off yield of 5.9090% and WAY of 5.8887%. Cut-off prices were ₹98.7056, ₹97.2558 and ₹94.4351 respectively.
  • The next T-bill auction was announced for Wednesday 9 Sep 2026, with settlement on 10 Sep. It covers ₹9,000 crore (91-day), ₹8,000 crore (182-day) and ₹7,000 crore (364-day), a total of ₹24,000 crore, on a price-based multiple price method. Retail investors are capped at 5% of the notified amount. Non-competitive allocation outside the notified amount is available to State Governments, Union Territories with legislature, eligible Provident Funds and designated Foreign Central Banks.
  • State Government Securities (SGS) auction of 1 Sep 2026 accepted ₹26,590.030 crore of ₹27,000 crore notified, with 15 states participating. Chhattisgarh accepted only ₹390.030 crore of ₹500 crore in the re-issue of 7.64% Chhattisgarh SGS 2042. Uttarakhand accepted nothing of ₹300 crore notified (re-issue of 7.64% Uttarakhand SGS 2038). Newly issued securities were Bihar SGS 2051 (25-year), Jharkhand SGS 2038 (12-year) and SGS 2042 (16-year), and Madhya Pradesh SGS 2056 (30-year). These were priced at 100 with cut-off yields of 7.76%, 7.72%, 7.72% and 7.75%.
  • SGS yield extremes help with data questions. The lowest cut-off yield was Odisha SGS 2030 at 7.1692% (weighted average 7.1484%), followed by West Bengal SGS 2031 at 7.3203% and Uttar Pradesh SGS 2032 at 7.3505%. The highest was Sikkim SGS 2039 at 7.8302%, followed by Chhattisgarh SGS 2042 at 7.8082%. Madhya Pradesh SGS 2056 drew the largest bid amount (₹6,345 crore from 32 bids), and Tamil Nadu SGS 2036 drew the most bids (105 bids, ₹3,340 crore).
  • RBI announced a ₹16,900 crore SGS auction for Tuesday 8 Sep 2026 (payment 9 Sep) on E-Kuber. Participants are Andhra Pradesh (₹1,000 crore, ₹1,200 crore and ₹1,600 crore re-issues), Goa (₹100 crore, 10-year), Gujarat (two ₹1,000 crore re-issues) and Jammu and Kashmir (₹500 crore re-issue plus ₹500 crore 18-year). Others are Maharashtra (₹1,000 crore, ₹2,200 crore and ₹2,400 crore), Meghalaya (₹400 crore, 7-year), Punjab (₹800 crore 9-year plus ₹1,200 crore re-issue) and Rajasthan (₹1,000 crore re-issue plus ₹1,000 crore 22-year). Non-competitive bidding is up to 10% of the notified amount per stock, with a single bid capped at 1%.
  • SGS legal and technical features are worth remembering. New stock pays interest half-yearly on 9 March and 9 September, while re-issues carry the rate fixed at original issue. SGS are governed by the Government Securities Act, 2006 and Government Securities Regulations, 2007. They count as eligible Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) investments under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, and qualify for the ready forward facility.
  • A GoI buyback auction on 3 Sep 2026 accepted nothing. The aggregate notified amount was ₹30,000 crore, but total offers were only ₹3,109.305 crore. Offers were 7.33% GS 2026 (₹750.055 crore), 5.74% GS 2026 (₹440.020 crore), 8.15% GS 2026 (₹818.950 crore) and 8.24% GS 2027 (₹1,100.280 crore). Accepted amount: NIL.
  • ★ Premature redemption of Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) 2021-22 Series VI (issued 7 Sep 2021) fell due on 7 Sep 2026 at ₹15,334 per unit. Premature redemption is allowed after the fifth year from issue, on an interest payment date. The price is the simple average of the closing price of 999-purity gold for the previous three business days (2, 3 and 4 Sep 2026), as published by India Bullion and Jewellers Association Ltd (IBJA). The governing GoI notification is F.No. 4(5)-B(W&M)/2021 dated 12 May 2021.
02

Banking & Financial Sector

  • ★ RBI penalised Jai Bhawani Sahakari Bank Ltd., Pune ₹2.03 lakh on 28 Aug 2026. This is an Urban Co-operative Bank (UCB) action under Section 47A(1)(c) read with 46(4)(i) and 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, and Section 25(1)(iii) read with 23(4) of the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005. The bank failed to report borrowers' credit information to all Credit Information Companies (CICs) and did not review the risk categorisation of accounts at the prescribed periodicity. The directions involved were on CIC membership by co-operative banks and Know Your Customer (KYC).
  • ★ The Mumbai District Central Co-operative Bank Ltd., Maharashtra was penalised ₹20,000 on 25 Aug 2026. The bank failed to upload customers' KYC records to the Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR) within the prescribed timeline. The inspection was carried out by the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD), and the penalty was imposed under Section 47A(1)(c) read with 46(4)(i) and 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
  • ★ Hinduja Leyland Finance Limited, a Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC), was penalised ₹6.20 lakh on 2 Sep 2026. The default concerned Directions on Pricing of Microfinance Loans and Securitisation of Standard Assets: it had no Board-approved policy on pricing of microfinance loans and undertook activities in the nature of "Synthetic Securitisation". The penalty was imposed under Section 58G(1)(b) read with 58B(5)(aa) of the RBI Act, 1934.
  • ★ Sammaan Finserve Limited was penalised ₹4.20 lakh on 31 Aug 2026. The NBFC failed to report a borrower's credit information to CRILC (the Central Repository of Information on Large Credits), breaching the framework on Early Recognition of Stress and Reporting to CRILC. The penalty was under Section 58G(1)(b) read with 58B(5)(aa) of the RBI Act, 1934.
  • Three credit information companies were penalised on 31 Aug 2026 for the same default. They failed to credit compensation to eligible complainants within the prescribed period under the framework for compensation to customers for delayed updation/rectification of credit information. The penalties were Equifax Credit Information Services Pvt Ltd ₹1,19,400, CRIF High Mark Credit Information Services Pvt Ltd ₹6,89,600 and TransUnion CIBIL Limited ₹26,82,800. All were imposed under Section 25(1)(iii) read with 23(4) of the CIC (Regulation) Act, 2005.
  • All penalty releases carry a standard disclaimer and common features. The action rests on regulatory-compliance deficiencies and is not a pronouncement on the validity of any customer transaction. Inspections referred to the financial position as on 31 Mar 2025, and the releases were signed by Chief General Manager Brij Raj. The pattern continues RBI's steady enforcement on co-operative banks, NBFCs and CICs.
  • Bank lending and deposit rates for Aug 2026 (covering Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs) excluding Regional Rural Banks and small finance banks) showed mixed moves. The Weighted Average Lending Rate (WALR) on outstanding rupee loans was 8.97% in Jul 2026 (8.96% in Jun), while WALR on fresh rupee loans edged down to 8.52% from 8.53%. The 1-year median Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate (MCLR) rose to 8.70% in Aug 2026 from 8.60% in Jul.
  • Deposit rates (Weighted Average Domestic Term Deposit Rate, WADTDR) were stable on outstanding deposits but softer on fresh ones. WADTDR on outstanding term deposits stayed at 6.58% from Jun to Jul 2026. WADTDR on fresh term deposits fell to 5.90% in Jul from 5.99% in Jun. The gap between outstanding and fresh deposit rates shows deposit-rate cuts are still passing through slowly.
  • RBI released the Quarterly Basic Statistical Returns (BSR-2) on Deposits with SCBs for June 2026. It is published on the "Database on Indian Economy" portal (data.rbi.org.in) and covers SCBs excluding RRBs, with the reference date being the last day of the quarter. It continues the earlier release for the end-March 2026 position.
  • India Post Payments Bank (IPPB) marked its 9th Foundation Day on 1 Sep 2026. As of 31 Mar 2026 it reported 13.25 crore customers (77% rural, 49% women) and deposits of ₹29,104 crore. Digital transactions were ₹21.61 lakh crore (of which UPI ₹7.41 lakh crore). Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) reached 6.5 crore beneficiaries (₹1.55 lakh crore), and Aadhaar-enabled Payment System (AePS) transactions were 12.06 crore (₹36,140 crore). Digital life certificates numbered 29.43 lakh, Aadhaar updates 9.47 crore and Bal Aadhaar 1.35 crore.
  • IPPB launched three new platforms. These are DakPay Sound Box (for merchants), a Digital Insurance Tech Platform and a Digital Mutual Fund Platform. They extend the bank's last-mile reach into insurance and investment products.
  • NPCI International signed an agreement with Uzbekistan's NIPC on 30 Aug 2026 to enable UPI payments through UZQR for Indian travellers. NPCI International is the international arm of the National Payments Corporation of India, and UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is already accepted in 10 countries including Uzbekistan. This continues India's push to export its digital public infrastructure.
  • A high-level meeting in Mumbai on 3 Sep 2026, chaired by Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan, reviewed Self-Help Group (SHG)–bank linkages under the Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana–National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM). The targets are ₹10 lakh crore of loans for SHGs and ₹1 lakh crore for individual women entrepreneurs in the next 5 years. More than ₹13.67 lakh crore of credit has been extended to over 10 crore women organised into 92–93 lakh SHGs. Private bankers must attend District Level Consultative Committee (DLCC) meetings, a helpline will be set up, and reviews will be quarterly (virtual) and bi-annual (physical), feeding the Financial Inclusion 2.0 vision.
  • The Corporate Mitra course launched its first batch on 4 Sep 2026 with 2,879 registered learners. Offered with the Institute of Cost Accountants of India (CMA), the Institute of Company Secretaries of India (ICSI) and the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI), it runs for 12 months: 6 months of lectures and 6 months of on-the-job training. It aims to create trained paraprofessionals for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) and ease compliance.

Government Schemes & Budget

  • ★ The Monthly Review of Union Accounts for July 2026 gave receipts of ₹13,06,709 crore, 35.8% of Budget Estimates (BE) 2026-27. Tax revenue was ₹8,44,560 crore, non-tax revenue ₹4,23,013 crore and non-debt capital receipts ₹39,136 crore. Transfers to State Governments were ₹3,72,354 crore. This is the key fiscal data release for exam purposes.
  • ★ On the expenditure side, the same review showed total expenditure of ₹17,61,853 crore, 32.9% of BE 2026-27. Revenue expenditure was ₹13,11,218 crore, capital expenditure ₹4,50,635 crore, interest payments ₹4,26,566 crore and major subsidies ₹1,53,513 crore. Interest payments being close to capital expenditure is a useful quality-of-spending observation.
  • ★ The Vibrant Villages Programme (VVP) has a combined outlay of Rs 11,639 crore across two phases, targeting more than 2,616 border villages. The 2,616 is the 662 villages of VVP-I plus the 1,954 villages of VVP-II. The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) is the nodal ministry. The press release of 6 Sep 2026 notes that India's border with Bangladesh (4,096.7 km) is the longest, followed by China (3,488 km) and Pakistan (3,323 km).
  • ★ VVP-I covers the northern border with a budget of Rs 4,800 crore (FY 2022-23 to FY 2026-27), of which Rs 2,500 crore is earmarked for road connectivity. It covers 662 priority villages in 46 blocks across 19 districts of 4 states and 1 Union Territory (UT): Arunachal Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Uttarakhand and Ladakh (UT). It was launched on 10 Apr 2023 at Kibithoo, Anjaw district, Arunachal Pradesh, with a population of about 1.42 lakh. Interventions include all-weather roads, drinking water, electricity, mobile and internet, healthcare, education, tourism, and a hub-and-spoke livelihood model.
  • ★ VVP-II, for other international land borders, was approved by the Union Cabinet on 2 Apr 2025 with a budget of Rs 6,839 crore till FY 2028-29. It is Centre-funded and implemented by MHA, and was launched on 20 Feb 2026 at Nathanpur village, Cachar district, Assam. It covers 1,954 villages in 334 blocks across 15 states and 2 UTs (Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh), excluding blocks already covered under VVP-I. Convergence with schemes such as PMGSY-IV is allowed, with relaxations through a High-Powered Committee chaired by the Cabinet Secretary.
  • VVP progress as of 31 Jul 2026 shows implementation is under way. MHA has sanctioned 1,248 projects with an outlay of Rs 3,020.32 crore, and other central ministries have sanctioned 1,655 more. Rs 953.47 crore has been released to States/UTs and 283 projects are reported complete. State-wise MHA projects: Arunachal Pradesh 832, Uttarakhand 152, Himachal Pradesh 98, Sikkim 87 and Ladakh 79.
  • VVP connectivity and tourism components are significant. There are 111 road projects (outlay Rs 2,481.93 crore) that bring road access to 134 previously unconnected villages, and 35 long-span bridges are sanctioned. Tourism has 327 projects with an outlay of Rs 145 crore. More than 8,800 outreach activities were held, led by Arunachal Pradesh (2,990), Ladakh (2,221) and Uttarakhand (2,038). The programme is linked to security, as local residents first reported the 1999 Kargil intrusion on 3 May 1999.
  • The National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems (NM-ICPS) was approved by the Union Cabinet in 2018 with an outlay of ₹3,660 crore for 9 years. It has 25 Technology Innovation Hubs (TIHs) and 4 Technology Translation Research Parks (TTRPs): IIT Kanpur (Cybersecurity), IISc Bengaluru (Robotics & AI), IIT (ISM) Dhanbad (Mining) and IIT Indore (Digital Health). By Aug 2026 it had enabled 1,146 technologies and 1,329 products, funded 5,824 CHANAKYA fellowships, incubated 1,136 start-ups (24,000+ jobs) and run 200 international collaboration projects.
  • NM-ICPS also hosts the BharatGen initiative of Indian-language AI models. Param-2 has 17 billion parameters and supports 22 scheduled languages, and Shrutam supports 12 languages. Other models are Sooktam, Patram, Ayur Param, Agri Param and Legal Param.
  • ★ The Surface Coal/Lignite Gasification Scheme was approved on 13 May 2026 with a total outlay of ₹37,500 crore. The first-round bidding deadline was 7 Sep 2026, and Talcher Fertilisers and NTPC have submitted applications through the portal. It supports coal gasification as a cleaner use of domestic coal.
  • The Watershed Development Component of the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (WDC-PMKSY) covers 1,220 projects over 52.93 lakh hectares. The sanctioned cost is ₹12,404 crore (₹8,134 crore central share), with ₹6,955.75 crore released (85% of the central share). Outcomes include 1.24 lakh water harvesting structures, 3.08 lakh ha of additional protective irrigation, 28.50 lakh farmer beneficiaries, 1.45 lakh ha of afforestation/horticulture and 2.85 crore man-days of employment.
  • Fisheries schemes under the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY) featured in two events. At Agatti on 31 Aug 2026 the Vice-President released "Mission Rangeen Machhli 2031" (an ornamental fisheries plan), and PMMSY projects worth ₹62 crore were approved for Lakshadweep, whose fisheries potential exceeds 1,00,000 t (about 94,000 t tuna). On 1 Sep 2026 projects worth ₹36.49 crore were launched in Puducherry: Fish Landing Centre Nallavadu ₹18.94 crore, Puducherry Fishing Harbour upgrade ₹15.63 crore and a seaweed farming centre at Karaika ₹1.92 crore.
  • BIO-NIVESH, launched by Dr Jitendra Singh on 3 Sep 2026, is a platform bridging biotech innovation and investment. It is backed by the ₹1 lakh crore Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund, with the Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council (BIRAC) as second-level fund manager. The first edition has 20 high-potential biotech/deep-tech start-ups and about 50 investors, and follows a "Big Five in Five Years" concept for anchor companies.
  • NAMASTE (National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem) rolled out occupational-safety training. The first batch began at Durgapur Municipal Corporation on 5 Sep 2026, covering 7,630 profiled sewer and septic-tank workers across 124 municipalities. It is a 5-day, 30-hour programme of 45 workers per batch, with a certificate and ₹1,000 stipend for at least 70% attendance. Personal protective equipment (PPE) kits, ID cards and a biometric database are also provided.
  • Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan marked its 6th anniversary, having reached 34.56 crore citizens (13.71 crore youth and 10.65 crore women). 1.64 lakh Nasha Mukti Mitras have joined through the e-pledge, the helpline is 14446, and financial assistance flows under the National Action Plan for Drug Demand Reduction (NAPDDR). The 89th Fit India Sundays cycling event (Surat, 6 Sep 2026) used the theme "Nasha Mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat".
  • The first phase of Census 2027 (House Listing and Housing Census) has been completed in Tamil Nadu. It used over 1,15,000 enumerators, 19,000 supervisors and about 1,20,000 houselisting blocks, and covered 33 indicators including household assets, housing, drinking water, electricity, sanitation and digital connectivity. Self-enumeration ran 17–31 Jul 2026, and the next phase, Population Enumeration, is nationwide in Jan–Feb 2027.
  • Railways and highways infrastructure outlays reported this week. These are Bhavnagar Para yard remodelling ₹125 crore, electronic interlocking at 21 stations in Samastipur Division ₹233 crore (to support Kavach), the Adra–Joychandipahar 11 km bypass ₹272 crore, and Kavach 4.0 on 712 route km of Moradabad Division ₹170 crore. The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) is building the 160 km Rameshwar–Paradeep access-controlled greenfield coastal highway on Hybrid Annuity Mode. It costs Rs 8,300.79 crore (approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs in June 2026), with Package-I (79.4 km, 4-lane, Rameshwar–Konark) and Package-II (80.7 km, 2-lane with paved shoulder, Konark–Paradeep).
  • Ahmedabad became the third spoke airport of the civil aviation hub-and-spoke model, after Varanasi and Amritsar. Flight AI 1117 serves 22 destinations and AI 1121 serves 17, with passengers completing check-in, immigration and customs once. Estimates are 0.4 million jobs and USD 30 billion GDP till 2030, and 16 million jobs and USD 1.4 trillion by 2047.
  • The Food Secretaries Conference (1 Sep 2026, chaired by DFPD Secretary Shri Sanjeev Chopra) set procurement and Public Distribution System (PDS) targets. Paddy procurement for the Kharif Marketing Season (KMS) 2026-27 is 708.64 LMT and coarse grains 18.85 LMT. Additional storage of 131 LMT (111 LMT by states, 20 LMT by FCI) is planned, and e-KYC stood at 90.64% (30 Jul 2026). Digital DBT of Fair Price Shop dealer margins is piloted in Gujarat, Puducherry, Chandigarh and Dadra Nagar, with 10 more states to follow.
  • Special Campaign 3 resolved 2,306 of 3,193 long-pending pension grievances (72%) between 7 Jul and 31 Aug 2026. Examples include ₹37.40 lakh family pension arrears to Ms Angoori Devi (from 1 Jan 1996). It shows the government's push on pensioner grievance redressal.

Economy & Trade

  • ★ Prime Minister Narendra Modi lauded India's 7.8% GDP growth in Q1 of FY 2026-27. He called it a "herculean feat" on 31 Aug 2026 and posted "7.8% growth. Strong numbers. Even stronger confidence." on 1 Sep 2026. This is the latest headline growth figure in the week's releases.
  • ★ India's Current Account Deficit (CAD) was US$ 4.2 billion (0.5% of GDP) in Q1 (Apr–Jun) 2026-27, against US$ 3.4 billion (0.4% of GDP) a year earlier. The merchandise trade deficit widened to US$ 86.1 billion from US$ 68.9 billion. The petroleum, oil and lubricants (POL) deficit was US$ 37.6 billion against US$ 32.2 billion. Data are preliminary and were released on 1 Sep 2026 (Press Release 2026-2027/1015).
  • Services and remittances cushioned the current account. Net services receipts rose to US$ 51.6 billion from US$ 47.9 billion, helped by computer services, other business services and transportation. Net primary income outgo fell to US$ 10.5 billion from US$ 13.3 billion. Personal transfer receipts (mainly remittances) rose to US$ 42.9 billion from US$ 33.2 billion.
  • ★ Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) turned into a net outflow of US$ 9.6 billion in Q1:2026-27, against an inflow of US$ 1.6 billion. Net Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) inflow rose to US$ 6.1 billion from US$ 5.2 billion. Non-resident (NRI) deposits brought in US$ 2.8 billion (US$ 3.6 billion a year ago), and External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) to India US$ 3.3 billion (US$ 4.4 billion). On a BoP basis reserves fell by US$ 8.1 billion, against an accretion of US$ 4.5 billion.
  • ★ Forex reserves fell by US$ 22.5 billion in nominal terms in Apr–Jun 2026, against a rise of US$ 29.8 billion a year earlier. Valuation was a loss of US$ 14.4 billion (gain of US$ 25.3 billion a year ago), mainly due to a lower gold price and appreciation of the US dollar. Excluding valuation, reserves decreased by US$ 8.1 billion. The press release (2026-2027/1016) uses the old BoP format, which differs from BPM6 (the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual, 6th edition) in treating current-account transfers and ADRs/GDRs. This is why its ECB figure (US$ 1.0 billion) differs from the BoP release (US$ 3.3 billion).
  • ★ India–Brazil trade stood at USD 15.07 billion in FY 2025-26, with a target of USD 30 billion by 2030. The 8th Trade Monitoring Mechanism meeting was co-chaired by Commerce Secretary Shri Rajesh Agrawal and Brazil's MDIC representative Ms Tatiana Lacerda Prazeres. It reviewed the Terms of Reference of the India–MERCOSUR Preferential Trade Agreement. India has no signed Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with Brazil, so this is a preferential arrangement.
  • ★ India's FTA push centred on the EU deal and a USD 1 trillion export target. The India–EU FTA (signed 27 Jan 2026, per the India–Belgium statement) covers a market of 2 billion people (25% of global GDP), with tariff cancellation/reduction on more than 95% of goods. Piyush Goyal said India has negotiated 9 trade agreements in the last 4.5 years and cited exports of US$ 863 billion, needing about US$ 12 billion a month for the remaining 7 months. The Belgian joint statement put current exports at 0.9 trillion. A national workshop on 3 Sep 2026 cited 9 FTAs covering an economy of about USD 60 trillion (about two-thirds of global trade), with a target of 75% of global trade access at lower rates.
  • Goyal also announced a ₹1 lakh crore R&D Innovation Fund at the 66th SIAM annual convention. He pushed localisation, technology investment, and focus on rare earths and critical minerals for the auto sector. Sports / Environment / Science & Tech

  • The Centre for Development of Telematics (C-DOT) unveiled 14 indigenous quantum products on its 43rd Foundation Day (31 Aug 2026). They cover Quantum Key Distribution and Post-Quantum Cryptography solutions, including Q-AKSHAY CD, Q-AKSHAY MD, C-SPD, C-RD, Q-SETU, Q-MAHASETU, Q-VIKRAM, Q-AMOGH, Q-DARSHAN, Q-VAACHAN, Q-RAQSHAK, Q-VAAYU, Q-VAJRA1000 and Q-PARAKRAM.
  • ★ Semicon 2.0 notified six pillars on 4 Sep 2026. They are Design, Machines & Materials, Fabs, Assembly Test Marking and Packaging/Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (ATMP/OSAT), R&D, and Talent Development. The target of 85,000 semiconductor engineers was achieved in 4 years of an original 10-year plan, and a new target of 1 lakh additional engineers was set. Projects can run up to 6 years, the scheme is open for 3 years, and 50,000–60,000 direct jobs are expected. Students from Tier-II and Tier-III cities have designed more than 250 chips.
  • The Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle GSLV-F17 launched EOS-05, the first geosynchronous imaging satellite. PM Modi praised the partnership between the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and Indian industry. EOS stands for Earth Observation Satellite.
  • India's first geothermal wells opened at Puga Valley, Ladakh, on 30 Jun 2026 under the National Geothermal Energy Policy (2025). There are two wells of 1,000 m depth and a 1-MW demonstration project. The policy aims to integrate geothermal into the renewable mix for Net-Zero 2070 and encourages private participation, ground-source heat pumps and reuse of abandoned wells.
  • INNS Nipun, the second Diving Support Vessel of the Nistar class, was commissioned at Naval Dockyard, Mumbai on 31 Aug 2026. It was built indigenously by Hindustan Shipyard Limited, Visakhapatnam.
  • A study by CeNS, IISER Kolkata and JNCASR on peptide-based piezoelectric biomaterials reported a tiny molecular switch. Piezoelectricity was switched on with 1% co-solvent, with a coefficient of about 30 pm V⁻¹. Applications include self-powered wearables and implantable sensors as a greener alternative to ceramic piezoelectrics.
  • The World Archery Para Series ran 5–9 Sep 2026 in Ahmedabad, inaugurated by Amit Shah by video-conference. It has 108 athletes from 19 countries, and Ahmedabad is being positioned as a training hub with the 2030 Commonwealth Games centenary and a 2036 Olympic bid in view. The Indian Air Force International Squash Championship ran at Air Force Station Chandigarh and culminated on 4 Sep 2026.
  • Air quality enforcement continued in the National Capital Region (NCR). The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) reviewed 121 inspections (8–23 Aug 2026) at its 138th Enforcement Task Force meeting: 4 project closures, 17 DG set sealings, 7 compliance orders and 5 environmental compensation proposals. A separate drive in Nuh, Haryana on 3 Sep 2026 inspected 35 units and found 17 non-compliant.
  • The Ministry of Ports is working on zero-emission shipping corridors. The focus is green bunkering hubs, shore power and green fuel readiness at Deendayal, Paradip and V.O. Chidambaranar ports, with Kamarajar already equipped with shore power. The goal is for India to lead low-carbon maritime trade routes.
  • Health, telecom and cultural initiatives were also reported. The India Valve Registry was launched at India Valves 2026 (Chennai, 5 Sep 2026) to collect de-identified real-world data on transcatheter valve interventions. The Department of Telecommunications warned that IMEI tampering attracts up to 3 years' imprisonment and a fine up to ₹50 lakh under the Telecommunication Act, 2023, and advised using the Sanchar Saathi portal. The All India Institute of Ayurveda's "AyurVarta" series noted that Ayurveda Day is fixed on 23 Sep (earlier Dhanvantari Jayanti).
