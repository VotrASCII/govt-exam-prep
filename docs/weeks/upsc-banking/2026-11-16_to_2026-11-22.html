16 Nov – 22 Nov, 2026

01

Polity & Governance

  • ★ Census 2027 Phase II begins with Self-Enumeration (SE) in Goa, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh and non-snow-bound Uttarakhand. The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) opened the online window till 30 Nov 2026. House-to-house Population Enumeration follows from 1 Dec 2026 to 4 Jan 2027, with a revisional round on 5–9 Jan 2027 and reference date 00:00 hrs on 5 Jan 2027. The remaining States/UTs have SE on 10–24 Jan 2027 and enumeration on 25 Jan–28 Feb 2027, with reference date 00:00 hrs on 1 Mar 2027. Phase I (Houselisting and Housing Census) covered 35 States/UTs except Manipur. This is India's first digital Census, with SE in 16 languages on se.census.gov.in and confidentiality protected by the Census Act, 1948.
  • ★ The Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006 backgrounder reports about 54 lakh claims received and 25.42 lakh titles distributed by 31 Aug 2026. The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act is administered by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs. It came into force on 31 Dec 2007, and titles cannot be sold or transferred. Claims move from the Gram Sabha to the Sub-Divisional Level Committee and then the District Level Committee, whose decision is final and binding. Chhattisgarh leads with 5,34,068 titles, followed by Odisha and Madhya Pradesh. A National FRA Digital Platform was launched during Janjatiya Gaurav Pakhwada (1–15 Nov 2026).
  • ★ The Central Registrar of Cooperative Societies (CRCS) opened its first Regional Office at Pune. Union Home and Cooperation Minister Amit Shah visited it under the theme 'Sahkar se Samriddhi'. The CRCS is a statutory authority under the Ministry of Cooperation, appointed under Section 4(1) of the Multi-State Cooperative Societies (MSCS) Act, 2002. Pune began operations on 15 April 2026 and oversees 818 MSCS, against 1,872 registered nationwide, of which 420 were registered after the Ministry was set up on 6 July 2021. Offices at Lucknow, Chennai and Kolkata are planned next.
  • ★ The National Sports Board (NSB) became operational, with Devesh Chaturvedi (IAS Retd.) as Chairperson. He assumed office on 16 Nov 2026 along with Member Commodore (Retd.) Pushpendra Kumar Garg, both appointed by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet. The Board is set up under the National Sports Governance Act, 2025 and operationalised through the 2026 NSB Rules. It is the apex central authority for recognising National Sports Bodies.
  • ★ The Press Council of India (PCI) marked National Press Day on 16 Nov 2026 and released the Norms of Journalistic Conduct, 2026 Edition. The theme was "Journalism: Populist or Responsible". The PCI is a quasi-judicial, self-regulatory body for the print media, set up by an Act of Parliament in 1966 and re-established in 1979. The new norms cover disaster and conflict reporting, children's privacy, and advertisements for online money games in line with the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025.
  • The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) penalty on ExportersIndia.com was upheld by the National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC). Two NCDRC orders of 12 Nov 2026 dismissed the appeals of Weblink.In Pvt. Ltd. against CCPA orders imposing ₹10 lakh each (₹20 lakh in total) for listing explosives such as Ammonium Nitrate and PETN. The authority held that safe-harbour protection under Section 79 of the IT Act, 2000 depends on due diligence. It also held that the Shreya Singhal "actual knowledge" standard does not remove this duty. The National Consumer Helpline number is 1915.
  • ★ The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) took suo motu cognizance of a mother and daughter's bodies being carried in a garbage vehicle at Ballabhgarh (Faridabad). The NHRC reiterated that the right to life and dignity under Article 21 extends to the dead and cited its Advisory of 14 May 2021. Notices went to the Haryana Health Secretary and the District Magistrate, Faridabad, with reports due in two weeks. The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) separately took suo motu cognizance under Section 13(1)(j) of the CPCR Act, 2005 in a Ranchi child-assault case.
  • The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) held a workshop for Secretaries of State Public Service Commissions at Dholpur House. It followed a decision of the 25th National Conference of Chairpersons of State PSCs (Bengaluru, January 2025). UPSC Chairman Dr. Ajay Kumar cited AI-enabled face authentication, a Universal Registration Number, digitised Departmental Promotion Committee proposals and computerised randomisation of interview boards. UPSC Advertisement No. 13/2026 invites applications from 14 Nov to 4 Dec 2026.
  • Vice-President C. P. Radhakrishnan addressed the orientation programme for the 17th Odisha Legislative Assembly. He stressed Question Hour, Zero Hour and the role of the Business Advisory Committee, and welcomed the Digital House under the National e-Vidhan Application (NeVA). He recalled Vaishali and the Chola-era Kudavolai system as evidence that India is the 'Mother of Democracy'. He also urged the use of AI and Natural Language Processing in legislatures.
  • The first Commonwealth Women Parliamentarians (CWP) India Region Conference was held at Vidhana Soudha, Bengaluru. Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla inaugurated it, with 135 women legislators attending. The theme was inclusive cyberspace and mentorship for new women legislators. Birla referred to the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (2023), which provides 33% representation for women in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. The CWP India Region Chairperson is Dr. Daggubati Purandeswari.
  • Amit Shah presented the President's Colour to Arunachal Pradesh Police at Itanagar. It is the 17th police force to receive the honour, which recognises more than 15 years of distinguished service. Shah also said every FIR registered after 2027 will be adjudicated within three years under the new criminal laws, and inaugurated the 12th Battalion campus of the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) in Itanagar. He reiterated the goal of a "drug-free India" by 2029.
  • NITI Aayog launched the first of five national workshops marking 25 years of PM Modi as head of government. Titled "Leadership as a Mission" (17 Nov 2026, Bharat Mandapam), it introduced the Karmayogi Sankalpa on the iGOT Karmayogi platform, with a target of 25,000 commitments by 25 Dec 2026 (Good Governance Day). The next workshop, "Governance as Delivery", is due in January 2027. NITI Vice Chairman Ashok Kumar Lahiri outlined a USD 30–40 trillion economy by 2047.
  • The Labour Codes completed one year (in effect since 21 Nov 2025), marked by a Chintan Shivir of State Labour Ministers at Gandhinagar on 21–22 Nov 2026. Union Labour Minister Dr. Mansukh Mandaviya said social security coverage grew from 25 crore (2014) to 101 crore (2026). The launches included the National Career Service (NCS) ONE portal with an Occupational Shortage Index, an e-Shram Livelihood Profile Module and eight Career Lounges.
  • Audit Diwas (16 Nov) and Vigilance Awareness Week 2026 (theme "Probity for Prosperity") were observed. Audit Diwas commemorates Sir Edward Drummond taking charge as the first Auditor General on 16 Nov 1860, which makes the Indian Audit and Accounts Department 166 years old. The Vigilance Week followed directions of the Central Vigilance Commission.
  • Rajya Sabha and constitutional-education items. Chairman C. P. Radhakrishnan administered the oath to re-elected member Hardeep Singh Puri (Madhya Pradesh) on 17 Nov 2026. The Vice-President also inaugurated Constitution Week 2026 at Bennett University and called duties as important as rights. A Constitution Day Quiz by MyGov and MY Bharat runs 18–26 Nov, ahead of Constitution Day on 26 Nov.
  • The Digital Life Certificate (DLC) Campaign 5.0 held mega camps. The Department of Pension and Pensioners' Welfare (DoPPW) ran camps at Nagpur and in Delhi for railway pensioners, using face authentication through the Jeevan Pramaan and AadhaarFaceRD apps. At the New Delhi camp 58 DLCs were generated on the spot.
02

Economy & Finance

  • ★ Wholesale Price Index (WPI) inflation rose to 12.29% in October 2026 (base year 2022-23). September's figure was 11.39%, and the All Commodities index rose from 112.5 to 113.3. Fuel and Power inflation was 32.84%, Mineral Oils 45.57% and Crude Petroleum and Natural Gas 70.80%. Primary Articles stood at 9.24% and Manufactured Products at 9.54%. Cumulative April–October FY 2026-27 inflation is 10.14%. The November data will be released on 14 Dec 2026, since provisional WPI, Output Producer Price Index and trial Input PPI come out on the 14th of the following month.
  • ★ The Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) monthly bulletin for October 2026 shows the overall unemployment rate at 4.9%. Conducted by the National Statistics Office (NSO) under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) using the Current Weekly Status approach, it is the 19th monthly bulletin. Labour force participation rate (15+) is 55.7% and worker population ratio 53.0%. Female LFPR is 35.0%, the highest since February 2026, and urban unemployment is 6.7%. The rural female unemployment rate of 3.8% is the lowest of 2026.
  • ★ The Index of Core Industries (ICI), base 2022-23, grew 10.9% year-on-year in October 2026. September's final growth was 8.2%, and the October index stood at 123.1. Iron Ore (26.4%), Coal (23.1%) and Electricity (21.6%) led, while Fertilizers (−12.2%), Natural Gas (−7.6%) and Crude Oil (−4.1%) contracted. Cumulative April–October growth is 6.0%. Electricity carries the highest weight (30.932). The November ICI will be released on 21 Dec 2026.
  • ★ MoSPI's Data Users Conference (Mumbai, 23 Nov 2026) focused on the new GDP, CPI and IIP series. The new National Accounts series has base year 2022-23 (released 27 Feb 2026) and uses double deflation in manufacturing. The new Consumer Price Index (CPI) has base 2024 (released 12 Feb 2026), based on the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey 2023-24, with 358 items including 50 services and rural house rent for the first time. The new Index of Industrial Production (IIP) has base 2022-23 (released 1 June 2026) and adds a water supply, sewerage and waste management sector.
  • ★ The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) announced liquidity and debt operations. It will hold a 4-day Variable Rate Repo (VRR) auction of ₹50,000 crore under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility on 23 Nov 2026, with reversal on 27 Nov. Treasury Bill auctions of ₹23,000 crore (91-day ₹8,000 crore, 182-day ₹8,000 crore, 364-day ₹7,000 crore) are set for 25 Nov. State Governments will convert/switch securities worth ₹10,800 crore by auction on 26 Nov. Individual retail non-competitive allocation in T-bill auctions is capped at 5% of the notified amount.
  • ★ The RBI Weekly Statistical Supplement shows reserves and credit data as issued. Total foreign exchange reserves were US$ 705,342 million on 13 Nov 2026, a weekly fall of US$ 14,205 million. Scheduled commercial banks' aggregate deposits stood at ₹2,82,51,076 crore on 31 Oct 2026, with bank credit at ₹2,31,82,499 crore. Year-on-year growth is 16.9% for deposits and 19.5% for credit, and broad money (M3) grew 16.5%. Since 15 Dec 2025 a banking "fortnight" means the 15th and last day of the month, under the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025.
  • ★ The RBI penalised Fusion Finance Limited ₹7.50 lakh by an order dated 17 Nov 2026. The action is under Section 58G(1)(b) read with Section 58B(5)(aa) of the RBI Act, 1934, for non-compliance with directions on Know Your Customer (KYC) and Internal Ombudsman. The lapses were failure to obtain PAN/Form 60 and failure to escalate rejected complaints to the Internal Ombudsman. The RBI stressed that the action is regulatory and does not pronounce on the validity of any customer transaction.
  • ★ Public sector banks (PSBs) posted record H1 FY2026-27 results (as on 30.09.2026). Aggregate net profit was ₹1,09,540 crore, up 16.9%, and aggregate business crossed ₹300 lakh crore for the first time (₹301.6 lakh crore). Gross NPA is 1.69% and net NPA 0.33%. Capital to Risk-weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR) is 16.89% and return on assets 1.11%. A High-Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat has held two sittings and consulted banks, NBFCs, regulators and fintechs.
  • ★ The Export Promotion Mission (EPM) guidelines were amended. The EPM was approved by the Cabinet on 12 Nov 2025 with an outlay of ₹25,060 crore for FY 2025-26 to FY 2030-31, with two sub-schemes, Niryat Protsahan and Niryat Disha. Under Niryat Disha, the Market Access Support ceiling for airfare reimbursement rose from ₹75 lakh to ₹50 crore, and the Export Promotion Council membership period was cut from 6 to 3 months. Eligible certificates under TRACE rose from 462 to 600, and LIFT's minimum distance criterion fell from 200 km to 100 km.
  • India–Egypt Joint Trade Committee (JTC) met for the first time at ministerial level. The 7th JTC was held at Egypt's New Administrative Capital and co-chaired by Piyush Goyal and Dr. Mohamed Farid Saleh. The sides agreed a Joint Working Group to explore Terms of Reference for a Preferential Trade Agreement or Free Trade Agreement. The trade target is USD 12 billion, against USD 6.5 billion in FY 2025-26 (+35%) and USD 5.04 billion in April–September 2026 (+65%). The next JTC will be in New Delhi in 2028 or earlier.
  • The 14th India–Thailand JTC (Nonthaburi, 16 Nov 2026) reviewed trade of USD 21.49 billion in FY 2025-26, up 12.6%. Agenda items included the ASEAN–India Trade in Goods Agreement (AITIGA) review, the Early Harvest Scheme, and linkage of India's UPI with Thailand's PromptPay. The RBI and the Bank of Thailand will cooperate on FinTech and local-currency settlement.
  • India's exports to Free Trade Agreement (FTA) partners and regions grew strongly in April–September 2026-27. Exports to ASEAN rose 58% to US$ 28.06 billion, to the EU 5% to US$ 38.79 billion, and to Japan 40%. By region, Africa grew 44% (US$ 31.42 billion), Asia 21%, the Americas 13% and Europe 8%. Electronics exports rose 44.0% to US$ 31.97 billion, with the smartphone share falling from 60.3% to 56.8%.
  • The Horticulture Third Advance Estimates 2025-26 put production at 3,869.15 lakh tonnes, up 4.36%. Fruits reached 1,230.66 lakh tonnes and vegetables 2,272.68 lakh tonnes. Potato rose to 655.70 lakh tonnes, while total area slipped to about 297.21 lakh hectares from 301.36 lakh hectares.
  • The 16th round of commercial coal mine auctions drew 85 bids for 18 of 26 blocks across eight states. Bids rose four-fold over the previous round, and 20 first-time bidders entered. Since 2020, 145 mines have been auctioned over 15 rounds, and the framework allows 100% FDI through the automatic route. Evaluation by a Technical Evaluation Committee precedes an e-auction on the MSTC portal.
  • Shipbuilding and industrial push. The India Shipbuilding Investment Conclave (16 Nov 2026) set targets of a top-10 shipbuilding nation by 2030 and top-5 by 2047, with about US$ 10 billion projected in five coastal clusters (Dugarajapatnam, Kuchhadi, Dighi, Kendrapada, Thoothukudi). The National Industrial Corridor Development Corporation (NICDC) showcased Krishnapatnam, Orvakal and Kopparthy nodes in Andhra Pradesh (7,223 acres, ₹31,360 crore potential) at the CII Partnership Summit, Visakhapatnam.
  • The Telecom Manufacturing Zone at Gwalior was described as Asia's first integrated telecom manufacturing cluster. The Centre is funding Phase-I at ₹493 crore, and Madhya Pradesh gave about 170 acres free, with the SPV held 51% by Madhya Pradesh and 49% by the Department of Telecommunications. Separately, NHAI's Multi-Lane Free Flow tolling went live at Chennasamudram on NH-48 (Tamil Nadu) on 21 Nov 2026.
  • MSME and trade-fair items. At the 45th India International Trade Fair (13–27 Nov 2026, Bharat Mandapam, theme 'Vikas aur Virasat'), the CBDT Taxpayers' Lounge showcased the new Income-tax Act, 2025 and the CBIC pavilion showcased Next-Gen GST reforms. The MSME Minister said registrations crossed 10 crore, and the MSME ministry marked Women Entrepreneurship Day on 19 Nov. The Unclaimed-dividend camp 'Niveshak Shivir' by the Investor Education and Protection Fund Authority (IEPFA) with SEBI was held on 17 Nov in New Delhi.

International Relations

  • ★ PM Modi attended the 23rd ASEAN–India Summit and 21st East Asia Summit in Manila on 17 Nov 2026. He was invited by Philippine President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. India–ASEAN ties are a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership and sit at the heart of the Act East Policy and the MAHASAGAR vision. The East Asia Summit is a platform for Indo-Pacific peace and stability. The PM also paid obeisance at the Khalsa Diwan Gurdwara in Manila.
  • ★ The 4th India–Japan Foreign and Defence Ministers' (2+2) Dialogue was held in Tokyo on 19 Nov 2026. Rajnath Singh and Dr. S. Jaishankar met Japan's Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi, reaffirming a free, open and rule-based Indo-Pacific. India and Japan share a Special Strategic and Global Partnership. Singh also toured the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force base at Yokosuka, and the Singh–Koizumi bilateral was their third meeting this year.
  • ★ India hosted the first SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum at Purana Qila, New Delhi (20–22 Nov 2026). The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) completed 25 years, and PM Modi inaugurated the forum. It drew 116 delegates from 22 countries, with the Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts (IGNCA) as Knowledge Partner. It concluded with the Indraprastha Communiqué, and the PM cited the Panchatantra, the Ramayana's Cambodian form (Reamker) and Nalanda as shared heritage.
  • ★ The BRICS Women Leaders Summit (21–23 Nov 2026) was inaugurated by Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla at Banasthali Vidyapith, Tonk (Rajasthan). Birla called BRICS "not a challenge to any country" and urged a move from 'Women Empowerment' to 'Women-led Development'. The agenda covered political participation, digital security and ethical AI.
  • The 23rd India–Italy Joint Commission for Economic Cooperation (JCEC) met in Rome on 18 Nov 2026. Piyush Goyal co-chaired it with Italy's Deputy PM Antonio Tajani. Ties were elevated to a Special Strategic Partnership during PM Modi's visit on 19–20 May 2026, and the Joint Strategic Action Plan 2025–29 targets €20 billion of trade by 2029. Italy is India's 4th-largest trading partner in the EU, and the 22nd JCEC was at Brescia in June 2025.
  • The 12th India–Belarus Inter-Governmental Commission (New Delhi, 16 Nov 2026) discussed a Roadmap for Cooperation 2027–2031. It was co-chaired by MoS Jitin Prasada and Belarus Foreign Minister Maxim Ryzhenkov, and the 13th session will be in 2027. CSIR and the National Academy of Sciences of Belarus (NASB) also signed a Second Programme of Cooperation, with a Second Joint Call for proposals proposed from 27 Nov 2026.
  • The India–Canada Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) talks reached their sixth round (New Delhi, 11–20 Nov 2026). Chief negotiators were Dr. Brij Mohan Mishra and Mary-Catherine Speirs, covering goods, services, rules of origin, trade remedies, IP and investment promotion. Goyal met Canada's trade minister Maninder Sidhu on 11 Nov.
  • India–Oman maritime ties featured at Oman's National Day reception. Sarbananda Sonowal recalled about 5,000 years of shared maritime heritage and INSV Kaundinya's January 2026 voyage from Porbandar to Muscat. The ship is inspired by a 5th-century vessel depicted in the Ajanta caves. The India–Oman CEPA entered into force in June 2026, and PM Modi visited Oman in December 2025.
  • The 8th India–Myanmar Naval Exercise (IMNEX-26) concluded at Visakhapatnam on 14 Nov 2026. INS Rana and INS Sumedha trained with Myanmar's UMS King Sin Phyu Shin in harbour and Bay of Bengal sea phases. It reflects MAHASAGAR (Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions) and the Neighbourhood First policy.
  • ★ India's national statement at UNFCCC COP31, Antalya (Türkiye), stressed equity and climate justice. Bhupender Yadav invoked Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC) and called equitable access to the remaining carbon budget "non-negotiable". He warned against unilateral trade measures and wants the Mitigation Work Programme to stay non-prescriptive. India supports a two-tier, Party-guided Just Transition Mechanism under the UAE Just Transition Work Programme, without uniform sectoral pathways. At the Second Global Stocktake event he asked why the world remains at risk of crossing 1.5°C and stressed finance and technology-transfer gaps.
  • Bulgaria's Foreign Minister Velislava Petrova-Chamova called on President Murmu on 20 Nov 2026. It was the first visit by a Bulgarian Foreign Minister in 17 years. Bulgaria backs UN Security Council reform, and the India–EU FTA implementation was mentioned.
  • The Global South outreach continued through the ICAR–RIS MoU. The Indian Council of Agricultural Research and the Research and Information System for Developing Countries signed it on DAKSHIN Day 2026 (17 Nov), in a Global South policy dialogue held with the Ministry of External Affairs. Separately, the Defence Headquarters Training Institute launched its first Global Leadership Programme for Fiji's Ministry of Defence in Suva (16–27 Nov 2026).
  • The International Coconut Community (ICC) held its 62nd Session and Ministerial Meeting in Kochi (17–20 Nov 2026). The ICC, headquartered in Jakarta, was established in 1969 under UN-ESCAP and is an inter-governmental body of coconut-producing nations. India's host was the Coconut Development Board, and MoS Ram Nath Thakur inaugurated the session.
  • Operation Monsoon Thunder was a joint action of India's Narcotics Control Bureau and the US Drug Enforcement Administration. It led to six arrests across four Indian cities and the identification of about 3,000 parcels carrying nearly 5 lakh tablets. Amit Shah and US Ambassador Sergio Gor praised the operation.

Environment & Ecology

  • ★ The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) revoked the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) Stage III in Delhi-NCR on 17 Nov 2026. The order moves the region to Stage II, withdrawing the 6 Nov 2026 order that had invoked Stage III. Delhi's AQI was 204, with 'Poor/Very Poor' forecast. The 30 Oct 2026 curbs on dust-generating demolition and construction remain in force.
  • ★ The India–Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM) advanced at COP31. It rests on a Memorandum of Cooperation signed on 7 Aug 2025 under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement and supports India's NDC and Long-Term Low Emission Development Strategy (LT-LEDS). The Joint Committee adopted Rules of Implementation on 8 June 2026, and the Operational Manual was launched on 30 Sept 2026.
  • ★ LeadIT 3.0 (Leadership Group for Industry Transition) was launched at COP31 on 17 Nov 2026, covering 2027–2030. India and Sweden launched LeadIT in 2019, and it now has 20 countries and 33 companies, with Brazil the newest country member. Phase 2 facilitated 11 pre-pilot projects in steel and cement, and core sectors are steel, cement and transport.
  • ★ The India–SIDS Partnership for Resilient Coasts and Oceans was launched at COP31. Organised by TERI and the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) with the International Solar Alliance, it cited India's coastline of about 11,099 km. Initiatives named include MISHTI (Mangrove Initiative for Shoreline Habitats and Tangible Incomes), CDRI, IRIS (Infrastructure for Resilient Island States) and FIPIC.
  • ★ A COP31 side event highlighted big cats as climate-mitigation allies. The International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA) covers seven big cat species. India has 58 Tiger Reserves covering about 85,000 sq km, and its tiger population was 3,682 in 2022, over 70% of the world's wild tigers. Project Tiger rests on the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 and the statutory National Tiger Conservation Authority.
  • ★ India reported strong climate numbers at COP31. Emissions intensity fell 37.38% (2005–2022), non-fossil installed capacity reached 50% five years ahead of target, and the additional forest carbon sink is about 2.44 billion tonnes CO₂e. Non-fossil capacity stood at 308.03 GW on 30 Sep 2026 (299.25 GW renewable and 8.78 GW nuclear). This continues the Panchamrit pledges of COP26 (500 GW non-fossil by 2030, net-zero by 2070).
  • ★ The National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) launched Phase III of its Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) end-to-end portal on 16 Nov 2026. A single application with one fee now reaches multiple State Biodiversity Boards and UT Biodiversity Councils. It works under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (amended 2023), Section 7, and supports Targets 13 and 19 of NBSAP 2024–2030.
  • ★ Glaw Lake in Arunachal Pradesh is India's 101st Ramsar site and the State's first. Amit Shah cited it during his Itanagar visit. Arunachal's Frontier Highway (1,500 km, ₹44,000 crore) and the 2,880 MW Dibang Hydropower Project were also highlighted.
  • Solar growth backgrounder. India's installed solar capacity grew from 2.82 GW (2014) to about 175 GW by 31 Oct 2026. The PM Surya Sarovar Yojana (approved 31 July 2026) supports 5,000 MW of floating solar at ₹5,070 crore. PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana (launched 13 Feb 2024, ₹75,021 crore) has solarised about 65 lakh households, and PM-KUSUM dates from 2019. The Bhadla Solar Park in Rajasthan (2,245 MW) is the best-known park.
  • Wind and small hydro. Wind capacity is 59.20 GW (about 19.8% of renewables), with India 4th globally. Targets are 100 GW by 2030 and 156 GW by 2036, and ₹6,853 crore of Viability Gap Funding supports 1,000 MW offshore wind off Gujarat and Tamil Nadu. Small Hydro Power (up to 25 MW) has 21,134 MW potential, of which about 5,182 MW is harnessed, and a ₹2,584.60 crore scheme for FY 2026-27 to 2030-31 targets about 1,500 MW.
  • Parvat Manthan Conclave 2026 (Dehradun, 17–18 Nov) focused on Himalayan cities. Convened by the National Institute of Urban Affairs for 11 Himalayan states, it launched the Himalayan Urban Bio-Sheds (HUBS) initiative with IUCN and the Haridwar carrying-capacity assessment. The National Mission on Himalayan Studies has supported over 255 projects.
  • Sanitation and seed-conservation items. The Swachh Shauchalay – Garimamay Bhavishya campaign (19 Nov–10 Dec 2026) under the Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) coincides with World Toilet Day, and ODF Plus (Model) status covers about 89.9% of villages. DIVERSIFARM-India mapped 652 Community Seed Banks (at least 1,000 exist), with a 10-point Odisha roadmap tied to the PPVFR Act and Biological Diversity Act.

Science & Technology

  • ★ The Atomic Energy Regulatory Board (AERB) permitted First Approach to Criticality of RAPP Unit-8 on 19 Nov 2026. It is the fourth unit of India's indigenous 700 MWe Pressurised Heavy Water Reactor series, after Kakrapar Units 3 and 4 and RAPP-7. The unit is at the Rawatbhata site in Chittorgarh (Rajasthan), which now has eight units.
  • ★ India's first indigenous H5N1 (avian influenza) chicken vaccine was dedicated to the nation at Bhopal on 20 Nov 2026. Developed by ICAR-National Institute of High Security Animal Diseases (NIHSAD), it is priced at ₹1–2 per dose, and a ₹300-crore animal disease research laboratory was announced. India's first bird flu outbreak was in 2006 at Navapur (Maharashtra). Annual livestock disease losses were cited at about ₹56,000 crore, led by Foot and Mouth Disease (₹22,000 crore).
  • ★ The space sector reforms were highlighted. PM Modi shared former ISRO Chairman S. Somanath's article on the Indian Space Policy 2023, which opened the sector to private players, and on the roadmap to the Bharatiya Antariksh Station. At IIT Kharagpur he cited Skyroot Aerospace, founded by an alumnus, as launching India's first private rocket. He also said India has begun exporting semiconductor chips under the Semiconductor Mission.
  • ★ The 4th Annual General Meeting of the Biotechnology Research and Innovation Council (BRIC) chaired by Dr. Jitendra Singh set bioeconomy goals. The targets are USD 300 billion by 2030 and USD 1 trillion by 2047, from about USD 195 billion now. BRIC ranked 1st in health sciences and 2nd in biological sciences among government institutions in the Nature Index India.
  • ★ The 12th India International Science Festival (IISF) 2026 will be held in Pune on 19–22 Dec 2026. The theme is "India's Science, World's Future", and it is organised by the Department of Biotechnology (DBT) with Vijnana Bharati. It is the public-facing festival, paired with the Emerging Science, Technology and Innovation Conclave (ESTIC), and the two replace scattered events including the erstwhile Indian Science Congress. Nobel Laureate Sir Tim Hunt will deliver a lecture.
  • Prof. Shekhar C. Mande, President of the Indian National Science Academy (INSA), was appointed to the UN Group of Ten High-level Representatives (2026–2028). The group is part of the UN Technology Facilitation Mechanism for science, technology and innovation for the SDGs. He is a former Director General of the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR).
  • The Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM), Pune marked its 65th Foundation Day. Operating since 1962, it is developing the next-generation Earth System Model IITM-ESM v3 for CMIP7 and has a climate observatory in the Maldives. It published 302 research papers and holds about 3 petabytes of data.
  • CSIR laboratory announcements. Geography & Places in News

  • ★ The Kutch Earthquake (26 Jan 2001, magnitude 6.9) backgrounder was released. It hit 7,633 villages in 181 talukas and caused 13,805 deaths. The Gujarat State Disaster Management Authority (GSDMA) was constituted on 8 Feb 2001, modelled on the Orissa SDMA, and became statutory through the Gujarat State Disaster Management Act, 2003, before the national Disaster Management Act, 2005. Reconstruction used an owner-driven approach, and the Bhuj District Civil Hospital uses base-isolation technology.
  • ★ A pollen study at Chakradhara Wetland, Bandhavgarh Tiger Reserve (Umaria, Madhya Pradesh), reconstructed Indian Summer Monsoon variability. The Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (a DST institute) used a sediment core and radiocarbon dating over about 2,700 years. The reserve lies in India's Core Monsoon Zone, and Sal-dominated moist deciduous forest marked the strong-monsoon phase. Findings appeared in Quaternary Research.
  • PM Modi referred to the West Asia war and the Strait of Hormuz crisis disrupting gas supplies. The strait links the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and is a key route for energy trade. The government had targeted 1 lakh piped natural gas connections a day, but actual numbers were 15,000–23,000 because of a plumber shortage.
  • NH-66 and the Canacona Bypass in South Goa featured in a highways update. NH-66 runs about 1,600 km from Panvel (Maharashtra) to Kanyakumari (Tamil Nadu) through five States and connects with NH-44 at Kanyakumari. The Canacona Bypass is 7.74 km and four-lane, crossing the Galgibag, Talpona and Maxem bridges.
  • Bihar's 'Nitish Setu' was dedicated by PM Modi at Vaishali on 21 Nov 2026 as part of ₹34,470 crore of projects. It is an extradosed cable-stayed bridge over the Ganga (about 19.7 km, ₹4,280 crore) linking Kachhi Dargah (Patna) with Bidupur (Vaishali). Other projects include Unit 2 (660 MW) of the Buxar Thermal Power Project and floating solar at Phulwaria Dam, Nawada.
  • Arunachal Pradesh's border geography and infrastructure were reviewed. Its international border is about 1,863 km (with China, Myanmar and Bhutan). Infrastructure cited includes the 12-km Sela Tunnel at 13,000 ft, the Bogibeel rail-cum-road bridge (2018) linking Dhemaji/Pasighat with Dibrugarh, and the Siang Upper Multipurpose Project. Arunachal is India's largest kiwi producer.
  • Industrial and port geography. Andhra Pradesh's Krishnapatnam, Orvakal and Kopparthy nodes and the five proposed shipbuilding clusters (Dugarajapatnam, Kuchhadi, Dighi, Kendrapada, Thoothukudi) dominated economic-geography news. Chennai Port handled a record 2.03 lakh tonnes of rice exports to Africa (April–18 Nov 2026), and Egypt's Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZONE) was pitched to Indian investors.
  • Water and wind geography. A national conference on Integrated River, Reservoir and Dam Systems at CWPRS Pune (19–20 Nov) reviewed the Kalpasar Project. Wind potential at 150 m is highest in Rajasthan (284.2 GW), Gujarat (180.8 GW) and Maharashtra (173.9 GW), and the Himalaya was called the "water tower of Asia".
