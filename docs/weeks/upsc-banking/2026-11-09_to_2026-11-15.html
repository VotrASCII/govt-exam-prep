01

Polity & Governance

  • ★ Special Campaign 6.0 (Special Campaign for Disposal of Pending Matters, SCDPM 6) concluded on 31 October 2026. The implementation phase ran from 2 to 31 October 2026, after a preparatory phase that began in mid-September. Its twin aims were institutionalising Swachhata (cleanliness) and reducing pendency in government offices. This year's focus area was "Effective Collection, Segregation and Disposal of E-waste", governed by the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022. Ministries report on the Special Campaign Portal hosted by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG).
  • The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) reported one of the largest tallies in Special Campaign 6. Swachhata sites grew from 6,757 to 7,963. It disposed of 147 Member of Parliament (MP) references, 141 State Government references, 11,902 public grievances and 1,666 appeals. It reviewed 2,19,644 physical and 30,385 electronic files and freed 78,125 sq ft. Scrap revenue exceeded ₹10.17 crore, of which ₹2.63 crore came from e-waste, and a one-day intensive drive was held on 13 October 2026.
  • Indian Railways and the Ministry of Power posted large campaign numbers. Railways ran over 1 lakh cleanliness drives and earned scrap revenue of ₹628.49 crore against a ₹600 crore target. It freed 6,20,121 sq ft and disposed of over 1.50 lakh e-waste units for ₹10.43 crore. Rail Jan Samvad was held at 5,884 railway stations on 22 October 2026. The Ministry of Power ran 342 drives, freed 2,51,757 sq ft and earned ₹15,74,20,231 (₹15.74 crore). It reviewed 26,885 physical files (13,282 weeded out) and 34,572 e-files (34,525 closed).
  • Other departments also reported results under Special Campaign 6. The Department of Military Affairs (DMA) covered 8,507 campaign sites, simplified 324 rules or processes, and earned about ₹89.68 crore from scrap and e-waste. It reviewed 5,28,443 files and freed 3,53,291 sq ft. The Department of Legal Affairs earned ₹4,71,881 and has uploaded Implementation Reports for 7 of its 14 parliamentary assurances on the OAMS Portal of the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs. The Department of Defence Production earned ₹22.19 crore against ₹15.62 crore in Special Campaign 5.0. The Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation (DDWS) disposed of 576 public grievances and all 29 appeals.
  • ★ NITI Aayog launched a series of five National Workshops (Nov 2026–Oct 2027) on lessons from 25 years of PM Narendra Modi as head of government. The themes run in order: "Leadership as Mission" (Nov 2026), "Governance as Delivery" (Jan 2027), "Development as Inclusion" (Apr 2027), "Civilisational Identity" (Jul 2027) and "India as a Global Force" (Sept 2027). The first workshop is on 17 Nov 2026 at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, with welcome by CEO Anurag Jain and a keynote by Dr P.K. Mishra, Principal Secretary to the PM. Cabinet Secretary Dr T.V. Somanathan will launch the Karmayogi Sankalpa on the iGOT Karmayogi platform. The PM became Gujarat Chief Minister on 7 Oct 2001 and Prime Minister in May 2014, completing 25 years on 7 Oct 2026.
  • ★ The National Commission for Allied and Healthcare Professions (NCAHP) proposed Draft Provisional Registration of Allied and Healthcare Professionals Regulations, 2026. They are framed under the NCAHP Act, 2021, a statutory regulator for allied health workers. Provisionally registered professionals would get a Provisional Registration Unique Identification Number (PR UID) through an online process. The draft also covers bridge courses, supervised clinical training and transition to the Central or State Register. Comments are due by 4 December 2026.
  • The MHA rationalised India's visa regime for the second time in a year. On 27 August 2026 main visa categories were cut from 13 to 9 and sub-categories from 49 to 23, after an October 2025 round that took them from 22 to 13 and from 63 to 49. Form fields were reduced by 65–70%. The e-Visa runs under 13 sub-categories for nationals of 172 countries. V-1 Visitor Visas are for 30 days with multiple entries, and V-2 runs for 1, 5 or 10 years. M-1 and M-3 Medical Visas (non-AYUSH and AYUSH treatment) are for one year, with M-2 and M-4 for attendants, tied to the "Heal in India" vision. B-1 covers long-term business and B-2 short-term contract-based business.
  • ★ Union Home Minister Amit Shah set 31 December 2029 as the deadline for a Drug-free India, to follow a "Naxal-free India". Speaking in Ahmedabad, he said Gujarat should become drug-free a year earlier, before 31 Dec 2028. Helpline 1033 takes confidential reports of drug sale or consumption. A control room becomes operational from 1 December and is to act within 3 hours of receiving information. The visit also covered works worth about ₹1,400 crore, including the Ramapir No Tekro slum rehabilitation project, which will serve over 55,000 people.
  • ★ The 9th Apex Level Meeting of NCORD (the narcotics coordination mechanism) directed a three-year action plan and a three-tier mechanism for drug demand reduction. The State/UT Steering and Monitoring Committee is chaired by the Chief Secretary and meets quarterly. The District Drug Demand Reduction Committee is chaired by the District Collector, meets monthly and makes mandatory quarterly inspections. The Block Drug Demand Reduction Committee is chaired by the Block Development Officer and meets and inspects monthly. On the enforcement side, the Central Bureau of Narcotics (CBN) dismantled a mephedrone lab in Tajopur Industrial Area, Mau, Uttar Pradesh on 8 Nov 2026 under Operation Vajra 3.0, seizing 11.7 kg of mephedrone and making 2 arrests under the NDPS Act, 1985.
  • The Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) opened two more Aadhaar Seva Kendras (ASKs) in Tamil Nadu. Tenkasi (6 Nov 2026) is managed by BLS International and Tiruppur (4 Nov 2026) by Protean eGov Technologies. Each has 4 operational kits. The state now has 27 ASKs, with 3 more planned for a total of 30. This is part of the e-governance push to widen enrolment, update and grievance services.
  • The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) debarred a toll-collection agency over user misbehaviour. M/s Riddhi Siddhi Associates was debarred for one month from NHAI tenders and fined ₹15 lakh after misbehaviour at Bijwasan Fee Plaza on NH-248BB on 5 Nov 2026. President Droupadi Murmu told IPS probationers (78 RR, 2025 batch) to reorient policing towards cyber crime and flagged deep-fakes and orchestrated disinformation. Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla met the same batch at Parliament House and stressed technology, public trust and impartiality. Vice-President C. P. Radhakrishnan visited Thoothukudi on 11 Nov 2026 and goes to Odisha on 16 Nov 2026 for an orientation programme for MLAs and to inaugurate a tower at Sri Sri University, Cuttack.

  • ★ The 8th Pay Commission has until May 2027 to submit its recommendations. Employee unions have proposed fitment factors of 3.61, 3.833 and 4.38, against 2.57 used by the 7th Pay Commission. 02

Economy & Finance

  • ★ India's Balance of Payments (BoP) for September 2026 showed a wider current account deficit but an overall surplus. The current account deficit was US$7.4 billion, against US$5.2 billion in September 2025, and US$25.8 billion for April–September 2026 against US$18.0 billion a year earlier. Merchandise exports were US$45.3 billion and imports US$75.3 billion, so the merchandise trade deficit narrowed to US$30.0 billion from US$33.4 billion. Net services were +US$19.2 billion. The capital account was +US$16.2 billion, including banking capital of US$22.2 billion, while net Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) was –US$6.7 billion. The overall balance was a US$8.8 billion surplus.
  • ★ The auction of 6.94% GS 2036 (maturing 11 May 2036) on 13 Nov 2026 was fully taken up. The notified amount was ₹34,000 crore. The cut-off price of ₹97.43 gave a yield of 7.3203%, and the weighted average price of ₹97.46 gave a yield of 7.3158%. Competitive bids of ₹73,016.600 crore (349 bids) were received, with partial allotment of 18.3591% at the cut-off. Devolvement on Primary Dealers was NIL. In the underwriting auction the Additional Competitive Underwriting (ACU) commission cut-off was 0.54 paise per ₹100.
  • ★ State Government Securities (SGS) auctions raised ₹27,083.882 crore on 9 Nov 2026 against ₹29,050 crore notified. Cut-offs ran from 7.61% (Haryana, 4-year) to about 8.14%. Bihar accepted only ₹1,533.882 crore in its 18-year security, and Chhattisgarh accepted nothing. Ten states (Andhra Pradesh, Goa, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Punjab, Rajasthan, Telangana, Tripura and West Bengal) will auction ₹23,300 crore on 17 Nov 2026, with payment on 18 Nov. SGS count towards the Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
  • Treasury Bill auctions show a rising yield curve. On 11 Nov 2026 cut-off yields were 5.8681% (91-day, price ₹98.5581), 6.5183% (182-day) and 6.6394% (364-day). The 18 Nov auction is for ₹23,000 crore (₹8,000 crore, ₹8,000 crore and ₹7,000 crore) with settlement on 19 Nov. Retail non-competitive allotment is capped at 5% of the notified amount, and the auction uses the multiple-price method.
  • ★ The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) kept absorbing surplus liquidity through variable-rate operations. The 4-day Variable Rate Repo (VRR) on 9 Nov allotted ₹50,003 crore at a cut-off of 5.51%. The overnight Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) on 12 Nov accepted ₹50,012 crore at a 5.49% cut-off. The 3-day VRRR on 13 Nov (notified ₹75,000 crore) drew offers of only ₹38,592 crore, all accepted at 5.49%. On 2–8 Nov the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) absorbed ₹1.36–2.02 lakh crore daily.
  • The government announced a ₹25,000 crore switch of securities and the final redemption of a Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) tranche. The switch auction on 16 Nov swaps source securities such as 7.38% GS 2027 for destination securities such as 7.18% GS 2037, at multiple prices, with settlement on 17 Nov. SGB 2018-19 Series-III (issued 13 Nov 2018) was redeemed on 13 Nov 2026 after eight years at ₹14,024 per unit. This is the simple average of the IBJA closing price of 999-purity gold over the previous three business days. SGBs pay 2.5% interest on the issue price.
  • ★ RBI's weekly and banking data for late October and early November show strong credit growth. Total forex reserves were US$719.547 billion (₹67,58,986 crore) on 6 Nov 2026, up US$2,490 million on the week. Gold was US$104.796 billion and foreign currency assets US$591.350 billion. As on 31 Oct 2026, aggregate deposits of scheduled commercial banks were ₹2,82,51,076 crore (up 16.9% year-on-year) and bank credit ₹2,31,82,499 crore (up 19.5%). Broad money (M3) was ₹3,37,68,416 crore, up 16.5% year-on-year.
  • RBI took supervisory and regulatory actions on several entities. It fined One Mobikwik Systems Limited ₹9.00 lakh under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 for Prepaid Payment Instrument (PPI) violations and delayed fraud reporting. It extended the supersession of the Abhyudaya Co-operative Bank board by one more year from 24 Nov 2026, with Satya Prakash Pathak continuing as Administrator. It cancelled the Certificate of Registration of Gandhinagar Leasing and Finance Ltd under Section 45-IA(6) of the RBI Act. Separately, Paytm got in-principle RBI approval for a wallet (PPI) licence.
  • ★ Food inflation and weather risks dominate monetary-policy talk. Reports flag uneven monsoons and El Niño as upside risks to inflation, with ICICI Bank expecting a 25 basis point (bps) hike in December and Axis Mutual Fund expecting a further 50–75 bps. Chief Economic Adviser V. Anantha Nageswaran said supply shocks weaken monetary policy and fiscal policy must do more. The inflation-targeting benchmark remains Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation at 4% with a 2–6% band. October retail inflation was 6.09% nationally, with onion at 116% and garlic at 61%. Tamil Nadu imposed stock limits (wholesalers 2,000 MT of rice, retailers 8 tonnes) to curb hoarding.
  • ★ October 2026 trade data showed record exports but a wide trade deficit. The merchandise trade deficit hit a one-year high of US$39.88 billion. Gold imports fell 62.57% to US$5.51 billion, and April–October gold imports fell 39.9% to US$24.78 billion. Five sectors (engineering goods, petroleum products, electronics, gems and jewellery, and chemicals) drove 83% of the export surge, rising 59.4% to US$34.37 billion. Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal cited about US$90 billion of October exports and said about US$480 billion was achieved in April–September towards the US$1 trillion FY27 target.
  • ★ Direct tax collections are on track but face a demanding second half. Net direct tax collections rose 11.76% to ₹14.42 lakh crore between 1 April and 10 Nov 2026, with gross collections at ₹17.50 lakh crore (up 14.02%). Corporate tax rose about 17.84% to ₹6.32 lakh crore. This is about 53% of the budgeted ₹26.97 lakh crore. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) upgraded India's potential growth to 6.8%, and the Budget 2027-28 may extend support for 100 products identified by DPIIT.
  • ★ The Union Cabinet continued Stand-Up India and the Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU) up to 31.03.2031. Stand-Up India (launched April 2016) targets Women, SC and ST entrepreneurs, with a credit guarantee corpus of ₹4,111 crore, 85% guarantee coverage and loans above ₹10 lakh up to ₹1 crore. CGFMU (launched January 2016) has a corpus of ₹5,000 crore and should facilitate PM Mudra Yojana (PMMY) loans of ₹12.6 lakh crore, with the guarantee fee cut from 1% to 0.75% (unchanged for SHGs). PMMY has sanctioned over 60.36 crore loans worth ₹43.34 lakh crore as of September 2026.
  • ★ Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS) rates for Rabi 2026-27 were approved with a budget of about ₹62,370.02 crore. This is ₹24,417.73 crore more than Rabi 2025-26 (₹37,952.29 crore) and covers 28 Phosphatic and Potassic (P&K) grades including DAP from 01.10.2026 to 31.03.2027. NBS has been in force since 01.04.2010 and reflects international prices of Urea, DAP, MOP and Sulphur. Fertiliser stocks are reported comfortable for rabi sowing, with annual fertiliser subsidy spending above ₹2.17 trillion.
  • ★ National Entrepreneurship Day (9 Nov) highlighted Startup India figures. There are over 2.62 lakh Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) recognised startups, against fewer than 500 in 2014, and India has the third-largest startup ecosystem. A record 55,200+ startups were recognised in FY 2025-26. The Fund of Funds for Startups (managed by SIDBI) has a ₹10,000 crore corpus, and FFS 2.0 (April 2026) has another ₹10,000 crore. The Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups raised cover from ₹10 crore to ₹20 crore, and the Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS) has a ₹945 crore corpus. GENESIS has an outlay of ₹490 crore over 5 years, and Atal Innovation Mission (AIM) 2.0 has ₹2,750 crore up to March 2028.
  • ★ The Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) quarterly bulletin for July–September 2026 showed improving employment. Labour Force Participation Rate (LFPR) was 55.5% (54.6% in April–June 2026), with female LFPR at 34.7%. The overall unemployment rate fell to 5.1% from 5.4%, with rural at 4.3% and urban at 6.8% (up from 6.7%). The survey is by the National Statistics Office (NSO), Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI), under the revamped methodology from January 2025 (Current Weekly Status). It estimates 57.9 crore workers aged 15+.
  • The Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) banned the export of lithium-ion battery black mass. Notification No. 49/2026-27 (10 Nov 2026) moved ITC(HS) 85491400 from 'Free' to 'Prohibited'. Black mass contains lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese and is meant to be kept for domestic recycling and "urban mining". The move complements the National Critical Mineral Mission and the ₹1,500 crore Critical Mineral Recycling Incentive Scheme (Cabinet, September 2025).
  • ★ NITI Aayog found only 4 of 14 Production Linked Incentive (PLI) sectors 'on track'. These are large-scale electronics, pharmaceutical drugs, telecom and networking products, and food products. PLI was launched in 2020 under Atmanirbhar Bharat with an outlay of about ₹1.97 lakh crore. Bharat Mobility Global Expo 2027 (4–9 Feb 2027, Bharat Mandapam and Yashobhoomi) is the third edition, expecting 1,750+ exhibitors under the theme "Smarter Mobility for a Better Tomorrow".
  • Household finance, commodity and state-level items also made the news. The Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme (SCSS) pays 8.2% for October–December 2026, with a ₹30 lakh cap. The Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI) reported an SIP stoppage ratio of 116% in October. A Tea Board committee proposed a minimum sustainable price for tea, linked to wages and CPI. Andhra Pradesh plans an Investment Protection Act, and Karnataka targets ₹7.5 lakh crore of investment and 20 lakh jobs by 2030. The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) seized foreign currency worth about ₹3.12 crore from 7 Bangkok-bound passengers at Kolkata airport under the Customs Act and FEMA.

Modi reaffirmed India's human-centric approach and its position that the conflict must be resolved through dialogue and diplomacy. Environment & Ecology

  • ★ The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) COP31 is under way in Antalya, Türkiye, co-led by Türkiye and Australia. The Presidency's vision is a "COP of Implementation" (Dialogue, Consensus, Action). India's opening statement was delivered by Narendra Bhooshan, Secretary, Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC). India stressed equity, Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC), just transition and adaptation. It also cited Article 9.1 of the Paris Agreement, which obliges developed countries to provide climate finance. India aligned with G77 & China, the Like-Minded Developing Countries (LMDC) and BASIC.
  • ★ The ban on export of lithium-ion battery black mass is a circular-economy and critical-minerals measure. Black mass is the powder from shredding end-of-life batteries and contains lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese. Keeping it at home feeds domestic recycling, reduces import dependence and supports Aatmanirbhar Bharat. It sits alongside the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 and the National Critical Mineral Mission.
  • ★ An India–Norway Dialogue on Waste to Resource was held in New Delhi on 10 Nov 2026. It was organised by the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) with SINTEF and the Research Council of Norway, on waste solutions for the cement and construction sector. Discussions covered co-processing of hazardous waste including POPs, DDT and PCB under India's Stockholm Convention implementation plan. They also covered the 2025 construction and demolition waste rules and use of fly ash, steel slag, red mud and copper slag. It follows the elevation of ties to a Green Strategic Partnership during PM Modi's visit to Norway in May, and 2–3 joint research proposals were prioritised.
  • Special Campaign 6 turned e-waste into "Waste to Wealth" showcases. The Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (DA&FW) planted over 2,000 plants, and its National Seeds Corporation has an 84 kW rooftop solar facility. Sites such as NERFMTTI in Assam built a life-sized rhinoceros model from circuit boards, and DA&FW reported 3,733 e-waste articles disposed against a 3,298 target. These illustrate the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 in practice.
  • ★ Island fisheries are central to India's Blue Economy push. Lakshadweep and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands hold nearly 40% of India's EEZ (about 24 lakh sq. km; coastline about 11,099 km) and lie on tuna migration routes (yellowfin, skipjack, bigeye). Lakshadweep is notified as a dedicated Seaweed Cluster and aims to double tuna output to 50,000 tonnes. A&N gets a Tuna Cluster under the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY), with envisaged investments of ₹218 crore (A&N) and ₹175 crore (Lakshadweep). Seafood exports were about ₹73,890 crore in FY 2025-26.
  • Defence units showcased green initiatives. INS Shivaji, with IIT Mumbai, installed a biomass gasifier steam-cooking system, and IAF Station Tambaram rejuvenated 9 heritage shallow wells adding about 1 lakh litres a day. IAF Maintenance Command's 13 Base Repair Depot demonstrated hydrogen fuel cell technology to power radars with the National Green Hydrogen Mission and NTPC.
  • Clean-energy infrastructure expanded in Karnataka. PM Modi's projects include Renewable Energy Zones Phase-II (Koppal-II and Gadag-II, about 2 GW) and a Solar Energy Zone at Bidar (2.5 GW). A GAIL Compressed Biogas (CBG) plant at Haralakunte, Bengaluru will process about 300 tonnes of municipal solid waste a day into 12.6 tonnes of CBG. India's largest underground LPG storage cavern (80,000 MT) is at Mangaluru.
  • El Niño and weather risks affect agriculture and prices. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) expects record wheat plantings, with final output dependent on April–May weather and El Niño. Reports link uneven monsoons and El Niño to food-inflation risks.
  • Wildlife trafficking and clean-energy supply chains were in the news. On 5 Nov 2026 two elephant tusks were seized near Sonapur Toll Gate, about 27 km from Guwahati, in an enforcement action reported by the DRI. Elephants are Schedule I species under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972. Indian solar manufacturers are looking to Germany for technology after China proposed curbs on exports of solar manufacturing technology, which threatens India's plan for about 100 GW of wafer, ingot and polysilicon capacity by 2030.

Science & Technology

  • ★ A PIB backgrounder traced the opening of India's space economy. The Indian National Committee for Space Research (INCOSPAR) began in 1962 under the Department of Atomic Energy. The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) was set up in August 1969, and the Department of Space (DoS) followed in 1972. NewSpace India Limited (NSIL) became the commercial arm in March 2019. The Union Cabinet approved private-sector reforms on 24 June 2020, creating IN-SPACe (Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre), an autonomous agency under DoS. The Indian Space Association (ISpA) was launched in October 2021 and the Indian Space Policy 2023 defines roles.
  • ★ Space-sector numbers show rapid growth. The space economy is about US$9 billion (Aug 2026), with about 440 registered space start-ups against 1 in 2014. ISRO launched 399 foreign satellites in 2014–Jan 2026, against 35 in 1990–2014, and projections are about US$44 billion by 2033 and US$100 billion by 2040. FDI limits under the automatic route are up to 74% for satellite manufacturing and operation, 49% for launch vehicles and spaceports, and 100% for components and subsystems. Funding tools include the IN-SPACe Seed Fund (₹1 crore maximum grant), the Technology Adoption Fund (₹500 crore), a ₹1,000 crore Venture Capital Fund and the Pre-incubation Entrepreneurship (PIE) Programme (21 months).
  • ★ Private launch milestones: Skyroot's Vikram-I reached orbit on 18 July 2026. This was Mission Aagaman, the first orbital launch by an Indian private company from Indian soil. It followed Vikram-S (Nov 2022, Mission Prarambh), India's first privately developed rocket, and Agnikul Cosmos' May 2024 launch from the Dhanush private launch pad at Sriharikota. Agnikul demonstrated the world's first single-piece 3D-printed semi-cryogenic engine. In 2025 NSIL, ISRO, IN-SPACe and HAL signed the SSLV Technology Transfer Agreement.
  • COSMOS-Mysuru Planetarium is India's first LED dome planetarium. It was built by the Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA) on the University of Mysore campus below Chamundi Hills. It has a 15-metre tilted dome with about 6,150 panels and 4.2 crore LEDs, and shows in Kannada, English and Hindi. PM Modi's Karnataka visit includes it.
  • The Biotechnology Research and Innovation Council (BRIC) marked its 3rd Foundation Day on 10 Nov 2026 at BRIC-National Institute of Immunology. BRIC unifies 13 biotech institutions across 11 cities and is led by Dr Rajesh S. Gokhale, Secretary DBT. MoS Dr Jitendra Singh said the bioeconomy grew from about USD 10 billion to over USD 195 billion and biotech startups from about 50–60 to over 11,000. A Wellcome Trust grant of up to ₹21.7 crore will support 250+ iBRIC+ PhD scholars, and BRIC signed a Letter of Intent with the UK's Centre for Process Innovation. The iBRIC+ Spotlight Scientist of the Year-2026 awards went to Dr Sunil Laxman, Dr Rashna Bhandari and Prof. Bhabatosh Das.
  • ★ Adi Vaani is the Ministry of Tribal Affairs' AI platform for tribal languages, launched on 1 September 2025. It was developed by a consortium led by IIT Delhi with BITS Pilani, IIIT Hyderabad, IIIT Nava Raipur and State Tribal Research Institutes (TRIs). It does text translation in nine tribal languages, speech-to-text, speech-to-speech, OCR (Optical Character Recognition) and subtitling, and works with 13 languages in total. AdiKosh is its word bank, and by 15 Oct 2026 TRIs had contributed about 13.9 lakh sentences. The Working Group on Tribal Languages is positioning it as a National Tribal Language Repository.
  • The 3rd National Conference on Cyber Security, Digital Forensics & Intelligence (NCCDFI 2026) runs 16–17 Nov 2026 in New Delhi. It is organised by the National Institute of Electronics & Information Technology (NIELIT) under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), with the theme "Cyber Secure Bharat: Fortifying India's Digital Future". It has 10 tracks including AI Security, Drone & IoT Forensics and Blockchain & Crypto. The Cyber Kushti 2026 hackathon drew 1,031 teams. Earlier editions were in Kohima (2024) and Guwahati (2025).
  • Telecom regulation and rollout advanced. The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) notified the Telecommunication Interconnection (Port Charges) (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026, effective 1 January 2027. They add annual ceilings for IP-port charges of ₹5.6 lakh per 1 Gbps port and ₹42.3 lakh per 10 Gbps port, alongside existing E1 (2.048 Mbps) provisions. The financial disincentive ceiling is ₹15 lakh per Service Area. Under the Department of Telecommunications' 4G Saturation Project, villages without 4G fell from 45,180 (2022) to 11,372 (2026), and BSNL has commissioned 18,291 of 24,680 sites.
  • Indigenous defence platforms and technology forums were in focus. The Ministry of Defence signed a ₹498.58 crore contract with Cochin Shipyard Limited to refit and re-power INS Betwa, a Brahmaputra-class frigate, replacing steam propulsion with diesel engines. Raksha Mantri Rajnath Singh addressed the 36th Annual Conference on Non-Destructive Evaluation, calling Aatmanirbharta in defence a strategic necessity and citing INS Vikrant, iDEX and the Technology Development Fund. The Mechanised Forces Symposium (11–12 Nov, New Delhi) unveiled "Armour 2040".
  • AI governance and risk featured in commentary. AI-safety researcher Stuart Russell urged rigorous safety certification and indigenous Indian AI models. Nobel laureate Joseph E. Stiglitz warned of an AI investment bubble with spillovers for India's IT sector. Google launched a lighter "workhorse" AI model, Gemini Hydrogen.

Government Schemes & Social Sector

  • ★ Swachh Survekshan 2025-26, the 10th edition, was conferred by President Droupadi Murmu at Vigyan Bhawan on 11 Nov 2026. It is run by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA) under Swachh Bharat Mission-Urban (SBM-U). Cities assessed grew from 73 in 2016 to 4,751, using 43 indicators across 10 areas, 3,000+ trained assessors and GPS-enabled monitoring. Urban waste processing rose from 16% to 80% since 2014 and door-to-door collection from 44% to over 96%. 60 national awards were given, including first-time awards for Swachh River Front Town, Swachh Coastal Town and Clean Himalayan City.
  • Swachh Shehar Jodi is an urban waste mentorship and peer-learning programme launched on 27 Sept 2025. It pairs 70 mentor cities with 191 mentee cities across 33 States/UTs, under the motto "Each One – Teach One". Mentee cities improved, for example, source segregation from 16% to 30% and access to sanitation from 11% to 38%. PRAGATI-50 will develop 50 Model Clean Cities.
  • ★ The Lakhpati Didi initiative under DAY-NRLM has crossed 3.76 crore achievers. The Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana–National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM, launched June 2011) defines a Lakhpati Didi as an SHG member whose household earns at least ₹1 lakh a year. The target was 2 crore (August 2023), raised to 3 crore (February 2024 Interim Budget) and revised in February 2026 to 6 crore by March 2029. An IIM Ahmedabad assessment found household income up 61%. DAY-NRLM has about 10.13 crore SHG members in 94.40 lakh SHGs.
  • ★ Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (NMBA) completed six years. It was launched on 15 Aug 2020 by the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (MoSJE) in 272 districts and extended to all districts from 15 Aug 2023. It is the flagship awareness programme of the National Action Plan for Drug Demand Reduction (NAPDDR), a ₹333 crore scheme in 2026-27. It has reached over 35.84 crore people and links to Article 47 of the Constitution. The treatment network includes 344 IRCAs, 71 ODICs, 45 CPLIs, 198 DDACs and 155 ATFs, with grant-in-aid of ₹10 lakh per district a year and National De-addiction Helpline 14446.
  • Vayo SAATHI is a volunteer initiative for citizens aged 18+ under Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana (AVYAY). It forms part of the Vayo Samman Rashtriya Abhiyan, a National Intergenerational Bonding Campaign, using the '5C' model (Connect, Converse, Co-create, Contribute, Continue). It has twelve thematic areas, including '30 Minutes Together' and 'Silver Skills – Seniors as Mentors', and registration is through the SCW SAMAVESH portal or the JEEVAN app.
  • MoSJE and MSME schemes support SC, OBC, sanitation workers and persons with disabilities. Shilp Samagam Mela 2026 (Dilli Haat, until 15 Nov) has 130+ stalls and 7 thematic zones. Sales were about ₹64.52 lakh in 1–9 Nov and about ₹73.49 lakh in 1–10 Nov across NSFDC, NBCFDC, NSKFDC and NDFDC. A round table with 30 CPSEs monitored the 4% procurement target from SC/ST MSEs under the Public Procurement Policy. Case studies featured the National Overseas Scholarship, NAMASTE (National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem) and the National Fellowship for SC Students.
  • Health indicators improved and drug prices were capped. National Health Accounts 2023–24 (11th round, prepared by NHATS at NHSRC) shows out-of-pocket expenditure falling from 64.2% (2013-14) to 42.7% of total health expenditure. Government health expenditure rose from ₹1,29,778 crore to ₹4,14,196 crore, and per capita from ₹1,042 to ₹2,959. Of the 22 new AIIMS approved under PMSSY, 18 are fully functional. The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) capped trade margins on 137 anti-cancer drugs from 9 Nov 2026, with 679 brands cutting prices by over 50% and estimated savings of ₹3,540.38 crore a year.
  • Cabinet cleared the Noida Aqua Line extension. It adds 8.449 km and 5 elevated stations from Noida Sector-51 to Greater Noida Sector-04, with an interchange with the Blue Line at Sector-61. The Noida–Greater Noida network will then total 71.878 km.
  • Credit and livelihood schemes were highlighted. PM SVANidhi (June 2020) offers street vendors ₹15,000, ₹25,000 and ₹50,000 loans, a UPI-linked RuPay credit card with a ₹30,000 limit, and has 80.7 lakh+ beneficiaries. PMMY categories are Shishu, Kishor, Tarun and Tarun Plus (₹10–20 lakh). PMEGP is implemented by KVIC under the Ministry of MSME, with project limits of ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) and ₹20 lakh (services).
  • Agriculture and tribal welfare programmes featured. An Odisha event in Bhubaneswar targets millet area rising from 1.79 lakh ha to 5 lakh ha by 2047, with Odisha Shree Anna Abhiyan cited as a national model. The Khet Bachao Abhiyan runs 22 Oct–30 Nov 2026 for Rabi crops. For tribal welfare, the Jan-HIT Portal and National FRA Digital Platform are being launched. PM-JANMAN and Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan together have over ₹1 lakh crore, and the National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission (July 2023) aims at elimination by 2047.
  • Youth drug-awareness campaigns continued under MY Bharat (Mera Yuva Bharat). The 100-week 'Nasha Mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat' Sankalp Abhiyan's week-8 activities drew 1.19 lakh+ participants in 30 States/UTs. The Nasha Mukt Yuva, Viksit Bharat Quiz runs 26 Oct–25 Nov 2026 on the MY Bharat portal.

Geography & Places in News

  • ★ The Yamuna is National Waterway-110 under the National Waterways Act, 2016, and now has Delhi's first recreational cruise. Union Minister Sarbananda Sonowal inaugurated it at Sonia Vihar on 10 Nov 2026. It runs on the Sonia Vihar–Jagatpur stretch with a 30-seater electric vessel and a roughly 6 km round trip. It was implemented by the Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI) with DDA, DTTDC, DJB and the Delhi Irrigation and Flood Control Department. Waterways carry freight at ₹1.06 per tonne-km against ₹1.41 for rail and ₹2.28 for road.
  • ★ Visakhapatnam Port handled a record 91.17 million metric tons in 2025-26. Sonowal laid foundation stones for projects of about ₹560 crore and dedicated a ₹19 crore Port Promenade of about 710 m. The sewage treatment plant is being upgraded from 10 to 15 MLD under the Harit Sagar green port initiative. The container terminal's permissible draft is 16 m, and the port is shifting to a landlord-port model. The 31st CII Partnership Summit is hosted by Andhra Pradesh on 12–13 November.
  • PM Modi's 13–14 Nov Karnataka programme spans Bengaluru, Mangaluru, Mysuru and coastal rail links. Projects worth over ₹15,600 crore include the Bengaluru Metro Pink Line (Kalena Agrahara–Tavarekere, about ₹3,470 crore). Rail items include electrification of the 55 km Sakleshpur–Subrahmanya Road ghat section, completing full electrification of Bengaluru–Mangaluru, and a Bengaluru–Mangaluru–Madgaon Vande Bharat Express. The Gadag–Hotgi doubling is a 284 km project supporting Kudgi NTPC coal supply.
  • Island geography underpins the Blue Economy push. The Andaman & Nicobar Islands (Bay of Bengal) and Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea) together hold nearly 40% of India's EEZ and sit on tuna migration routes. Lakshadweep hosts over 300 reef-associated ornamental species.
  • Key places abroad in this week's events. Jeddah (Haj Agreement signed), Muscat, Oman (Naseem Al Bahr), Safaga Naval Base on Egypt's Red Sea coast (INS Trishul), Colombo (BIMSTEC Health Ministers' Meeting), Doha (ITU PP-26), Antalya, Türkiye (COP31), Cairo and Heliopolis War Cemetery (Goyal's visit), and Ubon Ratchathani, Thailand (IOAA-Jr 2026). Czech venues for the Army Chief's visit are Prague and Brno.
  • Arunachal Pradesh hosts the Buddha relics exposition across three places. Tawang (31 Oct–7 Nov), Bomdila in West Kameng at Thubchog Gatsel Ling Monastery (8–13 Nov), and Namsai's Golden Pagoda, a Theravada centre (14–20 Nov). The Bomdila leg alone drew 35,000+ devotees.
  • Other places in news inside India. Gadchiroli, Maharashtra (PM's Janjatiya Gaurav Divas event), Thoothukudi, Tamil Nadu (VP's visit), Mau, Uttar Pradesh (CBN mephedrone lab), and Ahmedabad (Amit Shah's ₹1,400 crore projects). Bengaluru-linked energy sites include Koppal, Gadag and Bidar. Bhubaneswar hosted the millet event.
  • Weather and hazard geography. Cyclone Ditwah (Nov–Dec 2025) caused the damage behind India's USD 450 million rehabilitation support to Sri Lanka, and RAHAT MITRA's table-top exercise simulated a severe cyclone. Reports link El Niño and uneven monsoons to inflation and wheat-output risks.

History, Art & Culture

  • ★ Janjatiya Gaurav Divas on 15 Nov 2026 marked the 151st birth anniversary of Bhagwan Birsa Munda ("Dharti Aaba"). The day has been observed since 15 Nov 2021 to honour tribal freedom fighters. Birsa Munda led the Ulgulan against British rule and asserted tribal rights over water, forests and land. Leaders paid tribute at Prerna Sthal in Parliament House, and PM Modi joined a Gadchiroli event by video conferencing for 246 projects worth about ₹3,000 crore. Janjatiya Gaurav Pakhwada ran 1–15 Nov.
  • PM Modi cited many tribal resistance figures. These included Tirot Sing (Khasi Hills), the Kol movement, the Santhal revolt (Sidho-Kanhu), Govind Guru at Mangarh, Rani Gaidinliu, Tantya Bhil, Alluri Sitarama Raju and Rani Durgavati. Gadchiroli heroes named were Raghoji Bhangre, Bhagoji Naik and Veer Baburao Shedmake. A museum for Mizoram's freedom fighter Ropuiliani was among the projects.
  • ★ The Sacred Relics of Shakyamuni Buddha exposition is touring Arunachal Pradesh after 13 years. It is organised by the Ministry of Culture with the Arunachal Government, the National Museum and the International Buddhist Confederation (IBC). Cumulative footfall passed 66,000 across Tawang and Bomdila, and the Namsai leg runs 14–20 Nov. The relics return to the National Museum, New Delhi on 21 Nov 2026. Related exhibitions are "Unearthing the Sacred Piprahwa" (the Piprahwa jewels returned to India in 2025) and "Crossroads of Dhamma".
  • ★ The Union Cabinet approved the Yuge Yugeen Bharat Museum (YYBM) at a cost of ₹3,230 crore. It will be built by adaptive reuse of the North and South Block Complex in New Delhi, with about 1,78,509 sq m of built-up area, described as the world's largest display area. It was announced on 18 May 2023 and appraised on 27 July 2026. Development is phased over FY 2026-27 to 2030-31, with the first gallery ("Adornment") due in North Block in later 2027. It will draw on about 1.8 million objects, including from 52 ASI site museums, and handle 1 crore visitors a year.
  • Maulana Abul Kalam Azad's birth anniversary was observed on 11 Nov 2026. Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla paid floral tributes in the Central Hall of Samvidhan Sadan. Azad was independent India's first Education Minister and a Bharat Ratna recipient, and President Dr. Rajendra Prasad unveiled his portrait on 16 December 1959.
  • Regional and freedom-struggle heritage featured in the Vice-President's Thoothukudi visit. He attended the 105th Foundation Day of Tamilnad Mercantile Bank, which began in 1921 as Nadar Bank Limited. He also joined the Platinum Jubilee of V. O. Chidambaram College and paid tribute to freedom fighter V. O. Chidambaram Pillai. He recalled his own participation in the movement against the Emergency.
  • The birth centenary of H.V. Sheshadri (born 1926) was commemorated. He was RSS Sarkaryavah from 1987 to 2000. The Prashanti Kutiram Golden Jubilee celebration (13–23 Nov 2026), theme "Towards Viksit Bharat 2047", marks the 1975 founding of the S-VYASA campus; S-VYASA became a Deemed-to-be University in 2002.
  • War memory and cultural festivals. The Heliopolis War Cemetery in Cairo commemorates 4,000, of whom 3,727 are Indian (about 2,000 from WWI and about 1,700 from WWII). PM Modi greeted citizens on Nutan Varsh, Govardhan Puja, Bhai Dooj and Chhath Mahaparv, and Jharkhand Foundation Day (15 Nov).
